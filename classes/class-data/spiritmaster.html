“I am never alone. I have my Spirit with me.”

The premier sustained melee damage class, built around deliberate attack sequences, Momentum, Chain Links, and Finishers.

The Spiritmaster is a two-body tactical caster. One active elemental spirit shares the Spiritmaster’s turn and action economy; together they equal one complete player character. The class establishes two battlefield positions, casts through the spirit, and earns Spirit Bond only through meaningful master-spirit coordination.

Class Identity

Spiritmasters are binders and commanders of elemental spirits who fight as a partnership rather than as a solitary caster. Their strength comes from coordinating their own spellcraft with a Greater Spirit, applying pressure, disruption, and control across more than one position on the battlefield. Spirit Bond represents the relationship and command structure that makes this divided presence function as one class.

Tradition & Outlook

The Spiritmaster tradition develops from Atreia’s mage disciplines but turns outward toward spirits, elemental forces, and the difficult art of directing entities shaped by Aether. Spiritmasters are often patient observers, researchers, summoners, or specialists in threats that cannot be understood through ordinary physical study. Their beliefs about spirits differ: some emphasize command, others partnership, responsibility, curiosity, or respect for elemental power.

Appearance & Adventuring

Spiritmasters use cloth armor and magical implements, while the visible presence of a summoned spirit often defines their silhouette more strongly than clothing does. Their equipment may include ritual, scholarly, or elemental motifs without creating additional mechanics. They adventure to understand unusual spirits, recover lost summoning knowledge, counter supernatural threats, serve as versatile magical operatives, or deepen the bond between Daeva and elemental ally.

Core Mechanics & Resources

Spirit Bond

Generation: Once on each of your turns, gain 1 Spirit Bond if both you and your active spirit successfully affect hostile creatures during your turn. This includes a hit, actual damage, a failed save, meaningful hostil condition/penalty, forced movement, or another real combat effects. Merely targeting a hostile or applying an effect to which it is immune does not. Normal Bond generation is once on each of your turns. The master and spirit do not need to affect the same hostile unless a specific features says so.

Starting State and Reset: Spirit Bond begins at 0 when genuine combat starts and resets to 0 when combat ends. Artifical combat and initiatives cannot preload it.

Bond Techniques

  • Bonded Assault – Costs 1 Spirit Bond and done on Spirit Hit. Add 1d8 damage of that spirit's Basic Attack damage type, and 2d10 beginning at Level 17. Independent damage; not doubled on a critical hit. No Action required.
  • Protective Bond – Costs 1 Spirit Bond. When you or your spirit takes attack damage while within 30 ft of the other, use your Reaction to reduce the total damage from that triggering attack by 1d6 + INT; 2d8 + INT at L17. Apply the reduction once to the attack’s combined damage total, including all damage types and components.
  • Spirit Recall – Costs 1 Spirit Bond. As a Bonus Action, teleport spirit to within 5 ft of you, or if already within 5 ft to a visible space within 30 ft. L17 may exchange positions within 60 ft if perceived through Spirit Sight. This teleportation and position exchange do not provoke Opportunity Attacks; destinations must be legal, unoccupied spaces.

Summon Spirits and Elemental Spirits

A Spiritmaster spirit is a Large creature that occupies a 4-grid space. All spirits hover and remain displaced above the ground. They do not need to land. They can move vertically using their available speed. Fire, Water, and Earth spirits have a Speed of 30 ft; Wind has a Speed of 40 ft. Hovering and vertical movement do not by themselves prevent Opportunity Attacks; a spirit can provoke normally when it voluntarily moves out of an enemy's reach. Teleportation and movement that explicitly avoids Opportunity Attacks remain exceptions.

  • The Spiritmaster and spirit share one Reaction. If either uses it, neither can use another Reaction until the start of the Spiritmaster's next turn.
  • You know all four fundamental spirits—Fire, Water, Earth, and Wind—and choose among them tactically. Only one Spiritmaster spirit may be summoned at a time. Summoning another spirit will disperse the other one that is currently active. Summoning uses an Action and places the spirit in a visible unoccupied space within 15 ft.
  • A defeated spirit disperses into Aether and makes no death saves. Recovery depends on your level, and you may resummon it at certain HP levels depending on your Spiritmaster Level. Only a spirit actually reduced to 0 HP qualifies for reconstitution. Voluntary dismissal or replacement never qualifies for defeat recovery. Outside combat a newly summoned spirit appears at full HP; voluntary replacement during combat preserves the dismissed spirit's current HP percentage rather than healing by switching forms.
  • The spirit shares your Initiative and acts during your turn. It moves normally during your turn without consuming your action.
  • You normally spend your Bonus Action to command its basic Spirit Action. If not commanded, it may move normally, Dodge, or remain in place; it may move and Dodge during the same turn. It does not need a Bonus Action to move or Dodge when commanded.
  • A spirit normally makes only one basic Spirit Attack per turn.
  • Spirit attacks are class features, not spells. Spirit AC never inherits your armor or personal AC bonuses.
  • The spirit has its own designated Chassis separate from your own as described below.

Ultimate Ability

Primal ConvergenceUltimate

Ultimate Activation: Costs 100 Daeva Points (DP).

As your Action and Bonus Action, make one Ultimate spell attack against a creature you can see within 120 feet. After this Ultimate attempt resolves, you enter Ultimate Recovery during your next turn.

Convergent Damage: The attack deals the normal spell damage plus fixed additional damage of the ability.

Primal Suppression. The target's Speed becomes 0, it cannot take Reactions, and it has Disadvantage on attack rolls until the start of your next turn. At Level 15, the suppression instead lasts until the end of your next turn. At Level 20, the target also cannot benefit from increases to its Speed or teleport while suppressed. This is a bespoke restriction, not Stunned or Incapacitated.

Rules, Recovery and Restrictions:

Ultimate Attack Ability. Activating an Ultimate consumes both the character's Action and Bonus Action. The character cannot activate it unless both are available, but may still move and take Reactions normally. The Ultimate Ability is not an Attack action and cannot be duplicated by Extra Attack, Quick Shot, Akimbo, additional-action effects, reactions, or similar features.

Fixed Ultimate Output. Damage printed on the corresponding level as the Ultimate's own direct numerical output are not rolled; use the maximum value of those die. Normal weapon damage and damage dice from other features remain rolled normally.

Specializations

Three distinct paths, with advancement features at the established specialization levels.

SPIRIT CALLER

The Spirit Caller places its spirit companion at the center of its combat strategy, strengthening its attacks, persistence, and eventual ascendance. It is suited to players who want to command a powerful elemental companion and make their spirit a major source of battlefield damage.

HEX BINDER

The Hexbinder weakens opponents through persistent curses and debilitating magical effects. Its abilities deepen the impact of hostile hexes, allowing it to undermine enemy effectiveness and make important targets increasingly vulnerable.

BONDWEAVER

The Bondweaver strengthens the connection between Spiritmaster and spirit, improving resource efficiency, shared protection, and overall resilience. Rather than focusing solely on the spirit's offensive power, this path develops a more durable and adaptable partnership.

Level Details

  1. Level 1: Spellcasting, Summon Spirit, Spirit Bond, Bond Techniques, Shared Aether

    Spellcasting

    Maximum Spell Level 1 and you gain Spiritmaster spellcasting and 6 spells:

    • Gale Sigil. Action. At-Will. Damage / Positioning
    • Spirit Bolt. Action. At-Will. Damage
    • Spirit Flame. Action. At-Will. Damage / Bond Setup
    • Spirit Mend. Action. At-Will. Repair / Healing
    • Stone Mark. Action. At-Will. Damage / Protection Setup
    • Water Sigil. Action. At-Will. Debuff

    Spirit Bond and Bond Techniques

    Your maximum Spirit Bond is 3 and you can use them on Bond Techniques according to their designated timing rules.

    Shared Aether

    Once on each of your turns when you cast a Spiritmaster spell that affects a hostile creature within 10 feet of your spirit, choose one:

    • Channel Through Spirit. makes your spell to come from your spirit's location instead of yours. Measure the spell's range from where the spirit is standing, even though you are still the one casting it.
    • Resonant Target. gives +1 to the spell attack to an ally or +1 to the first save DC against one creature adjacent to your spirit.

    Only AOE spells that has defined range and area like Cone, Line, Sphere, are considered eligible spells.

  2. Level 2: Spirit Link, Aetheric Command, Spiritual Reconstitution

    You now begin to gain +2 Spell Advancements every time you level up.

    Your Maximum Spell Level is 2. Use the gained Spell Advancements to increase the level of spells up to the spell level maximum. You can also bank in your spell advancements to increase the spell level of a certain spell when you obtain them.

    Your Spirit Bond maximum becomes 4.

    Spirit Link

    As a Bonus Action, while your spirit is within 60 ft, you may perceive through the spirit. Until the start of your next turn, attack rolls against you or your spirit have Disadvantage as long as your spirit is within 30 ft from you.

    Aetheric Command

    Once each turn, when you command your active spirit to take its Spirit Action, choose one command mode:

    • Aggressive – When the spirit's basic Spirit Attack hits, it deals additional damage equal to your Proficiency Bonus. This does not double on a critical hit.
    • Guarding – The spirit uses Dodge and protects an adjacent creature; while guarding, it may use its Reaction to impose Disadvantage on an attack targeting that protected creature.
    • Mobile – The spirit may move up to half its Speed as part of the Spirit Action. This move is added on top of the current Speed of the spirit or whether it has already repositioned or not.

    Spiritual Reconstitution

    Once per Long Rest and as a full Action, you may resummon any one of your spirit that was reduced to 0 HP. A resummoned spirit would only have 1 HP although it would have a temporary HP equivalent to your PB + INT. Water Spirit would have 5 HP, same temporary HP as other spirits.

  3. Level 3: Specialization Advancement

    You gain +2 Spell Advancements, and 1 additional spell:

    • Hex of Frailty. Action. Cooldown 2. Debuff

    Choose one specialization path to view its Level 3 feature.

    SPIRIT CALLER — Empowered Spirit

    Once per turn on a basic Spirit Attack hit, add one attack die: Fire +1d8; Water, Earth, or Wind +1d6, doubled on a critical hit. When spending Bonded Assault, the spirit may move 5 feet without provoking an Opportunity Attack from the creature hit.

    HEXBINDER — Cursed ConduitOnce per turn after a creature fails a Spiritmaster spell save, choose one until your next turn:

    • Enfeeble reduces its next damage by PB (minimum 1);
    • Hinder reduces its Speed by 10 feet, or
    • Expose gives the next Spirit Attack against it +2.

    When Shared Aether originates a save spell from the spirit, one creature within 5 feet of the spirit takes -1 on that save.

    BONDWEAVER — Efficient Bond

    Increase maximum Spirit Bond by 1. Once per turn when you spend Bond, roll 1d6; on a 6, regain 1 Bond after the feature resolves. Protective Bond used on a creature within 15 feet reduces the triggering damage by an additional PB.

  4. Level 4: Feat / Ability Score Improvement

    Gain +2 Spell Advancements, Maximum Spell Level 3, universal Feat / Ability Score Improvement for this level.

  5. Level 5: Coordinated Manifestation

    Gain +2 Spell Advancements. You also gained 1 additional spell:

    • Shared Aether Pulse. Action. Short Rest 1. Dual-Origin Support.

    Your Spirit Bond maximum becomes 5.

    Coordinated Manifestation

    When both you and your active spirit successfully affect hostile creatures during your turn, choose its listed benefit:

    • 1d8 additional Force damage (does not double on critical);
    • 5 ft Speed reduction;
    • Temporary HP to you or your active spirit equivalent to your Proficiency Bonus + INT modifier.

    When you perform a qualifying action on your turn, both Coordinated Manifestation and Perfect Coordination (level 15) activate at the same time as separate abilities. You can only trigger each of these features once per turn, and you apply their bonuses individually rather than combining them into a single effect. Additionally, any extra actions or bonus effects created by one of these abilities cannot be used to set off the other one in a chain reaction.

  6. Level 6: Specialization Advancement

    You gain +2 Spell Advancements, Maximum Spell Level 4 and 1 additional spell:

    • Spirit Erosion. Action. Cooldown 2. Damage / Debuff.

    Choose one specialization path to view its Level 6 feature.

    SPIRIT CALLER — Relentless Spirit

    Once/turn on basic hit:

    • Fire deals INT Fire at start target next turn;
    • Water adds another -5 Speed;
    • Earth gives Disadvantage on target next attack;
    • Wind moves spirit 10 ft safely.

    It cannot stack immediate damage with later Ascendance unless one effect is delayed/control/movement.

    HEXBINDER — Malignant Echo

    Once/turn after a failed Spiritmaster save, choose a creature within 5 ft of spirit:

    • -2 next save vs your Spiritmaster spell,
    • -2 modifier to its next dealt damage,
    • or no Reactions until your next turn.

    BONDWEAVER — Resonant Bond

    Once/turn after normal Bond generation choose:

    • you/spirit heal equivalent to your Proficiency Bonus;
    • both move 5 ft safely;
    • or next Protective Bond before your turn reduces +PB.

  7. Level 7: Higher Spiritual Reconstitution

    You gain +2 Spell Advancements and 5 additional spells:

    • Burning Brand. Action. Cooldown 2. Damage / Debuff.
    • Drowning Hex. Action. Cooldown 2. Control / Debuff.
    • Earthen Shackles. Action. Cooldown 2. Control.
    • Protective Link. Action. Cooldown 2. Protection.
    • Vacuum Curse. Action. Cooldown 2. Debuff / Forced Movement.

    Higher Spiritual Reconstitution

    Your Spiritual Reconstitution is now more advanced in terms of recovery. Once per Long Rest, when your spirit has actually been reduced to 0 HP, you may use your Bonus Action to resummon it at half its maximum HP + INT modifier after the normal recovery delay. It also gains a temporary HP equal to your INT modifier and has Advantage against forced repositioning, dismissal, or incapacitation while within 30 ft. The Water Spirit being resummoned gains temporary HP equal to PB + INT.

  9. Level 9: Advanced Spirit Bond

    Your Spirit Bond maximum becomes 6.

    You gain +2 Spell Advancements.

    Spirit Link

    Your Spirit Link now functions at 80 ft with exact position/HP/conditions, and sensory sharing no longer causes attacks against your body to have Advantage. Attack rolls against you or your spirit have Disadvantage as long as your spirit is within 45 ft from you.

    Resonant Casting

    When you cast a Spiritmaster spell using your Shared Aether, you can choose any of the following:

    • Piercing – hostile target gets a -1 to its first save; no stack Cursed Conduit;
    • Bonded – your Spirit has Advantage on its next basic attack;
    • Protective – choose either heal current HP or gain Temp HP. The value is equivalent to your Proficiency bonus. The effect applies to either you or your spirit.

  10. Level 10: Specialization Decision / Advancement, Primal Convergence

    You gain +2 Spell Advancements, Maximum Spell Level 6, and 1 additional spell:

    Choose one specialization path to view its Level 10 feature.

    SPIRIT CALLER — Spirit Ascendance

    Your Spirit has more advanced abilities:

    • Fire: next Fire damage before your turn +1d8.
    • Water: first damaged creature gets additional Speed reduction.
    • Earth: resistance to next damage instance before your turn.
    • Wind: immediate 15-ft safe move; if ending adjacent different hostile, +1 next attack against it.

    HEX BINDER — Compounding Hex

    Once/turn when already-Hexed creature fails another Spiritmaster save choose:

    • Wither (-PB next damage),
    • Suppress (no Reactions/no Dash),or
    • Expose Aether (+1 DC or spell attack for your next Spiritmaster spell against it).

    BONDWEAVER — Overflowing Bond

    Once per round, when normal Spirit Bond generation would occur while you are already at maximum Bond, replace that Bond gain with one benefit:

    • heal 1d8 + INT;
    • gain PB + INT temporary HP; or
    • both you and your spirit move 10 feet safely.

    This does not trigger from Efficient Bond refunds, Perfect Coordination’s Bond recovery, or other Bond restoration effects.

    Primal Convergence

    Your Spiritmaster makes an ultimate move. The Primary Damage is a fixed amount of 90 Force Damage. The target's Speed becomes 0, cannot take Reactions, and has Disadvantage on attack rolls until the start of your next turn. This is a restriction, not Stunned nor Incapacitated status.

  11. Level 11: Twin Aether, Greater Spirit Chassis

    You gain +2 Spell Advancements, and 1 additional spell:

    • Twin Aether Burst. Action. Short Rest 1. Dual-Origin Damage

    Twin Aether

    Twin Aether lets one Action-cost Spiritmaster spell include the spirit’s one normal basic Spirit Attack immediately before or after the spell, freeing your Bonus Action. The included attack uses the spirit’s basic attack for that turn; you cannot also command another basic Spirit Attack with your Bonus Action that turn. The freed Bonus Action remains available for another legal purpose. Twin Aether never grants a second normal spirit attack.

    Greater Spirit Chassis

    Your spirit advances and changes HP formula to 12 + 6 × Spiritmaster level + INT (Water Spirit adds PB to the HP calculation and retains the same temporary HP equal to your INT modifier), grants additional to modifier to all saves equivalent to your Proficiency Bonus, Advantage to rolls against Charm/Fear, and adds one attack die: Fire 2d8 + PB; others 2d6 + PB before specialization dice.

  12. Level 12: Feat / Ability Score Improvement

    Gain +2 Spell Advancements, and universal Feat / Ability Score Improvement for this level.

  13. Level 13: Greater Shared Aether

    You gain +2 Spell Advancements.

    Greater Shared Aether

    Greater Shared Aether reaches 30 ft from your spirit. In addition to the normal Shared Aether choice (Channel Through Spirit or Resonant Target), choose one more Relay option:

    • Extend an eligible spell's range by 20 ft;
    • give the spirit Advantage on its next saving throw; or
    • empower the next basic Spirit Attack against a hostile creature affected by the spell, adding 1d8 damage of the spirit's basic attack damage type. The Empower attack must occur after the spell. A Twin Aether attack made before the spell cannot receive this benefit. The additional 1d8 is part of the qualifying attack's damage and is doubled on a critical hit.

    Spirit Persistence

    You may spend 2 Spirit Bond once per turn when your spirit would fall to 0 as a Reaction, leaving it at 1 HP and a temporary HP of PB + INT.

  14. Level 14: Final Specialization Decision / Advancement

    You gain +2 Spell Advancements, Maximum Spell Level 8, Maximum Spirit Bond of 7, and 1 additional spell:

    • Fusion Veil. Bonus Action. Long Rest 1. Mastery.

    Choose one specialization path to view its Level 14 feature.

    SPIRIT CALLER — Apex Spirit

    Your spirit has advanced further:

    • Fire ignores resistance (not immunity) and once/turn +INT to one Fire roll;
    • Water strengthens Speed suppression and prevents Speed increases until your next turn;
    • Earth once/round resists Bludgeoning/Piercing/Slashing attack damage;
    • Wind gains flying Speed equal Speed, may pass hostile spaces as difficult terrain, and OAs vs it have Disadvantage after 10+ ft movement.

    HEX BINDER — Dominion Hex

    Once/turn when an already-Hexed creature fails a Spiritmaster save, spend 1 Bond for one until your next turn:

    • Weakness halves its next successful damaging attack/ability;
    • Binding sets Speed 0 and no Dash;
    • Silence removes Reactions and gives Disadvantage on first save to maintain a spell/magical effect.

    Flat save penalties do not stack.

    BONDWEAVER — Perfect Resonance

    Efficient Bond will refund a Spirit bond when you roll 5 or 6 in a d6 dice.

    Shared Vitality heals the other pair member for half when one receives Bondweaver-feature healing.

    Harmonic Guard may spend +1 Bond with Protective Bond to apply the same rolled reduction to the next damage instance suffered by the other member before your next turn; one active at a time.

    • Abyssal Torrent. Action. Short Rest 1. Apex Control.
    • Spirit Conflagration. Action. Short Rest 1. Apex Damage.

    • Titanic Binding. Action. Short Rest 1. Apex Control / Protection.

    Perfect Coordination

    At once per turn when both you and your active Spirit successfully affect hostile creatures during the turn, choose one benefit:

    • deal 2d8 independent Force damage which does not double on critical;
    • impose -2 on the target’s next save against your Spiritmaster spell;
    • regain 1 Spirit Bond thus totaling to 2 Spirit Bonds after resolving the base core mechanics of gaining Spirit Bond, or heal PB + INT if already at maximum Bond to you or your spirit;
    • or both you and your spirit move 15 feet safely from one creature.

    When you perform a qualifying action on your turn, both Coordinated Manifestation (level 5) and Perfect Coordination activate at the same time as separate abilities. You can only trigger each of these features once per turn, and you apply their bonuses individually rather than combining them into a single effect. Additionally, any extra actions or bonus effects created by one of these abilities cannot be used to set off the other one in a chain reaction.

    Primal Convergence

    Your Spiritmaster makes an ultimate move. The Primary Damage is a fixed amount of 110 Force Damage. The target's Speed becomes 0, cannot take Reactions, and has Disadvantage on attack rolls until the end of your next turn. This is a restriction, not Stunned nor Incapacitated status.

  16. Level 16: Feat / Ability Score Improvement

    Gain +2 Spell Advancements, and universal Feat / Ability Score Improvement for this level.

  17. Level 17: Apex Twin Aether, Apex Bond Techniques

    You have reached the apex of your spellcasting with the Max Spell Level of 9. You also gain +2 Spell Advancements, and 1 additional spell:

    • Soul Rupture. Action. Long Rest 1. Apex Damage / Debuff.

    Apex Twin Aether

    Apex Twin Aether applies one Aetheric Command mode (Aggressive, Guarding, or Mobile) to its granted spirit attack and then moves the spirit 10 ft safely. This use counts as your once-per-turn use of Aetheric Command; you cannot apply a separate command mode to another Spirit Action that turn.

    Apex Bond Techniques

    Your Bond Techniques has reached the apex level with enhanced abilities:

    • Bonded Assault – becomes 2d10.
    • Protective Bond – becomes 2d8 + INT modifier.
    • Spirit Recall – can now exchange positions within 60 ft.

  18. Level 18: Sovereign Spirit Link

    You gain +2 Spell Advancements.

    Sovereign Spirit Link

    Sovereign Spirit Link extends Spirit Sight/Shared Aether to 100 ft; within 60 ft grants Advantage vs Charm, Fear, force movement, banishment/dismissal, or magical incapacitation. Attack rolls against you or your spirit have Disadvantage if your spirit is within 45 ft from you. Spirit Persistence costs 1 Bond and grants 2×PB + INT temp HP.

  19. Level 19: Empyrean Boon

    Gain +2 Spell Advancements and the universal Empyrean Boon.

  20. Level 20: Perfect Spiritmaster

    You gain +2 Spell Advancements.

    Perfect Bond starts genuine combat with 3 Spirit Bond.

    Perfect Manifestation

    Perfect Manifestation makes ordinary summoning and voluntary replacement Bonus Actions; voluntary replacement still preserves the dismissed spirit’s current HP percentage. If your spirit is actually reduced to 0 HP, you may immediately resummon it at full HP once per Short Rest. This full-HP recovery replaces Spiritual Reconstitution and its recovery limit; it does not trigger from voluntary dismissal or replacement. The once-per-Short-Rest limit applies only to this special full-HP recovery, not to ordinary summoning or replacement. A Short Rest restores eligibility but does not automatically resummon a defeated spirit. Summoning another spirit still disperses the currently active spirit.

    Perfect Coordination

    Perfect Coordination chooses two different benefits instead of one. Each completed coordination also grants the Spiritmaster the active spirit’s element-specific Perfect Spirit Interaction until the start of the Spiritmaster’s next turn:

    • Fire — the first time you deal damage with a Spiritmaster spell, add PB to one damage roll of that spell (any damage type);
    • Water — immediately gain temporary HP at the start of genuine combat equal to PB, replacing any existing temporary HP;
    • Earth — gain +2 AC;
    • Wind — gain +10 feet Speed.

    These are separate from the two benefits chosen for Perfect Coordination.

    Primal Convergence

    Your Spiritmaster makes an ultimate move. The Primary Damage is a fixed amount of 130 Force Damage. The target's Speed becomes 0, cannot take Reactions, and has Disadvantage on attack rolls until the end of your next turn. It also cannot benefit from increases to its Speed or teleport while suppressed. This is a restriction, not Stunned nor Incapacitated status.

How to Play Spiritmaster

The Spiritmaster is a two-body tactical caster who fights alongside a summoned elemental spirit. Your strength comes from coordinating your own spellcasting with your spirit's attacks, positioning, and special abilities. Rather than functioning as two separate characters, you and your spirit operate together as one complete player character.

Your spells provide magical damage, debuffs, and battlefield control, while your spirit gives you another position from which to attack and influence combat. By coordinating both, you generate Spirit Bond and unlock techniques that improve your offense, defense, and mobility.

Core Gameplay Loop

Summon the appropriate elemental spirit → Position yourself and your spirit to cover useful areas of the battlefield → Cast a Spiritmaster spell and command your spirit to act → Generate Spirit Bond when both successfully affect hostile creatures → Spend Bond on attacks, protection, or repositioning → Adapt your spirit and spell choices to the encounter.

Your JobMagical damage, debuffs, battlefield control, and tactical support through coordination with an elemental spirit.
Your ResourceSpirit Bond, generated when both you and your active spirit successfully affect hostile creatures. It powers Bonded Assault, Protective Bond, Spirit Recall, and other class features.
Your PositionManage two battlefield positions. Keep your spirit within useful range for Shared Aether and Bond techniques while maintaining a safe position for your own spellcasting.

1. Manage Your Spirit as an Extension of Yourself

Your elemental spirit is central to nearly everything you do. You can choose between Fire, Water, Earth, and Wind, each with its own attack and persistent identity. Select the spirit that best supports your current tactical needs.

  • Fire: Provides additional Fire damage pressure and interacts with resistance to Fire damage.
  • Water: Offers Cold damage, Speed reduction, and temporary HP when summoned or resummoned.
  • Earth: Provides defensive durability through its increased AC and effects that hinder attempts to force-move it.
  • Wind: Offers greater movement speed, Force damage, and safe movement after a successful attack.

Your spirit shares your Initiative and acts during your turn. It can move without consuming your Action. Normally, you spend your Bonus Action to command its basic Spirit Action. If you do not command it, it can still move, Dodge, or remain in place.

You can have only one active spirit at a time. Summoning another spirit disperses the currently active one, so switching spirits should be a deliberate tactical decision.

2. Coordinate Your Actions to Generate Spirit Bond

Spirit Bond is generated through meaningful cooperation between you and your active spirit. Once on each of your turns, you gain 1 Bond if both you and your spirit successfully affect hostile creatures during that turn.

A qualifying effect can include a hit, actual damage, a failed saving throw, a meaningful hostile condition or penalty, or forced movement. Merely targeting a creature or applying an effect to which it is immune does not qualify.

  • Cast a spell that damages or debuffs a hostile creature.
  • Command your spirit to attack or otherwise affect a hostile creature.
  • Complete both qualifying effects during the same turn to generate Spirit Bond.
  • You and your spirit do not need to affect the same hostile creature unless a feature specifically requires it.

Consider what you may need Bond for before spending it. Bonded Assault improves your spirit's successful attacks, Protective Bond can reduce incoming attack damage, and Spirit Recall can reposition your spirit without provoking Opportunity Attacks.

3. Use Shared Aether to Coordinate Spellcasting

Shared Aether lets you use your spirit's position as part of your spellcasting strategy. Once on each of your turns, when a Spiritmaster spell affects a hostile creature within 10 feet of your spirit, choose one of its two options.

  • Channel Through Spirit: Measure the spell's range, origin, cover, and line of effect from the spirit's space while you remain the caster.
  • Resonant Target: Gain +1 to the spell attack or +1 to the first saving throw DC against one creature adjacent to your spirit.

This allows your spirit to establish a useful casting position while you remain elsewhere on the battlefield. Self-range spells cannot normally be projected through your spirit.

At higher levels, Greater Shared Aether increases its range and provides additional Relay options. Use these improvements to extend your tactical reach and coordinate your spell effects with your spirit's actions.

4. Balance Your Offensive and Defensive Bond Techniques

Spirit Bond is not exclusively an offensive resource. Your basic Bond techniques give you different ways to respond to combat situations.

  • Bonded Assault: Spend Bond when your spirit hits to add independent damage. You do not need to spend it before knowing whether the attack hits.
  • Protective Bond: Use your Reaction when you or your spirit takes attack damage while within 30 feet of the other. It reduces the triggering attack's combined damage.
  • Spirit Recall: Use your Bonus Action to teleport your spirit to a legal destination under the feature's distance restrictions. This can help restore a useful position without provoking Opportunity Attacks.

Try to preserve enough Bond for an important defensive reaction when facing dangerous enemies. Spending every available point on damage can leave you without the resources needed to protect yourself or your spirit.

5. Develop Your Partnership Through Your Specialization

Your Specialization determines how your partnership with the elemental spirit develops. Choose the path that best matches the way you want to contribute to combat.

  • Spirit Caller: Strengthens your spirit's attacks and elemental capabilities. Choose this path if you want your spirit to become a major source of offensive pressure.
  • Hexbinder: Improves your ability to weaken enemies through curses and debilitating effects. Choose this path if you prefer controlling and undermining important enemy targets.
  • Bondweaver: Improves Spirit Bond efficiency, protection, and shared resilience. Choose this path if you want a more durable and resource-efficient partnership.

You gain Specialization advancements at Levels 3, 6, 10, and 14. At Levels 6, 10, and 14, you may continue your current Specialization or reassign according to the class's Specialization Reassignment rules.

6. Maintain the Partnership and Adapt to the Battlefield

Your spirit is powerful, but it is also a creature that can be targeted, damaged, and defeated. Pay attention to its HP, position, and distance from you. Protective Bond and Spirit Recall can help keep the partnership functioning when the battlefield becomes dangerous.

  • Do not leave your spirit unnecessarily exposed when it is already low on HP.
  • Keep the spirit close enough to support Shared Aether and Protective Bond when those features are important.
  • Use its movement to establish a better position rather than treating it as a stationary summon.
  • Remember that the spirit shares your Reaction. If either of you uses it, neither can use another Reaction until the start of your next turn.
  • Plan for spirit recovery carefully. Voluntary dismissal or replacement does not qualify as the spirit being reduced to 0 HP.

A successful Spiritmaster does not simply cast spells while a spirit attacks independently. You are constantly coordinating two positions, two sources of battlefield influence, and a shared resource. The better you manage that partnership, the more effective you become.
