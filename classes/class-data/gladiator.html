“Nothing can stand in my way!”

The premier sustained melee damage class, built around deliberate attack sequences, Momentum, Chain Links, and Finishers.

The Gladiator is the premier sustained melee damage class. It wins through sequences rather than single isolated attacks: open a Chain, spend Momentum on Links that alter the battlefield, then cash the sequence out with a Finisher. Polearms provide the highest sustained weapon output, while weapon discipline and specialization determine whether the Gladiator emphasizes execution, cleaving pressure, or relentless pursuit.

Class Identity

Gladiators are Daevas who turn martial mastery into relentless battlefield pressure. They are not defined by a single weapon or one decisive blow, but by the ability to seize momentum, force an opening, and keep an enemy under pressure until the fight breaks in their favor. In Wings of Atreia, this identity is expressed through Momentum and Chain Attacks: the Gladiator becomes more dangerous when attacks, movement, and finishers are linked into a deliberate sequence.

Tradition & Outlook

The Gladiator tradition descends from the broad warrior disciplines of Atreia and is associated with soldiers, arena fighters, legion veterans, weapon masters, and Daevas who believe that skill must survive contact with a real battlefield. Gladiators commonly value courage, physical discipline, adaptability, and decisive action. Some fight for glory or personal mastery; others see themselves as the hard edge of a legion, the Daeva who advances when everyone else needs the line to move.

Appearance & Adventuring

Gladiators usually present themselves as practical combatants: heavy armor, battle-worn weapons, and equipment chosen for reach, leverage, or sustained close combat. Polearms and other approved Gladiator weapons may suggest very different fighting schools, but the class is recognized by an aggressive, forward-driving presence. A Gladiator adventures to test mastery, protect comrades through offensive pressure, earn renown, serve a military cause, or become strong enough to face the threats of the Abyss and Balaurea directly.

Core Mechanics & Resources

Momentum

Generation: Your first melee hit on your own turn grants 1 Momentum. Once on each of your turns, Aggressive Momentum grants 1 additional Momentum when one of the three rules are met:

  • A melee hit follows at least 15 ft of movement toward that target.
  • You scored a critical hit
  • You reduced a hostile creature to 0 HP.

Expenditure: Gained Momentum is used to spend specified Chain Links and Finishers.

Recovery: Later features may refund or grant Momentum after a Link or Finisher resolves or at a stated timing. A refund is not a new Momentum-generation event unless the feature explicitly says otherwise.

Momentum and Chains and Chain Links

Momentum represents the Gladiator forcing the battle forward. It exists only in genuine combat and resets to 0 when combat ends. Normal Momentum does not decay simply because you failed to spend it; later features care about maintaining an active Chain instead.

  • A single attack cannot normally grant more than the Momentum allowed by the generation rules above.
  • A Chain begins when you declare a successful melee hit on your own turn to be an Opener. You can maintain only one Chain at a time. The Chain belongs to you, not to a particular enemy, so later Links may strike another legal target.
  • An active Chain remains in effect until the end of the Gladiator's next turn. If the Gladiator successfully hits at least one hostile creature with their own melee weapon attack during that turn, the Chain's duration refreshes until the end of the following turn. If the Gladiator fails to hit any hostile creature with their own melee weapon attack before the current duration expires, the Chain ends. A Chain also ends when the Gladiator uses a Finisher, voluntarily abandons it, or becomes unable to maintain it.
  • A Gladiator may apply a Chain Link to any successful melee weapon attack made on their own turn provided that the Gladiator can pay the Momentum cost and the specific Chain Link's requirements are satisfied.
  • A Finisher ends the Chain after it resolves.
  • Chain Dice added directly to the triggering weapon attack double on a critical hit. Independent secondary damage does not.
  • Chain Links occur when there is at least 1 link after the declared Opener is applied. This further strengthens the Finisher. The more links there are, the stronger the Finisher move is. Waiting for the right moment before cashing the links out with a Finisher rewards the Gladiator with additional damage modifiers after the Finisher move as shown in the Class Progression table under Damage per Chain Link column. Chain Links and damage modifiers of the Chain Links do not double on a critical hit.
  • When a Gladiator uses a Finisher, the Finisher gains an additional damage bonus based on the number of Chain Links accumulated before it. Each Chain Link contributes a bonus determined by the Gladiator's level: +1 damage per Chain Link at levels 1–6, +2 damage per Chain Link at levels 7–14, and +3 damage per Chain Link at levels 15–20. The Opener does not count as a Chain Link. Add the total bonus to the Finisher's Chain Dice damage.

Universal Gladiator Chain Techniques

Driving Strike. Costs 1 Momentum. Requires a melee hit that can carry a Link. Add 1 Chain Die to the attack. If the target is no more than one size larger, push it 5 ft. Does not double on critical hit.

Sweeping Strike. Costs 1 Momentum. Requires a melee hit and can only be done once per turn. You can choose another hostile within your reach and within 5 ft of the original target. It takes 1 Chain Die + STR modifier damage. Does not double on critical hit.

Crushing Finisher. Costs 2 Momentum. Requires at least 1 prior Link. Add 2 Chain Die to the triggering attack. The Chain ends after resolution. This doubles on a critical hit.

Hamstring Strike. Costs 1 Momentum. Level 5 ability. Requires at least 1 prior Link. Add 1 Chain Die and reduce the target's Speed by 10 ft until the start of your next turn. Does not double on a critical hit.

Staggering Strike. Costs 2 Momentum. Level 11 ability. Requires at least 1 prior Link. Add 1 Chain Die. The target cannot take Reactions until the start of your next turn.

Devastating Finisher. Costs 3 Momentum. Level 11 ability. Requires at least 2 prior Links. Add 3 Chain Die. The target makes a STR save against your Class Save DC. On a failure, the target falls prone. Creatures more than one size larger have Advantage on the Save. The Chain ends. This doubles on a critical hit.

Titanic Finisher. Costs 4 Momentum. Level 17 ability. Requires at least 3 prior Links. Add 4 Chain Die. The target makes a STR save against your Class Save DC. On a failure, you can choose the target to become Prone and push 20 ft, Prone only, or push 30 ft and not prone. Creatures more than one size larger have Advantage. The Chain ends. This doubles on a critical hit.

Ultimate Ability

HeavenbreakerUltimate

Ultimate Activation: Costs 100 Daeva Points (DP).

As your Action and Bonus Action, move up to your Speed toward a creature you can see without provoking Opportunity Attacks. You must end within your melee reach, then make one Ultimate melee weapon attack against it. After this Ultimate resolves, you enter Ultimate Recovery during your next turn.

Primary Damage: The attack deals its normal weapon damage plus fixed additional damage of the ability.

Shockwave. Every other hostile creature within 10 feet of the primary target makes a DEX save against your Gladiator Class Save DC. Every affected creature takes fixed Force damage on a failure, or half that amount on a success.

Rules, Recovery and Restrictions:

Ultimate Attack Ability. Activating an Ultimate consumes both the character's Action and Bonus Action. The character cannot activate it unless both are available, but may still move and take Reactions normally. The Ultimate Ability is not an Attack action and cannot be duplicated by Extra Attack, Quick Shot, Akimbo, additional-action effects, reactions, or similar features.

Fixed Ultimate Output. Damage printed on the corresponding level as the Ultimate's own direct numerical output are not rolled; use the maximum value of those die. Normal weapon damage and damage dice from other features remain rolled normally.

Specializations

Three distinct paths, with advancement features at the established specialization levels.

DESTROYER

The Destroyer specializes in devastating single-target attacks, turning carefully built Chains into increasingly lethal finishing blows. Its abilities reward targeting weakened enemies, making it ideal for players who enjoy setting up powerful executions and delivering decisive damage.

TEMPEST

The Tempest expands the Gladiator's Chain Attacks into powerful multi-target assaults. It excels at pressuring groups of nearby enemies, striking multiple opponents with increasingly destructive sweeping attacks and controlling the flow of melee combat.

WARPATH

The Warpath is a relentless frontline pursuer that thrives on movement and aggressive engagement. It improves the Gladiator's ability to charge, break through enemy positions, and maintain pressure while moving from one opponent to another.

How to Play Gladiator

1. Your Role and Gameplay Loop

Your Role as a Gladiator

The Gladiator is a sustained melee damage dealer who excels at maintaining offensive pressure through deliberate attack sequences.

Rather than relying on a single powerful attack, you build Momentum through successful melee attacks, establish a Chain, apply Chain Links, and eventually unleash a Finisher.

Your primary objectives are to:

  • Engage enemies and establish your Chain.
  • Generate and manage Momentum.
  • Apply Chain Links to strengthen your eventual Finisher.
  • Maintain your Chain by continuing to hit hostile creatures.
  • Choose the right moment to unleash your Finisher.

A Gladiator should remain an active threat throughout combat. Your effectiveness depends on combining offensive pressure, resource management, and good positioning.

Your Core Gameplay Loop

  1. Engage: Move into melee reach of a suitable enemy.
  2. Open: Land a successful melee hit and declare it as your Opener.
  3. Build: Apply Chain Links to subsequent successful melee weapon attacks when their requirements are met.
  4. Maintain: Hit at least one hostile creature with your own melee weapon attack during the required turn to refresh your Chain.
  5. Finish: Spend the required Momentum to unleash a Finisher.
  6. Continue: Look for another opportunity to establish a new Chain.

You do not need to use a Finisher as soon as you accumulate your first Link. Building a longer Chain can increase your Finisher's damage bonus. However, do not wait so long that your Chain expires or your target becomes inaccessible.

Your JobSustained melee damage and continuous offensive pressure.
Your ResourceMomentum, used to power Chain techniques and Finishers.
Your PositionStay within melee reach, anticipate enemy movement, and look for opportunities to engage multiple targets.

2. Momentum and Chain Management

Generating Momentum

Momentum fuels your Chain techniques and Finishers. Your first melee hit on your own turn grants 1 Momentum. Once on each of your turns, Aggressive Momentum can grant 1 additional Momentum when a qualifying condition is met:

  • A melee hit follows at least 15 feet of movement toward that target.
  • You score a Critical Hit.
  • You reduce a hostile creature to 0 HP.

Your maximum Momentum increases as you advance in level.

Practical tips:

  • Keep track of your current Momentum and maximum capacity.
  • Consider the cost of your intended Chain technique or Finisher before spending.
  • Do not spend Momentum simply because you have it.
  • Unused Momentum does not normally decay.
  • Momentum resets to 0 when combat ends.

Establishing Your Chain

A Chain begins when you declare a successful melee hit on your own turn as an Opener. You can maintain only one Chain at a time.

Your Chain belongs to you, not to a particular enemy. This means you can change targets without automatically losing your Chain.

Once your Chain is active, you may apply a Chain Link to any successful melee weapon attack made on your own turn, provided that you satisfy its requirements and can pay its Momentum cost.

Maintaining Your Chain

An active Chain remains in effect until the end of your next turn. During that turn, you must successfully hit at least one hostile creature with your own melee weapon attack. If you do, your Chain refreshes until the end of the following turn.

Your Chain ends when its duration expires without a qualifying hit, you use a Finisher and it resolves, you voluntarily abandon it, or you become unable to maintain it.

Practical tips:

  • You do not need to attack the same enemy to maintain your Chain.
  • You do not need to apply a Chain Link to every successful attack.
  • If your original target moves away, look for another hostile creature within reach.
  • Remember that maintaining your Chain and generating Momentum are separate mechanics.

Building Toward a Finisher

Every accumulated Chain Link contributes additional damage to your Finisher. The bonus per Link is +1 at levels 1–6, +2 at levels 7–14, and +3 at levels 15–20.

The Opener does not count as a Chain Link. The accumulated bonus is added to the Finisher's Chain Dice damage and does not double on a Critical Hit.

Practical tips:

  • Build additional Links when the opportunity is available.
  • Consider your remaining Momentum before committing to a Finisher.
  • Do not risk losing an entire Chain just to gain one more Link.
  • Consider the enemy's position and ability to escape before deciding to continue building.

3. Chain Techniques and Finishers

Driving Strike

Driving Strike costs 1 Momentum. It adds 1 Chain Die to the triggering attack and pushes the target 5 feet if the target is no more than one size larger than you.

When to use it: When pushing an enemy away from an important position is useful, when you want to create space between an enemy and its allies, or when both the additional damage and forced movement are valuable. Consider where the enemy will end up before using the push.

Sweeping Strike

Sweeping Strike costs 1 Momentum and can be used once per turn. It allows you to damage another hostile creature within your reach and within 5 feet of the original target. The secondary target takes 1 Chain Die plus your Strength modifier in damage.

When to use it: When multiple enemies are close together, when you can reach more than one enemy, or when spreading damage across a group is more valuable than focusing on one target. Look for opportunities to position yourself near groups of enemies.

Hamstring Strike

Hamstring Strike is available at Level 5 and costs 1 Momentum. It requires at least one prior Chain Link. It adds 1 Chain Die and reduces the target's Speed by 10 feet until the start of your next turn.

When to use it: Against enemies that rely on movement, are attempting to escape, or whose ability to reposition would be troublesome for your party.

Staggering Strike

Staggering Strike is available at Level 11 and costs 2 Momentum. It requires at least one prior Chain Link. It adds 1 Chain Die and prevents the target from taking Reactions until the start of your next turn.

When to use it: Against enemies that rely on Reactions, when your allies need an opportunity to reposition, or when preventing a particular enemy from using Reactions would benefit the party.

Crushing Finisher

Crushing Finisher costs 2 Momentum and requires at least one prior Chain Link. It adds 2 Chain Dice to the triggering attack and ends your Chain after resolution.

Use it when you want to convert your accumulated Links into additional damage without waiting for the higher-level Finishers.

Devastating Finisher

Devastating Finisher is available at Level 11. It costs 3 Momentum and requires at least two prior Chain Links. It adds 3 Chain Dice to the triggering attack.

The target must make a Strength saving throw against your Class Save DC. On a failure, it becomes Prone. Creatures more than one size larger than you have Advantage on this saving throw. The Chain ends after resolution.

Use this Finisher when both its damage and its potential knockdown are valuable.

Titanic Finisher

Titanic Finisher is available at Level 17. It costs 4 Momentum and requires at least three prior Chain Links. It adds 4 Chain Dice to the triggering attack.

The target makes a Strength saving throw against your Class Save DC. On a failure, choose one of the following:

  • Knock the target Prone and push it 20 feet.
  • Knock the target Prone without pushing it.
  • Push the target 30 feet without knocking it Prone.

Creatures more than one size larger than you have Advantage on the saving throw. The Chain ends after resolution.

Use this Finisher when you need a powerful attack that can also significantly alter an enemy's battlefield position.

4. Positioning and Aerial Combat

Understanding Melee Positioning

The Gladiator depends on melee attacks. Your position determines which enemies you can engage and whether you can maintain your Chain.

  • Most melee weapons have 5 feet of reach.
  • Reach weapons generally have 10 feet of reach.
  • Being near an enemy does not automatically mean you are within reach.
  • Consider enemy movement and likely position before committing to an attack sequence.

A good Gladiator does not simply chase the nearest enemy. A good Gladiator positions themselves to maintain offensive pressure.

Understanding Aerial Altitude

Wings of Atreia uses four airborne altitude layers:

Low30 feet
Mid60 feet
High90 feet
Legendary120 feet

Ground level is 0 feet and is not an altitude layer. Voluntarily ascending or descending one altitude layer consumes all movement for that turn. Dash does not permit an additional altitude-layer change during the same turn.

Practical tips:

  • Decide which altitude you need before committing to an altitude change.
  • Do not assume that you can change altitude and still use your remaining movement to pursue an enemy.
  • Anticipate where your target will be before choosing your altitude.

Understanding Aerial Melee Reach

Aerial combat uses ordinary melee reach, but distance is calculated differently. WoA uses whichever is greater: horizontal distance or vertical distance.

For example, if you are 10 feet horizontally from an enemy and 20 feet vertically from that enemy, your aerial targeting distance is 20 feet.

Being directly above an enemy does not automatically put you within melee reach.

Practical tips:

  • Pay attention to both horizontal and vertical separation.
  • Reach weapons can provide additional engagement distance.
  • Position yourself before committing to an altitude change.
  • Do not assume that sharing an altitude layer automatically puts you within reach.

Using Descending Attack

Descending Attack provides an important offensive opportunity for melee combatants. If you begin your turn higher than your target, descend at least 25 feet, and then make your first qualifying melee attack against that lower target, the attack has Advantage.

Example:

  1. You begin your turn at a higher altitude than your enemy.
  2. You descend at least 25 feet.
  3. You are positioned within melee reach of the enemy after descending.
  4. You make your first qualifying melee attack against that lower target.
  5. That attack has Advantage.

Practical tips:

  • Look for opportunities to engage enemies below you.
  • Plan your horizontal position before descending because the altitude change consumes all your movement.
  • Consider using a successful attack as the Opener of a new Chain.
  • Descending Attack does not automatically grant Advantage to every attack you make during that turn.

Aggressive Momentum in Aerial Combat

Aggressive Momentum can be particularly useful when pursuing enemies. However, its movement requirement still matters: you must move at least 15 feet toward the target before hitting it.

Do not assume that every vertical descent automatically qualifies. The actual movement must satisfy the requirement of moving toward that target.

Dealing with Flying Enemies

Flying enemies can reposition themselves by changing altitude or moving away from melee reach. This can make maintaining a Chain more difficult.

Practical tips:

  • Avoid committing to an attack sequence if you cannot reasonably reach your target.
  • Watch for opportunities when flying enemies descend toward you.
  • If your original target becomes inaccessible, look for another hostile creature to attack and maintain your Chain.
  • Consider your available movement and altitude before pursuing an enemy.
  • Do not spend all your resources attempting to reach a target if you cannot complete your intended attack.

Using Relentless Advance

Relentless Advance becomes available at Level 7. When a hostile creature within your melee reach willingly moves away, you can use your Reaction to move up to half your Speed, ending closer to that creature. This movement does not provoke an Opportunity Attack from the triggering creature.

This ability does not grant an attack.

Practical tips:

  • Keep your Reaction available when engaging mobile enemies.
  • Use the movement to remain closer to an enemy attempting to withdraw.
  • Remember that this ability does not override the normal altitude-change restrictions.

5. Higher-Level Features and Specializations

Level 5 — Extra Attack

At Level 5, you gain Extra Attack. Your Attack action allows you to make two attacks instead of one.

Practical tip: Having two attacks gives you more opportunities to land a successful melee hit, apply Chain Links, and maintain your Chain.

Level 9 — Momentum Mastery

Your maximum Momentum increases to 5.

Practical tip: A larger Momentum capacity gives you more flexibility when deciding whether to spend resources on Chain techniques or save them for a Finisher.

Level 13 — Unbroken Momentum

Unbroken Momentum provides two important benefits:

  • A successful Chain Link refreshes your active Chain.
  • If you begin your turn with an active Chain and 0 Momentum, you gain 1 Momentum once per turn.

Practical tip: Maintaining your Chain becomes especially valuable at this level. Even when your Momentum is depleted, this feature can help you continue your sequence.

Level 15 — Endless Assault

After a Finisher hits and fully resolves, you can spend 1 Momentum to make one melee weapon attack as a Bonus Action.

This additional attack cannot carry a Chain Link or Finisher. However, it may be declared as the Opener of a new Chain after the previous Chain ends.

Practical tip: Think of Endless Assault as an opportunity to transition from one Chain sequence into another.

Level 17 — Perfect Chain Sequence

Perfect Chain Sequence rewards you for using three different Chain Links in the same Chain. After resolving three different Chain Links in that Chain, your next Finisher costs 1 less Momentum, to a minimum cost of 1.

Practical tip: Consider which different Chain Links are useful in the current encounter. You can build toward this benefit while adapting to the battlefield.

Choosing Your Specialization

Your specialization determines the direction of your Gladiator's combat style.

  • Destroyer — Single-target execution: Focuses on devastating individual enemies. Build your Chain and look for opportunities to deliver powerful finishing attacks against priority targets.
  • Tempest — Multi-target pressure: Focuses on attacking groups of enemies. Look for opportunities to engage multiple opponents and apply pressure across the battlefield.
  • Warpath — Movement and pursuit: Focuses on aggressive engagement, movement, and pursuit. Use its abilities to close gaps, advance through enemy positions, and maintain pressure against mobile opponents.

6. Combat Examples and Common Mistakes

Example: Ground Combat Against One Enemy

  1. Move into melee reach.
  2. Make a successful melee attack and declare it as your Opener.
  3. Generate Momentum according to your class rules.
  4. Continue attacking and apply Chain Links when their requirements are met.
  5. Maintain your Chain by successfully hitting a hostile creature during the required turn.
  6. Use a Finisher when you have accumulated enough Links and have sufficient Momentum.

Lesson: Build your Chain deliberately. Do not rush your Finisher, but do not risk losing the Chain by waiting unnecessarily.

Example: Descending Attack Against an Enemy Below

  1. Begin your turn at a higher altitude than the target.
  2. Descend at least 25 feet.
  3. Make sure you are within melee reach after descending.
  4. Make your first qualifying melee attack against the lower target.
  5. Apply the Advantage granted by Descending Attack.
  6. If the attack hits and you want to establish a Chain, declare it as your Opener.

Lesson: Aerial positioning can create offensive opportunities. Plan your position before descending because the altitude change consumes all your movement.

Example: Your Enemy Moves Away

  1. You have an active Chain against an enemy.
  2. The enemy willingly moves away while within your melee reach.
  3. If you have Relentless Advance, consider using your Reaction to move closer.
  4. If the enemy remains inaccessible, look for another hostile creature within your reach.
  5. Successfully hit that creature with your own melee weapon attack to maintain your Chain.

Lesson: Your Chain does not belong to one enemy. Adapt to the battlefield instead of allowing one target's movement to end your sequence.

Example: Several Enemies Are Grouped Together

  1. Position yourself where multiple enemies are within your reach.
  2. Establish a Chain against one of them.
  3. Use Sweeping Strike when its requirements are met.
  4. Continue building your Chain while applying pressure to available targets.
  5. Choose a Finisher based on the situation.

Lesson: The Gladiator can pressure groups of enemies as well as individual targets.

Common Gladiator Mistakes

  1. Spending Momentum too quickly. Consider what you may need for your next Chain technique or Finisher.
  2. Forgetting Chain maintenance. You must successfully hit at least one hostile creature with your own melee weapon attack during the required turn.
  3. Fixating on one enemy. Your Chain belongs to you, not to a particular target.
  4. Waiting too long to use a Finisher. Your Chain can expire before you get another opportunity.
  5. Ignoring positioning. Melee reach and enemy movement are important.
  6. Treating altitude changes as free movement. Ascending or descending one altitude layer consumes all your movement for that turn.
  7. Assuming every aerial attack has Advantage. Descending Attack has specific requirements.
  8. Forgetting your Reaction abilities. Relentless Advance can help you pursue an enemy that moves away.
  9. Ignoring different Chain techniques. Choose techniques based on their tactical benefits rather than repeating the same option automatically.
