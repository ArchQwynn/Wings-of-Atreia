“A second of elemental fury. An eternity of silence.”

The Aetheric spellcaster wielding destructive and controlling magic.

The Sorcerer is the raw magical-damage benchmark. It casts through Fire, Ice, Lightning, and Wind spell chains, then spends Aether Focus only when a particular spell needs more damage, area, control pressure, or reliability. Aether Focus enhances spells; it is never the fuel required to cast ordinary Sorcerer spells.

Class Identity

Sorcerers are masters of direct Aetheric spellcraft, combining destructive magic with the ability to control the shape and tempo of a fight. They can punish clustered enemies, deny movement or action, and unleash powerful spells when an opening appears. Aether Focus represents the concentration and arcane discipline required to turn immense magical potential into reliable battlefield power.

Tradition & Outlook

The Sorcerer tradition grows from Atreia’s mage disciplines and is associated with formal magical study, experimentation, academies, battlefield arcanists, and Daevas fascinated by the structure of Aether itself. Sorcerers commonly value knowledge, precision, preparation, and intellectual mastery, although their personalities range from scholarly restraint to spectacular confidence. For many, magic is both a weapon and a language through which Atreia can be understood.

Appearance & Adventuring

Sorcerers favor cloth armor and magical implements such as Orbs and Spellbooks under the Wings of Atreia equipment rules. Their appearance may reflect academic, military, ceremonial, or highly individual magical traditions. They adventure to recover lost knowledge, study Aetheric phenomena, test magical theories, defend their people with overwhelming spellcraft, or confront powers that ordinary weapons cannot adequately answer.

Core Mechanics & Resources

Aether Focus

Maximum: Your Maximum Aether Focus is equivalent to your Proficiency Bonus + your INT modifier, minimum of 2. A Long Rest restores all expended Aether Focus. A Short Rest restores Aether Focus equal to PB, up to your maximum. The resource is an enhancement reserve: casting an ordinary Sorcerer spell never requires Aether Focus unless a spell or class feature explicitly says otherwise. Aether Sage specialization and the Level 20 capstone explicitly increase this maximum.

Starting State: Aether Focus is a rest-based enhancement reserve and does not reset merely because combat begins or ends. After a Long Rest it is normally full.

Recovery: Long Rest restores all expended Aether Focus. Short Rest restores Focus equal to PB, up to maximum; Aether Sage specialization can improve this recovery. Unused Aether Focus persists between encounters until spent or restored.

Base Generation: The base Sorcerer does not generate Aether Focus from ordinary spell hits or damage. Combat refunds exist only where a class or specialization feature explicitly grants them.

Spellcasting Acquisition and Spellcasting

Spells are class-granted abilities. They cannot be bought, sold, looted, copied from spellbooks or scrolls, learned from vendors, or acquired by commerce or exploration. A spell becomes available only when the character reaches its Unlock Level and, when applicable, satisfies the immediately preceding spell in its linear prerequisite chain. Unlocking a later spell never removes the earlier spell. Newly unlocked spells begin at Spell Level 1 unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Spell Advancement raises one eligible unlocked spell by 1 Spell Level, subject to your class’s Maximum Spell Level and listed prerequisites. Unspent Spell Advancements may be banked indefinitely, but banked or newly earned Spell Advancements may be spent only when you gain a level; they cannot normally be spent during an adventure, between encounters, or during a rest. You cannot spend more Spell Advancements than what you have earned.

Spell Retraining. There is no universal spell retraining. Once a Spell Advancement is spent, it is permanent unless a specific class feature, explicit story effect, or GM-approved exceptional circumstance directly permits reassignment.

Spell Shaping

Intensify= Costs 1 Aether Focus. When an eligible Sorcerer spell deals damage, increase one damage resolution by +1d6.

Widen= Costs 1 Aether Focus. Increase an eligible radius or cone by 5 ft; a line by 10 ft; or a cube by 5 ft on each side. The spell must have an area that can legally be widened.

Potent Control= Costs 1 Aether Focus. Choose one creature affected by an eligible spell whose non-damaging control or movement rider requires a save. That creature has Disadvantage on its first save against that rider for this casting.

Spell Shaping – Your Spell Shaping enhances only a single spell. You choose and pay the shaping cost which is 1 of your Aether Focus when the spell is cast, before its affected rolls are fully resolved unless the feature says otherwise. Spell Shaping is not required for every spells. Spending on Spell Shaping depends entirely on you.

Ultimate Ability

Aetheric CataclysmUltimate

Ultimate Activation: Costs 100 Daeva Points (DP).

As your Action and Bonus Action, make one damaging spell attack against a creature you can see within 120 ft. After this Ultimate resolves, you enter Ultimate Recovery during your turn.

Primary Damage: The attack deals its normal spell damage calculation plus fixed Force damage. This Force damage is independent from the type of damage the triggering spell has.

Cataclysm. Every other hostile creature within a certain distance from the primary target makes a DEX save against your Spell Save DC. Every affected creature takes fixed Force damage on a failure, or half that amount on a success. You may excluse allies from the sphere.

Rules, Recovery and Restrictions:

Ultimate Attack Ability. Activating an Ultimate consumes both the character's Action and Bonus Action. The character cannot activate it unless both are available, but may still move and take Reactions normally. The Ultimate Ability is not an Attack action and cannot be duplicated by Extra Attack, Quick Shot, Akimbo, additional-action effects, reactions, or similar features.

Fixed Ultimate Output. Damage printed on the corresponding level as the Ultimate's own direct numerical output are not rolled; use the maximum value of those die. Normal weapon damage and damage dice from other features remain rolled normally.

Specializations

Three distinct paths, with advancement features at the established specialization levels.

ELEMENTALIST

The Elementalist devotes its spellcraft to one chosen element: Fire, Ice, or Lightning. It progressively strengthens the destructive or controlling properties of that element, making it ideal for players who want a defined magical identity and specialized elemental attacks.

SPELLBINDER

The Spellbinder manipulates the battlefield through magical seals, persistent spell areas, and restrictions on enemy actions. It specializes in controlling enemy positioning and movement while improving its ability to maintain powerful ongoing spells.

AETHER SAGE

The Aether Sage focuses on mastering Aether Focus and expanding the flexibility of the Sorcerer's spell-shaping techniques. It gains greater adaptability, improved resource efficiency, and access to increasingly versatile casting options.

How to Play Sorcerer

The Sorcerer is a powerful Aetheric spellcaster specializing in magical damage and battlefield control. Your strength comes from choosing the right spell for the situation and deciding when to enhance it with Aether Focus. You can devastate groups of enemies, pressure individual targets, restrict enemy movement, and manipulate the battlefield through elemental and arcane magic.

Your ordinary spells do not consume Aether Focus. This allows you to cast your available spells without constantly worrying about exhausting your class resource. Aether Focus is reserved for moments when enhancing a spell will make a meaningful difference.

Core Gameplay Loop

Choose a spell that suits the situation → Identify whether Spell Shaping would improve its effect → Spend Aether Focus if the enhancement is worthwhile → Resolve the spell and its effects → Reposition or prepare your next spell → Recover Aether Focus when appropriate.

Your JobDeal substantial magical damage while controlling enemy movement, actions, and battlefield space.
Your ResourceAether Focus enhances spells but is not required for ordinary spellcasting.
Your PositionMaintain a position that allows you to cast effectively, reach important targets, and avoid unnecessary exposure.

1. Choose the Right Spell for the Situation

Your spell selection is the foundation of your effectiveness. Sorcerers have access to Fire, Ice, Lightning, and Wind spell chains, each providing different offensive and control opportunities.

  • Use damaging spells when you need to pressure or eliminate a priority target.
  • Use area-of-effect spells when enemies are clustered together.
  • Use control spells when restricting enemy movement or actions would benefit your party.
  • Consider the secondary effects of your spells, not just their damage.

You do not need to spend Aether Focus every time you cast a spell. An ordinary Sorcerer spell works without it unless the spell or a class feature explicitly requires Focus.

2. Enhance Important Spells with Aether Focus

Spell Shaping gives you three ways to improve your spells:

  • Intensify: Increase one damage resolution when an eligible spell deals damage.
  • Widen: Increase the area of an eligible spell, allowing it to affect a larger space.
  • Potent Control: Make an affected creature less likely to resist an eligible non-damaging control or movement effect.

At Level 5, Greater Spell Shaping improves all three options. At Level 11, Dual Shaping lets you combine two different options on one spell. At Level 14, an Aether Sage can use Triune Shaping to apply all three together when legal.

Choose enhancements according to what the spell needs. Intensify is useful when damage matters most, Widen when area coverage matters, and Potent Control when landing a control effect is especially important.

3. Manage Your Aether Focus

Aether Focus is an enhancement reserve, not a spellcasting cost. Consider how much Focus you have available before committing to combinations of Spell Shaping options.

  • A Long Rest restores all expended Aether Focus.
  • A Short Rest restores Focus equal to your Proficiency Bonus, up to your maximum.
  • At Level 9, Aetheric Recovery lets you recover additional Focus once per Short Rest as a Bonus Action.
  • At Level 15, Near-Apex Spell Mastery improves this recovery to twice per Short Rest.
  • Aether Sage features improve your maximum Focus and resource efficiency.

Avoid spending Focus simply because it is available. Save it for spells where the additional damage, area, or control pressure is likely to matter.

4. Control the Battlefield and Maintain Your Concentration

Your spells can do more than deal damage. They can restrict movement, deny reactions, force enemies into unfavorable positions, and maintain pressure over an area.

Pay attention to the placement of area spells and the creatures affected by them. Your Focused Casting Doctrine can help improve your reliability:

  • Stable Channel improves the consistency of rolled spell damage.
  • Precise Projection allows you to exclude one visible creature from an eligible area spell.
  • Anchored Concentration helps protect a shaped Concentration spell against the first qualifying hostile interruption.

At Level 13, Dual Concentration allows you to maintain two Concentration spells simultaneously once per Long Rest. Casting a third Concentration spell while maintaining two requires you to end one of them.

5. Flight and Aerial Combat

Sorcerers can participate in aerial combat, but they must understand that altitude-based combat adjustments apply differently to martial combatants and spellcasters.

Spellcasting and Altitude

Flight mechanics affect melee and ranged martial combat, not spellcasters. Do not treat the altitude-based adjustments to melee attacks or martial ranged weapon range as automatic changes to a Sorcerer's spell range.

Your spellcasting remains governed by the spell's own range and normal casting requirements. Flight is therefore primarily a positioning and survival consideration for the Sorcerer rather than a way to increase spell range.

Watch for Melee Combatants Above You

Be especially careful when hostile melee combatants occupy a higher altitude than you. An enemy above you can threaten you with a descending melee attack. Do not assume that remaining airborne automatically protects you from melee attackers.

Keep track of hostile creatures' altitude and consider whether an enemy can descend into an advantageous attack position. Maintain awareness of your surroundings to avoid being caught in an unfavorable aerial engagement.

Respect High-Altitude Ranged Threats

Ranged martial combatants become especially dangerous when they occupy higher altitudes because their effective weapon range increases under the flight rules.

Pay particular attention to hostile Rangers, Gunslingers, and Aethertechs. Their elevated positions can allow them to threaten you from farther away than they could from a lower altitude.

Avoid remaining exposed to these attackers when possible. Use available cover, battlefield positioning, and your spell choices to respond to their presence.

Aerial Positioning Priorities

  • Watch the altitude of hostile melee combatants, especially those positioned above you.
  • Identify elevated ranged attackers and account for their increased weapon range.
  • Avoid assuming that greater altitude automatically makes you safe.
  • Choose aerial positions that let you cast your spells while limiting your exposure to martial attacks.
  • Remember that your spell range is determined by the spell itself, not by the altitude-based range adjustments used by martial combatants.

6. Adapt Your Strategy to Your Specialization

Your specialization determines how you develop your Sorcerer's strengths.

Elementalist — Elemental Specialization

Choose Fire, Ice, or Lightning as your Devoted Element. Focus your spell selection and enhancements around that element. Your specialization rewards you for successfully affecting hostile creatures with Devoted Element spells and improves the additional effects you can apply after shaping them.

Spellbinder — Battlefield Control

Use your spells to restrict enemy movement and actions. Arcane Seal and Residual Constraint provide additional control opportunities, while Binding Geometry lets you reposition eligible persistent spell areas. Anchored Formula helps protect your Concentration, and Perfect Binding strengthens the control effects of Potent Control.

Aether Sage — Spell Shaping and Resource Mastery

Use your Aether Focus more efficiently and gain greater flexibility when enhancing spells. Deep Reservoir increases your resource capacity, Adaptive Doctrine gives you access to another Focused Casting Doctrine, and Efficient Channel improves resource efficiency. Triune Shaping eventually allows you to combine all three Spell Shaping options.

Advanced Sorcerer Features

As you advance, your Sorcerer gains additional ways to influence combat:

  • Double Casting (Level 7): Once per Short Rest, cast two eligible single-target Sorcerer spells instead of one, targeting two creatures.
  • Enhanced Double Casting (Level 14): Expand your choices for how the two spells target creatures, including the option to combine a single-target spell with an area-of-effect spell.
  • Spell Mastery (Level 17): Once per Short Rest, reduce the turn-based cooldown imposed by a Sorcerer spell you cast by 1, to a minimum of 0.
  • Aetheric Transposition (Level 18): Temporarily teleport to a valid location within the spell's effective range when casting a Sorcerer spell, then return to your original position before your turn ends.
  • Perfect Sorcerer (Level 20): Increase your maximum Aether Focus, gain Battle-ready Focus, and improve the efficiency of Spell Shaping through Perfect Shaping.

Using Your Ultimate

Aetheric Cataclysm costs 100 Daeva Points and consumes both your Action and Bonus Action. It makes a damaging spell attack against a creature you can see within 120 feet, followed by secondary Force damage against hostile creatures around the primary target.

Use it when a major target or group of enemies presents a worthwhile opportunity. Remember that activating an Ultimate consumes both actions, and the Ultimate cannot be duplicated by additional attacks or similar effects.
