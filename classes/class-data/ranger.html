“Chase me or run away. Either way, you'll only die tired.”

A nonmagical ranged hunter who uses battlefield traps to control enemy movement and spends Focus on precise or evasive shots against ensnared targets.

The Ranger is a nonmagical ranged hunter that controls enemy movement with persistent battlefield traps, spends Focus on precision or evasive shots, and is rewarded for exploiting targets that walk into prepared terrain. Its sustained damage comes from two ranged attacks, not from spellcasting or a third standard attack.

Class Identity

Rangers are mobile ranged specialists who control distance through marksmanship, movement, traps, and battlefield awareness. They are most effective when they can read terrain before committing, choose favorable lines of attack, and punish enemies that approach carelessly. Focus represents the Ranger’s ability to convert patience and perception into precise action.

Tradition & Outlook

The Ranger tradition also descends from Atreia’s scout disciplines, but where Assassins close with a chosen target, Rangers cultivate distance, fieldcraft, and control of the approach. Rangers are common among scouts, hunters, pathfinders, border wardens, reconnaissance troops, and independent Daevas accustomed to surviving beyond secure settlements. They tend to value preparedness, observation, mobility, and freedom of movement.

Appearance & Adventuring

Rangers usually wear mobile armor and carry bows, ammunition, traps, and field equipment suited to extended movement. Their gear often reflects the terrain in which they learned their craft. Rangers adventure to explore dangerous regions, hunt monsters or enemy operatives, guide expeditions, secure routes, protect remote communities, or prove that no battlefield is truly open ground when preparation and range are on their side.

Core Mechanics & Resources

Focus

Maximum Focus: Maximum Focus equals your PB + WIS modifier, minimum 2. A Long Rest restores all expended Focus. A Short Rest restores Focus equal to PB, up to your maximum. Focus is a tactical reserve rather than ammunition or a tax on ordinary ranged attacks; Ranger Traps normally cost no Focus.

Starting State: Focus is a rest-based reserve and does not reset merely because combat begins or ends. After a Long Rest it is normally full.

Combat Recovery: Hunter’s Rhythm and certain later Ranger/specialization features can regain Focus during genuine combat when their tactical conditions are actually satisfied.

Trapcraft: Ranger Traps do not cost Focus. They are however subject to the Learned Limits and Maximum per Level requirements.

  • Deploy one known Ranger Trap as a Bonus Action in an unoccupied space within 5 feet. A trap occupies one 5-foot space.
  • You normally deploy no more than one Ranger Trap during a turn unless a feature explicitly says otherwise.
  • Only hostile creatures trigger your traps unless you designate otherwise when deploying a trap.
  • A trap remains until triggered, dismantled, made inert because you exceed your active-trap limit, or until you finish a Long Rest.
  • Base active-trap limits and traps known is designated in the Level Progression Table. The Trapper Specialization increases this number under its explicit mechanics.
  • You can change your known traps every time you level up. Subject to Trap learning level requirements, and learn limits.
  • Trap damage is independent damage and does not double because one of your weapon attacks was a critical hit.
  • Traps spring to life the moment a hostile creature steps onto, passes through, or is shoved into an adjacent space. You must be within 30 ft of the trap for this to take effect.
  • Traps only function on the ground. Flying or hovering creatures will not trigger traps unless they land or fall directly onto them or on an adjacent 5 grid space.
  • Once on each of your own turns, when you make a ranged weapon attack against a creature that triggered one of your Ranger Traps since the end of your previous turn, you may make that attack with Advantage.

Focus Techniques and Ranger Trap Reference

Focus Techniques:

  • Precision Shot: Costs 1 Focus and can be triggered when one of your ranged weapon attack hits. Adds current Precision Shot damage. Doubles on a critical hit.
  • Pinning Shot: Costs 1 Focus and can be triggered when one of your ranged weapon attack hits. Applies the current Speed reduction. Reductions do not stack with themselves.
  • Evasive Step: Costs 1 Focus and can be triggered when one of your ranged weapon attack hits. Make the current safe movement. This is movement, not teleportation, and never allows passage through barriers or unsupported spaces.

Ranger Trap Reference

  • Snare Trap− Level 1. Target rolls DEX save. Failure would make speed become 0 until end of the target's next turn. Success only reduces 10 ft until end of the target's next turn. When you successfully hit the target on your turn that is affected by this trap, add +1 to your damage roll against the target. This applies to only 1 attack while the ffects of the trap is active. Allies do not benefit from this trap.
  • Spike Trap− Level 1. Target rolls DEX save. On a Failure, the target suffers 1d10 Piercing damage and its speed is reduced by 10 ft until start of target's next turn. On a Success, it will only suffer half the damage rounded down. Speed does not get reduced. When you successfully hit the target on your turn that is affected by this trap, add +1 to your damage roll against the target. This applies to only 1 attack while the ffects of the trap is active. Allies do not benefit from this trap.
  • Disrupting Trap− Level 1. Target rolls CON save. On a failure, the target cannot take Reactions until the start of its next turn. Success would let the target still take Reactions. When you successfully hit the target on your turn that is affected by this trap, add +1 to your damage roll against the target. This applies to only 1 attack while the effects of the trap is active. Allies do not benefit from this trap.
  • Revealing Trap− Level 1. Until start of your next turn, the target cannot benefit from Invisible, cannot become Hidden and its location is revealed to you and your allies. No extra effects apply.
  • Repulsion Trap− Level 1. Target rolls STR save. On a Failure, the target suffers 2d6 Bludgeoning damage and is pushed fixed 20 ft away from the trap. You will decide which direction to push the target to. On a Success, it will only suffer 1d6 Bludeoning damage. Forced movement is only 10 ft away from the trap at your preferred location. This may trigger Opportunity Attacks from any enemy of the target. Creatures more than one size larger than you have Advantage on the saving throw.
  • Blinding Trap− Level 9. Target rolls WIS save. On a Failure, the target is Blinded until end of target's next turn. On a Success, it is not Blinded but it has Disadvantage to its attack rolls until the start of its next turn. No extra effects apply.
  • Binding Trap− Level 9. Target rolls STR save. On a Failure, the target becomes Prone and its speed drops to 0 for the rest of the turn, preventing it from standing back up. While your attacks against the target from beyond 5 ft won't suffer disadvantage, this advantage does not extend to your allies. Nothing happens on a Success.
  • Aether Disruption Trap− Level 9. Target rolls DEX save. On a Failure, the target cannot become Invisible, teleport, or use Aetheric displacement movements. Until start of the target's next turn, target cannot do 1 Action. It can only do Bonus Actions and Reactions. Nothing happens on a Success. When you successfully hit the target on your turn that is affected by this trap, add +1 to your damage roll. This lasts until the start of target's next turn. This applies to only 1 attack while the effects of the trap is active. Allies do not benefit from this trap.

Ultimate Ability

Starfall VolleyUltimate

Ultimate Activation: Costs 100 Daeva Points (DP).

As your Action and Bonus Action, make one Ultimate ranged weapon attack against a creature within the weapon's normal range. Starfall Volley ignores Half Cover; Full Cover remains absolute. After this Ultimate resolves, you enter Ultimate Recovery during your next turn.

Primary Damage: The attack deals its normal weapon damage plus fixed additional Piercing damage.

Falling Volley. Every other hostile creature within a certain distance from the primary target makes a DEX save against your Ranger Class Save DC. Every affected creature takes fixed Piercing damage on a failure, or half that amount on a success.

Rules, Recovery and Restrictions:

Ultimate Attack Ability. Activating an Ultimate consumes both the character's Action and Bonus Action. The character cannot activate it unless both are available, but may still move and take Reactions normally. The Ultimate Ability is not an Attack action and cannot be duplicated by Extra Attack, Quick Shot, Akimbo, additional-action effects, reactions, or similar features.

Fixed Ultimate Output. Damage printed on the corresponding level as the Ultimate's own direct numerical output are not rolled; use the maximum value of those die. Normal weapon damage and damage dice from other features remain rolled normally.

Specializations

Three distinct paths, with advancement features at the established specialization levels.

DEADEYE

The Deadeye favors patience, positioning, and carefully aimed attacks over constant movement. It improves ranged accuracy, cover penetration, and finishing shots, rewarding players who prefer deliberate attacks against carefully selected targets.

TRAPPER

The Trapper controls enemy movement through an expanding network of specialized traps. It improves trap capacity and effectiveness, eventually allowing traps to interact with one another or be activated remotely to create dangerous zones across the battlefield.

WINDRUNNER

The Windrunner combines ranged attacks with continuous movement, maintaining pressure while avoiding enemy retaliation. Its abilities enhance repositioning, reward movement during combat, and allow it to remain an elusive threat while firing on the move.

Level Details

  1. Level 1: Focus, Trapcraft, Exploit Opening

    Focus

    Maximum Focus is equivalent to your Proficiency Bonus + WIS modifier, minimum 2. Long Rest restores all; Short Rest restores only a number equal to your Proficiency Bonus. You gain Precision Shot +1d6 to your damage roll, Pinning Shot -10 Speed, and Evasive Step 10 feet, each for 1 Focus under the timing rules above.

    Trapcraft

    You know 3 Ranger Traps and may maintain 2 active traps. Choose from Snare, Spike, Disrupting, Revealing, and Repulsion when selecting known traps.

    Exploit Opening

    Once on your turn, one ranged attack against a creature that triggered your trap since the end of your previous turn can be made with Advantage.

  2. Level 2: Hunter’s Awareness and Quick Reposition

    Aether Disturbance

    You sense an Invisible creature within 10 feet if it is not behind Full Cover. You know its occupied space, it cannot become Hidden from you solely because it is Invisible, and you do not suffer Disadvantage on ranged attacks against it solely for that invisibility.

    Watchful Hunter

    If you are not already proficient, add your Proficiency Bonus to a WIS (Perception) or WIS (Survival) check used to detect concealed creatures, ambushes, tracks, disturbed terrain, or hidden traps. This does not stack with proficiency.

    Quick Reposition

    Once on each of your turns after making a ranged weapon attack, move 5 feet without provoking an Opportunity Attack from the creature you attacked. This costs no action and no Focus. Quick Reposition and Evasive Step do not stack on the same attack unless a later feature explicitly allows it.

  3. Level 3: Specialization Advancement

    Choose one specialization path to view its Level 3 feature.

    DEADEYE — Patient Aim

    Once on each of your turns, if the total distance you have moved before making a ranged weapon attack is no more than half your Speed, you may empower one hit with this feature for +1d6 damage, or +1d8 if the target triggered one of your traps since the end of your previous turn. Count movement from all sources, including movement granted by Ranger features. Choose the hit to empower after it hits. This costs no Focus.

    TRAPPER — Expanded Trapcraft / Potent Traps

    Learn one additional Ranger Trap beyond the normal class total and increase your active-trap limit by 2. Once on each of your turns when a creature fails a saving throw against one of your traps, choose one Potent rider to apply to that creature: Exposed Footing gives +1 to all attack rolls against it until the end of your next turn; or Disrupted Recovery prevents it from benefiting from Dash before the start of your next turn. Durations are measured by the Trapper’s turns. Choose the rider each time this feature triggers. Reapplying Exposed Footing refreshes its duration but does not stack its +1 bonus.

    WINDRUNNER — Mobile Archer / Running Shot

    Quick Reposition increases to 10 feet. Once on each of your turns, after you have moved at least 10 feet away from the position where you started your turn, the next ranged weapon hit that turn deals +1d6 damage. Measure the distance between your turn-start position and your current position; do not add together separate movement segments. Movement granted by your Ranger features counts, but forced movement does not. This bonus damage can stack with Slipstream Shot on the same hit.

    Additional Trap

    Learn one additional Ranger Trap, bringing your base total to 4 known.

  4. Level 4: Feat / Ability Score Improvement

    Gain the universal Feat / Ability Score Improvement.

  5. Level 5: Extra Attack and Improved Focus Techniques

    Extra Attack

    When you take the Attack action, make two ranged weapon attacks instead of one. Ranger never gains a third standard Attack-action attack.

    Improved Focus Techniques

    Your Precision Shot now becomes +1d8, Pinning Shot becomes Speed reduction of 15 ft, and Evasive Step becomes 15 ft.

  6. Level 6: Specialization Decision / Advancement

    Choose one specialization path to view its Level 6 feature.

    DEADEYE — Steady Hand

    When you make a ranged weapon attack, if you have not moved at all before making that attack during the current turn, choose one benefit for that attack: ignore Half Cover; or, if the target triggered one of your traps since the end of your previous turn, the attack deals additional damage equal to your Proficiency Bonus. Movement after the attack does not affect this benefit. Ignoring Half Cover does not allow you to attack through Full Cover.

    TRAPPER — Hair-Trigger

    Once per round when a hostile creature fails a saving throw against one of your Ranger Traps, including a trap activated by Chain Reaction or Remote Trigger, you may use your Reaction to make one ranged weapon attack against that creature if it is within your weapon’s range and can be legally targeted. Hair-Trigger can activate only once per round, even if multiple creatures fail saves or multiple traps resolve during the same Chain Reaction.

    WINDRUNNER — Slipstream Shot

    Once on each of your turns, if you have moved at least 15 feet before a ranged weapon attack, one hit deals +1d6 damage and you may move 5 feet after the hit without provoking Opportunity Attacks from that target. Movement granted by your Ranger features counts, and movement may be in any direction; it does not need to be in a straight line. This 5-foot movement occurs after the hit and cannot count toward the movement requirement for the attack that triggered Slipstream Shot. It may count toward the movement requirement for a later attack on the same turn. This bonus damage stacks with Running Shot on the same hit.

  7. Level 7: Skirmisher's Escape, Additional Trap

    Skirmisher's Escape

    When a hostile creature ends its movement within 10 feet of you, use your Reaction to move up to half your Speed. This movement does not provoke an Opportunity Attack from the triggering creature, grants no attack, and does not ignore difficult terrain or barriers.

    Additional Trap

    You now learn all level 1 Traps, bringing the total to 5 known.

  8. Level 8: Feat / Ability Score Improvement

    Gain the universal Feat / Ability Score Improvement.

    You can now prepare 3 total traps in the field. Trapper Specialization increases the limit.

  9. Level 9: Hunter’s Rhythm and Advanced Trapcraft

    Hunter's Rhythm

    Once on each of your turns, you regain 1 Focus after you successfully hit a creature AND satisfy at least one tactical condition:

    • the target triggered one of your traps since the end of your previous turn;
    • you moved at least 15 ft from the position where you began the turn before hitting; or
    • the target's Speed is currently reduced by any of your Ranger feature.

    Hunter's Rhythm restores no more than 1 Focus on each of your turns, and cannot exceed Maximum.

    Advanced Trapcraft

    You may learn 1 additional trap technique. Choose between Blinding Trap, Binding Trap, or Aether Disruption Trap.

  10. Level 10: Specialization Decision / Advancement, Starfall Volley

    Choose one specialization path to view its Level 10 feature.

    DEADEYE — Kill Zone

    Once on each of your turns when you use the trap-triggered damage of Patient Aim, add one additional weapon damage die as a separate bonus damage instance. This additional damage instance ignores resistance to the weapon’s damage type, but never immunity. This resistance bypass applies only to the additional damage die, not to the attack’s other damage.

    TRAPPER — Chain Reaction

    Once on each of your turns when one of your Ranger Traps triggers, you may activate one different active Ranger Trap within 15 feet of the triggering trap against the same creature. The creature does not need to occupy or enter the second trap’s trigger space. However, you must be within 30 feet of the second trap when activating it; you do not need to remain within 30 feet while it resolves. The second trap must already be deployed and active, and resolves using its normal saving throw, damage, and effects. Hair-Trigger can trigger when the creature fails its saving throw against this second trap. A trap activated by Chain Reaction cannot trigger another Chain Reaction. Chain Reaction can be used only once on each of your turns, regardless of how many traps trigger during that turn.

    WINDRUNNER — Untouchable Momentum

    Once on each of your turns, after you have moved at least 20 feet before hitting with a ranged weapon attack, choose one benefit: move 10 feet without provoking Opportunity Attacks from the target of that attack; gain +1 AC until the start of your next turn; or restore 1 Focus, provided the restored point does not exceed your current Focus maximum. Movement granted by your Ranger features counts. The +1 AC bonus does not stack with itself; applying it again refreshes its duration. The movement granted by this feature prevents Opportunity Attacks only from the target of the qualifying attack, not from other creatures.

    Starfall Volley

    Your Ranger makes an ultimate move. The Primary Damage is a fixed amount of 100 Piercing damage. Secondary hostile creatures within 15 ft of the primary target roll a DEX save. Failure will suffer fixed damge of 40 piercing damage. Success will only suffer 20.

  11. Level 11: Focus Mastery and Exploit Weakness

    Focus Mastery

    Once on each of your turns when you spend Focus on a Ranger technique, reduce that expenditure by 1, minimum 0. Only one Focus expenditure each turn receives this reduction. A technique reduced to 0 still counts as using the technique but not as spending Focus.

    Exploit Weakness

    Whenever a creature triggers one of your Ranger Traps, it becomes Exposed to you until the end of your next turn. Once during that duration when you hit it with a ranged weapon attack, add 2d8 damage to the attack; then that exposure ends. You can deal Exploit Weakness damage only once on each of your turns.

  12. Level 12: Feat / Ability Score Improvement

    Gain the universal Feat / Ability Score Improvement.

  13. Level 13: Master Hunter

    Master Hunter

    Aether Disturbance increases to 30 feet. Once on each of your turns, when you have a reasonable indication a Hidden creature may be present or designate an area you are actively searching, use Survey the Field to make a WIS (Perception) check to locate it within 60 feet without using an action. Resolve normally against Stealth. You also gain Surefooted Pursuit: nonmagical difficult terrain no longer costs additional movement, though hazards, barriers, unsupported gaps, and magical terrain still function.

  14. Level 14: Final Specialization Decision / Advancement

    Choose one specialization path to view its Level 14 feature.

    DEADEYE — Perfect Shot

    Once on each of your turns when you make a ranged weapon attack after moving no more than half your Speed, spend 1 Focus and choose one shot before rolling the attack: Piercing Shot ignores Half Cover; Killing Shot adds 2d8 damage on a hit against a creature that has half its maximum HP or fewer when you declare the shot; you may check the target’s current HP before declaring Killing Shot; or Unerring Shot grants Advantage on the attack. Unerring Shot does not remove Disadvantage from a non-cover source; resolve Advantage and Disadvantage normally.

    TRAPPER — Master Trapper and Remote Trigger

    Your active-trap limit increases by 1 again. Once on each of your turns as a Bonus Action, remotely trigger one active trap you can perceive within 60 feet if a hostile creature currently occupies its trigger space. Remote Trigger overrides Trapcraft’s normal 30-foot distance requirement; you do not need to be within 30 feet of the trap. The creature must still occupy that trap’s trigger space. A trap activated by Remote Trigger counts as a trap triggering for features that respond when one of your traps triggers, including Chain Reaction, Exploit Opening, Exploit Weakness, and Hair-Trigger, provided each feature’s other requirements are met. You may use Remote Trigger and Chain Reaction during the same turn if each feature’s trigger and requirements are met. Resolve the remotely triggered trap normally against that creature.

    WINDRUNNER — Gale Step and Flowing Assault

    Once on each of your turns after a ranged weapon hit, you may move 10 feet without provoking Opportunity Attacks from that target. If you moved at least 30 feet during the turn before hitting with Slipstream Shot, Slipstream Shot’s bonus damage becomes 2d6 for that attack; Running Shot’s separate +1d6 bonus can still apply to the same hit. Count movement from your Ranger features toward this 30-foot requirement. Movement granted by Gale Step or Slipstream Shot after the qualifying hit cannot count toward that attack’s requirement. Gale Step and Slipstream Shot may both grant their movement after the same hit; you choose the order, and each movement retains its own restrictions.

  15. Level 15: Relentless Hunt

    Relentless Hunt

    Once on each of your turns when your ranged weapon attack or Ranger Trap reduces a hostile creature to 0 HP, choose another hostile creature you can perceive within 60 feet as your Quarry until the end of your next turn. You may immediately move up to 15 feet without provoking Opportunity Attacks from one creature. Your first ranged attack against the Quarry before the effect ends has Advantage; if it hits, regain 1 Focus. Hunter’s Rhythm and this refund can both occur in the same turn, but normal maximum Focus still applies.

    Trapcraft

    You can now prepare 4 total traps in the field. Trapper Specialization increases the limit.

    Starfall Volley

    Your Starfall Volley now deals normal weapon damage plus fixed amount of 120 Piercing damage. Every other hostile creature within 15 ft of the primary target makes a DEX save against your Ranger Class Save DC. Failure will inflict 50 Piercing damage, while a Success only inflicts 25 Piercing damage.

  16. Level 16: Feat / Ability Score Improvement

    Gain the universal Feat / Ability Score Improvement.

  17. Level 17: Apex Focus Techniques, Perfect Exploitation

    Apex Focus Techniques

    Your Precision Shot damage now becomes +2d8. Pinning Shot increases Speed Reduction to 20 ft; if this reduces the target's Speed to 0, it also cannot Dash until the start of your next turn. Evasive Step becomes 25 ft, does not provoke Opportunity Attacks during that movement, and ignores nonmagical difficult terrain.

    Perfect Exploitation

    Exploit Weakness increases from 2d8 to 3d8. You may deal Exploit Weakness damage twice on your turn if the two instances affect different Exposed creatures; each exposure is still consumed after use.

  18. Level 18: Apex Hunter

    Apex Hunter, Advanced Skirmisher's Escape

    Aether Disturbance increases to 60 feet. Survey the Field is made with Advantage. A creature you successfully locate with Survey the Field cannot become Hidden from you until the start of your next turn unless it gains Full Cover.

    Advanced Skirmisher's Escape

    Skirmisher’s Escape now lets you move up to your full Speed and ignore nonmagical difficult terrain during that Reaction movement.

  19. Level 19: Empyrean Boon

    Gain the universal Empyrean Boon available at Level 19.

  20. Level 20: Perfect Ranger

    Prepared Battlefield

    When Initiative is rolled for genuine combat, you may immediately deploy up to two known Ranger Traps in legal unoccupied spaces you can perceive within 30 feet, without using an action. You cannot place them directly under a hostile creature, and normal active-trap limits still apply.

    Perfect Focus

    When you roll Initiative, if you have fewer than 3 Focus, gain enough Focus to reach 3, up to your maximum.

    Perfect Hunter's Rhythm

    The first time on each of your turns Hunter’s Rhythm would trigger, choose one reward instead of only regaining Focus:

    • regain 1 Focus;
    • gain Advantage on your next ranged weapon attack before the end of the turn;
    • or move 10 feet without provoking Opportunity Attacks from one creature you can see.

    Starfall Volley

    Your Starfall Volley now deals normal weapon damage plus fixed amount of 140 Piercing damage. Every other hostile creature within 20 ft of the primary target makes a DEX save against your Ranger Class Save DC. Failure will inflict 60 Piercing damage, while a Success only inflicts 30 Piercing damage.

Rules & Clarifications

Trapcraft, Chain Reaction, and Remote Trigger

Normal Trapcraft Distance. A hostile creature triggers a Ranger Trap when it steps onto, passes through, or is shoved into an adjacent space, provided the Ranger is within 30 feet of that trap. This 30-foot requirement continues to apply to traps activated by Chain Reaction.

Chain Reaction. Once on each of the Ranger’s turns when one of their Ranger Traps triggers, the Ranger may activate one different active Ranger Trap within 15 feet of the triggering trap against the same creature. The creature does not need to occupy or enter the second trap’s trigger space. The Ranger must be within 30 feet of the second trap when activating it, but does not need to remain within 30 feet while it resolves. The second trap must already be deployed and active and resolves using its normal saving throw, damage, and effects. A trap activated by Chain Reaction cannot trigger another Chain Reaction. Chain Reaction can be used only once on each of the Ranger’s turns.

Remote Trigger. Remote Trigger is a specific exception to the normal 30-foot Trapcraft distance requirement. The Ranger may remotely activate one perceived active trap within 60 feet as a Bonus Action when a hostile creature occupies its trigger space. The creature must still occupy the trap’s trigger space. A trap activated by Remote Trigger counts as a trap triggering for features that respond when one of the Ranger’s traps triggers, including Chain Reaction, Exploit Opening, Exploit Weakness, and Hair-Trigger, provided each feature’s other requirements are met.

Hair-Trigger. Hair-Trigger uses the Ranger’s Reaction and can activate only once per round. This limit applies even if multiple creatures fail saving throws or multiple traps resolve during one Chain Reaction. A failed save against a trap activated by Chain Reaction or Remote Trigger can qualify.

Deadeye: Movement, Aim, and Damage

Patient Aim. Check the total distance the Ranger has moved before making the ranged weapon attack. Movement from all sources counts, including movement granted by Ranger features. The Ranger chooses one hit to empower after it hits.

Steady Hand. Check whether the Ranger has moved at all during the current turn before the particular ranged weapon attack. Movement after that attack does not invalidate the benefit. The Ranger chooses one benefit for the attack. Ignoring Half Cover does not bypass Full Cover.

Kill Zone. Kill Zone applies when the trap-triggered damage of Patient Aim applies. Its additional weapon damage die is a separate bonus damage instance. Only this additional damage instance ignores resistance; immunity still applies, and the attack’s other damage is unaffected.

Perfect Shot. Choose and declare Piercing Shot, Killing Shot, or Unerring Shot before rolling the attack. For Killing Shot, the target must have half its maximum HP or fewer when the option is declared. Unerring Shot grants Advantage but does not remove Disadvantage from a non-cover source; resolve Advantage and Disadvantage normally.

Windrunner: Movement and Attack Timing

Running Shot. Measure the distance between the Ranger’s turn-start position and current position; do not add separate movement segments together. Movement granted by Ranger features counts, but forced movement does not. The qualifying movement must occur before the attack that receives the bonus.

Slipstream Shot. Movement granted by Ranger features counts toward its movement requirement and may be in any direction; it need not be in a straight line. The 5-foot movement granted after the hit cannot qualify the attack that triggered Slipstream Shot, but may count toward the movement requirement for a later attack on the same turn.

Untouchable Momentum. Movement granted by Ranger features counts toward its 20-foot requirement and must occur before the qualifying hit. Its +1 AC benefit does not stack with itself; applying it again refreshes its duration. Its movement prevents Opportunity Attacks only from the target of the qualifying attack.

Gale Step and Flowing Assault. Count movement from Ranger features toward Flowing Assault’s 30-foot requirement if it occurs before the Slipstream Shot hit. Movement granted by Gale Step or Slipstream Shot after that hit cannot qualify the same attack. Gale Step and Slipstream Shot may both grant movement after the same hit; the Ranger chooses the order, and each movement retains its own restrictions.

How to Play Ranger

1. Your Role and Gameplay Loop

Your Role as a Ranger

The Ranger is a mobile ranged hunter who controls the battlefield through marksmanship, traps, and careful positioning. Rather than relying on spellcasting or repeated heavy attacks, the Ranger prepares the battlefield, identifies vulnerable targets, and punishes enemies that enter dangerous areas.

Your primary objectives are to:

  • Maintain a favorable distance from enemies while keeping them within your weapon's range.
  • Deploy Ranger Traps to restrict movement, disrupt enemy actions, reveal hidden threats, or deal damage.
  • Exploit enemies that trigger your traps with ranged attacks.
  • Spend Focus on techniques that improve your attacks or help you reposition safely.
  • Use your movement and awareness to avoid being surrounded or caught in melee.

A Ranger is most effective when thinking ahead. Your traps influence where enemies can safely move, while your ranged attacks punish those who ignore the danger.

Your Core Gameplay Loop

  1. Observe: Identify enemy positions, movement routes, and useful spaces for traps.
  2. Prepare: Deploy a Ranger Trap in a strategic location.
  3. Control: Allow your trap to hinder, damage, reveal, or otherwise affect an enemy.
  4. Exploit: Attack a creature that has triggered one of your traps, using Exploit Opening when eligible.
  5. Reposition: Use your movement and Ranger features to maintain a favorable distance.
  6. Adapt: Deploy another trap, change targets, or spend Focus when the situation calls for it.

You do not need to deploy a trap every turn. Sometimes maintaining your position and taking advantage of an enemy that has already triggered a trap is the better decision.

Your JobRanged damage and battlefield control.
Your ResourceFocus, used for Focus Techniques and certain specialization features.
Your PositionMaintain useful firing distance, prepare trap locations, and avoid being cornered.

2. Focus and Trap Management

Managing Focus

Focus is your tactical resource. It is used to enhance ranged attacks and provide controlled movement through your Focus Techniques.

Your maximum Focus equals your Proficiency Bonus + Wisdom modifier, with a minimum of 2.

  • A Long Rest restores all expended Focus.
  • A Short Rest restores Focus equal to your Proficiency Bonus, up to your maximum.
  • Focus does not automatically reset when combat begins or ends.
  • Ordinary Ranger Traps do not cost Focus.

Do not spend Focus simply because it is available. Consider whether the additional damage, movement, or control provided by a technique is worth its cost.

Managing Ranger Traps

Trapcraft allows you to learn and deploy Ranger Traps. Each trap occupies one 5-foot space.

You can deploy one known Ranger Trap as a Bonus Action in an unoccupied space within 5 feet. Normally, you deploy no more than one trap per turn unless a feature explicitly allows otherwise.

  • Your Active Trap Limit determines how many traps you may have deployed and active at once.
  • Your Traps Known determines how many different trap options you have learned and may choose from when deploying.
  • You can change your known traps when you gain a level, subject to the trap's learning-level requirements and your limit.
  • Only hostile creatures trigger your traps unless you designate otherwise when deploying them.
  • A trap remains until it triggers, is dismantled, becomes inert because you exceed your Active Trap Limit, or you finish a Long Rest.
  • Normal trap activation requires you to be within 30 feet of the trap.

Choosing Where to Place Traps

Avoid placing traps randomly. Think about what you want each trap to accomplish.

  • Use movement-restricting traps to interfere with an enemy's approach or escape.
  • Use damaging traps to punish enemies moving through an important space.
  • Use disruption effects against enemies that depend on Reactions or special movement.
  • Use revealing effects when dealing with hidden or invisible threats.
  • Use forced movement to alter an enemy's position when doing so benefits your party.

Remember that Ranger Traps function on the ground. Flying or hovering creatures normally do not trigger them unless they land or fall directly onto the trap or an adjacent 5-foot space.

3. Focus Techniques and Ranger Traps

Using Focus Techniques

Your Focus Techniques provide different tactical benefits. Each costs 1 Focus and is triggered when a ranged weapon attack hits.

  • Precision Shot: Adds the current Precision Shot damage to the attack.
  • Pinning Shot: Reduces the target's Speed by the amount established by your level.
  • Evasive Step: Allows you to make the current amount of safe movement without provoking an Opportunity Attack under the technique's rules.

Choose a technique based on what you need from the attack. Precision Shot improves damage. Pinning Shot makes it harder for an enemy to reposition. Evasive Step helps you maintain distance or move away from a dangerous position.

At Level 5, Improved Focus Techniques increase the effectiveness of these options. At Level 17, Apex Focus Techniques improve them further.

Understanding Your Ranger Traps

Your traps provide different forms of battlefield control. Learn what each trap does so you can choose the right option for the encounter.

Exploit Opening

Once on each of your turns, when you make a ranged weapon attack against a creature that triggered one of your Ranger Traps since the end of your previous turn, you may make that attack with Advantage.

This is one of your most important combat incentives. You are rewarded for preparing the battlefield and attacking enemies that interact with your traps.

Try to keep track of which enemies have triggered your traps and whether they remain eligible for Exploit Opening.

4. Positioning and Aerial Combat

Maintaining Distance

The Ranger depends on ranged attacks and should avoid allowing enemies to close the distance without consequence.

Your positioning should accomplish three things:

  • Keep enemies within the range of your ranged weapon.
  • Give you room to move away from approaching threats.
  • Allow you to maintain useful trap locations without becoming isolated from your allies.

Do not focus exclusively on dealing damage. Moving to a better position can be more valuable than making an attack from a dangerous location.

Using Quick Reposition

At Level 2, Quick Reposition allows you to move 5 feet after making a ranged weapon attack. This movement does not provoke an Opportunity Attack from the creature you attacked.

It costs no action and no Focus and can be used once on each of your turns.

Use it to maintain distance, move behind useful terrain, or improve your position after attacking. Remember that Quick Reposition and Evasive Step do not stack on the same attack unless a later feature explicitly allows it.

Using Your Defensive Movement

As you advance, you gain additional ways to escape dangerous positions.

  • Skirmisher's Escape: Provides Reaction movement when a hostile creature ends its movement within 10 feet.
  • Evasive Step: Allows safe movement after a ranged weapon hit.
  • Quick Reposition: Allows a short movement after an attack without provoking from the creature attacked.

These features give you several ways to respond to enemies attempting to engage you at close range.

Aerial Combat and Trap Limitations

The Ranger's ranged attacks allow you to remain an active combatant while airborne, but your traps have an important limitation: they function on the ground.

Flying or hovering enemies will not normally trigger your traps. They must land or fall directly onto the trap or an adjacent 5-foot space.

When fighting airborne enemies, do not rely on ground traps as your primary method of dealing with them. Use your ranged attacks and positioning, and look for opportunities when enemies return to the ground.

The Ranger's class features do not establish a separate altitude system. Apply the normal Wings of Atreia aerial combat rules when determining aerial positioning and attack distance.

Choosing Your Specialization

Your specialization determines how you approach combat.

Deadeye — Precision and Patience

The Deadeye favors deliberate attacks, careful positioning, and precision against vulnerable targets.

  • Patient Aim rewards controlled movement before an attack.
  • Steady Hand provides an attack benefit when you have not moved before attacking.
  • Kill Zone improves the damage of qualifying trap-assisted attacks.
  • Perfect Shot provides specialized attack options.

Choose Deadeye if you prefer carefully planned attacks and strong individual shots.

Trapper — Battlefield Control

The Trapper focuses on expanding the Ranger's trap network and making traps more dangerous.

  • Expanded Trapcraft increases trap capacity and adds a trap option.
  • Potent Traps adds additional effects when enemies fail their saving throws.
  • Hair-Trigger allows a Reaction attack against enemies that fail saves against your traps.
  • Chain Reaction allows one active trap to activate another.
  • Remote Trigger allows you to activate a perceived trap from a distance.

Choose Trapper if you enjoy controlling enemy movement and preparing dangerous areas of the battlefield.

Windrunner — Movement and Mobility

The Windrunner combines ranged attacks with continuous movement.

  • Running Shot rewards movement before a ranged weapon hit.
  • Slipstream Shot improves attacks after sufficient movement and allows additional movement after a hit.
  • Untouchable Momentum rewards sustained movement with a choice of benefits.
  • Gale Step and Flowing Assault further improve mobile attacks and repositioning.

Choose Windrunner if you prefer to remain mobile, attack while repositioning, and avoid enemy retaliation.

6. Combat Examples and Common Mistakes

Example: Preparing an Approach

  1. Observe the enemies and identify the route they are likely to take.
  2. Deploy a Snare Trap in a useful unoccupied space.
  3. Maintain a position where you can make ranged attacks.
  4. When an enemy triggers the trap, use Exploit Opening on an eligible ranged weapon attack.
  5. Reposition after attacking if doing so improves your safety.

Lesson: A trap is not just a source of damage. It can create an opportunity for your ranged attacks and influence enemy movement.

Example: An Enemy Approaches You

  1. An enemy moves toward your position.
  2. Use your existing traps to interfere with its approach if it enters a trigger space.
  3. Make ranged weapon attacks while maintaining distance.
  4. Use Quick Reposition after an attack to move away from the creature you attacked.
  5. If the enemy closes in, consider Evasive Step or Skirmisher's Escape when their requirements are met.

Lesson: Do not wait until you are surrounded before using your movement features.

Example: Several Enemies Approach Through One Route

  1. Identify a route that multiple enemies are likely to use.
  2. Deploy an appropriate trap along that route.
  3. Maintain your distance and prepare to attack enemies that trigger it.
  4. Use Exploit Opening against an eligible target.
  5. If you are a Trapper, consider whether your specialization features can create additional trap effects.

Lesson: Trap placement can influence the movement of multiple enemies, not just the first creature that approaches.

Example: Fighting a Flying Enemy

  1. Maintain a position from which you can make ranged weapon attacks.
  2. Do not expect ground traps to trigger against a creature that remains airborne.
  3. Attack while considering your available movement and the enemy's position.
  4. Watch for opportunities when the enemy lands or falls into a trap's trigger area.

Lesson: Understand the difference between your ranged combat capabilities and the ground-based limitations of Trapcraft.

Common Ranger Mistakes

  1. Deploying traps without considering enemy movement. A trap is more useful when placed where an enemy is likely to enter its trigger area.
  2. Confusing Active Trap Limit with Traps Known. One determines deployed traps; the other determines available trap options.
  3. Forgetting the normal 30-foot trap activation requirement. Remote Trigger is a later Trapper feature with its own range and requirements.
  4. Spending Focus on every attack. Save Focus for situations where a technique provides meaningful value.
  5. Ignoring Exploit Opening. Keep track of enemies that have triggered your traps.
  6. Remaining stationary for too long. Use your movement features to maintain a favorable position.
  7. Assuming traps affect airborne enemies. Ranger Traps normally function on the ground.
  8. Using the wrong trap for the situation. Consider whether you need damage, movement restriction, disruption, revealing, or forced movement.
  9. Forgetting specialization features. Your specialization adds important options to your normal Ranger gameplay.
