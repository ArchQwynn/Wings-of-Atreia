Songweaver

“Here's a note you won't forget.”

Website rules source: Wings of Atreia Player Handbook v3.10 — Publication Master, 8 September 2026. This page preserves the handbook's class material and ordering.

The Songweaver changes battlefield purpose from moment to moment. Every Songweaver spell belongs to Harmony, Guard, Dissonance, or Requiem. Successful performances create short-lived Refrains, changing Song Roles builds Resonance, and Resonance is spent to amplify, sustain, counter, or conduct the performance.

Class Identity

Songweavers shape Aether through music, using performance as destructive spellcraft, support, control, and restoration. Their strength lies in versatility: the same class can pressure an enemy, sustain an ally, or shift the emotional and tactical rhythm of an encounter. Resonance represents the accumulating harmony between performance, spellcraft, and the unfolding battle.

Tradition & Outlook

The Songweaver tradition is rooted in Atreia’s Muses and in remembrance of Siel. The wider Aion tradition describes Muses who continued their studies after the Cataclysm and eventually joined music with Aether in service to their people; their battlefield contribution led to recognition as an established Daeva class. Songweavers may therefore approach performance as remembrance, artistry, scholarship, service, or a declaration that beauty still has power in a broken world.

Appearance & Adventuring

Songweavers use Lutes and Lyres as their approved implements, often making instrument, costume, gesture, and magical display part of a single visible identity. Their appearance can range from formal performer to traveling battle-musician without changing class mechanics. They adventure to preserve stories, honor Siel, inspire allies, master Aetheric music, carry culture into war-torn regions, or use beauty and sound as weapons against forces that would reduce Atreia to silence.

Song Roles and Resonance

Song RolePrimary Function
HarmonyHealing and positive support
GuardProtection and defensive enhancement
DissonanceDebuffs and control
RequiemOffensive magical pressure

Track the most recent Song Role of a Songweaver spell that successfully affected at least one creature, whether or not that casting generated Resonance. Resonance is genuine-combat only: it begins at 0, resets to 0 at combat end, and artificial initiative cannot generate it. Maximum Resonance is 3 at Levels 1–4, 4 at 5–8, 5 at 9–12, 6 at 13–16, and 7 at 17–20. After a Songweaver spell resolves, if that casting satisfies at least one of the generation conditions below, gain 1 Resonance, subject to the normal per-turn limit. The first successful Song Role in a combat establishes role history for Contrasting Verse; it cannot contrast with a role that does not yet exist.

  • Harmonic Support: a single Songweaver spell meaningfully benefits two or more creatures.
  • Dissonant Performance: a single Songweaver spell meaningfully affects two or more hostile creatures.
  • Contrasting Verse: you successfully cast a Songweaver spell whose Song Role differs from your previous successful Song Role.
  • Each generation condition triggers at most once per casting. Normal generation is capped at 1 Resonance per turn unless a feature explicitly increases it.
  • A Refrain, Chorus Echo, Resonance rider, specialization rider, or other secondary Songweaver effect cannot recursively generate Resonance, create/trigger another Refrain, or activate another Songweaver rider unless explicit.

Level 1 Refrains

Once on each of your turns after a Songweaver spell successfully affects at least one creature, create the Refrain matching that spell’s Song Role. A Refrain is a class feature, not a spell, requires no Concentration, is centered on you unless moved, and lasts until the start of your next turn unless extended. At Level 1 you maintain one Refrain; a new one replaces the old one.

RefrainLevel 1 Trigger
HarmonyFirst ally within 15 ft that regains HP before Refrain ends gains +PB healing.
GuardFirst ally within 15 ft that takes damage reduces that damage by PB.
DissonanceFirst hostile within 15 ft making a saving throw suffers -1 to that save.
RequiemFirst hostile within 15 ft damaged by you takes +PB damage of the triggering type.

Resonance Techniques

Flowing Performance

Once on each of your turns after casting a Songweaver spell, move 5 feet without provoking Opportunity Attacks from one creature affected by that spell. If the spell affected only allies, choose one hostile within 10 feet; it does not provoke against this movement.

Harmonic Recovery

Once per round when you successfully generate Resonance, choose PB + INT temporary HP for yourself or move 5 feet without provoking from one creature you can see.

Gain spellcasting, Resonance max 3, Song Roles, one Refrain, Resonance Techniques, Flowing Performance, Performance Focus, and Harmonic Recovery. Max Spell Level 1.

Performance Focus. A suitable magical instrument functions as your Songweaver spellcasting focus. Losing your usual instrument does not permanently disable your spellcasting; another suitable magical instrument can serve as your focus. Performance Focus grants no additional attack, damage, healing, Resonance, or action beyond allowing the instrument to function as your spellcasting focus.

Empyrean Finale — Ultimate Ability

Ultimate Activation - 100 DP. As your Action and Bonus Action, make one Ultimate spell attack against a creature you can see within 120 feet. After this Ultimate attempt resolves, you enter Ultimate Recovery during your next turn.

Specialization Reassignment

Initial choice — Level 3. Choose one Specialization available to your class.

Reassignment checkpoints — Levels 6, 10, and 14. When gaining one of these levels, you may either continue your current Specialization or change to another Specialization available to your class.

If you change Specialization, remove every feature granted by the previous Specialization and replace it with the complete feature package of the newly selected Specialization through your current checkpoint. Features from different Specializations cannot be mixed, retained, or combined.

When newly entering a Specialization, make any choices required by the features you now gain. Continuing the same Specialization does not reset earlier internal choices unless an explicit feature says otherwise.

If the removed Specialization granted extra known options, selections, capacities, or Specialization-dependent maxima, remove, reselect, or recalculate them as necessary to satisfy the new path. Reassignment itself does not restore HP or class resources, and current values cannot remain above any new maximum.

Reassignment occurs only as part of gaining Level 6, Level 10, or Level 14. It cannot be performed through a rest, downtime, training, ordinary retraining, or between adventures. The Level 14 decision is the final normal Specialization choice and remains locked through Level 20 unless a later explicit rule says otherwise.

Example Performance Sequence

  1. Cast a Harmony spell that meaningfully heals two allies: Harmonic Support may generate Resonance, then create Harmony Refrain.
  2. Next turn cast a Requiem spell: if it successfully affects a hostile and differs from your previous Song Role, Contrasting Verse may generate Resonance. The Requiem spell may create or replace a Requiem Refrain subject to your active-Refrain limit.
  3. Spend Crescendo only on the specific creature whose healing/damage needs amplification. Because Crescendo is secondary, it does not recursively trigger another Refrain or generate Resonance.
  4. At Level 15, maintain three different Refrains and use Perfect Echo Weaving to shape the new one. At Level 18, Grand Symphony expands to all four roles.
  5. At Level 20, Grand Crescendo accelerates the performance but still does not grant another full spellcasting Action or allow Refrain recursion.
