How to Play Gladiator

The Gladiator is a frontline martial combatant whose engine rewards sustained engagement, Momentum management, and attack chains. Your goal is to create pressure that keeps paying off rather than treating every turn as an isolated attack.

Core Gameplay Loop

Engage → Build Momentum → Maintain your chain → Choose the right spender → Continue pressure

Your Job

Stay relevant in the melee, pressure important targets, and turn successful attacks into stronger follow-up opportunities.

Your Resource

Momentum is a combat resource. Its value comes from what it enables, not from reaching a high number for its own sake.

Your Position

Remain close enough to keep your sequence functioning, while still respecting threats that make staying engaged dangerous.

1. Understand Momentum

Momentum is best viewed as stored offensive pressure. Build it through your normal combat engine, then decide whether spending it now improves the current sequence. Do not automatically empty the resource whenever it becomes available.

A good Gladiator asks: “If I spend Momentum now, what does it let me accomplish that I could not accomplish by continuing my chain?”

2. Understand Your Chains

Your attacks are not merely separate buttons. Think about how one action creates the opportunity for the next. A strong turn preserves the useful parts of your sequence while still responding to the actual battlefield.

  • Start with the attack or feature that establishes your current sequence.
  • Continue the chain when the follow-up remains valuable.
  • Break the sequence when positioning, defense, or target priority makes it necessary.

3. What to Do on Your Turn

  1. Identify the target that matters most.
  2. Check whether you can safely remain engaged.
  3. Use the action that advances your chain and combat objective.
  4. Check your Momentum before choosing a spender.
  5. Spend when the payoff is meaningful.
  6. End the turn in a position that lets you continue pressure next round.

4. Decision Guide

  • Momentum low: prioritize actions that advance your engine rather than forcing a premature spend.
  • Momentum high: look for a meaningful spender or a stronger sequence before the resource is wasted or becomes less useful.
  • Target nearly defeated: finish efficiently if doing so removes an important threat; do not overcommit just to preserve a sequence.
  • Under heavy pressure: survival and positioning can be more valuable than preserving an offensive chain.
  • Multiple targets: do not abandon a high-value target simply because another enemy is easier to reach.

5. Positioning

Gladiators usually want dependable melee access. Avoid unnecessary movement that breaks your pressure, but never confuse “staying in melee” with “standing somewhere unsafe.” Your position should let you threaten the important target while keeping a useful path into the next round.

6. Specialization Play

Your Specialization changes what you value inside the Gladiator engine. Read its features together with this guide and identify which part of the base loop it strengthens: sustained pressure, resource use, target control, survivability, or another defined specialization role. Do not abandon the base Gladiator loop simply because your Specialization adds a new option.

7. Common Mistakes

  • Spending Momentum simply because it is available.
  • Breaking a useful chain for a low-value attack.
  • Chasing damage while ignoring target priority.
  • Remaining in danger solely to preserve a sequence.
  • Thinking only about the current turn instead of the next attack opportunity.

8. First Session

For your first session, focus on three things: stay engaged, learn when Momentum is worth spending, and recognize when continuing a chain is better than forcing a new action. You do not need to optimize every sequence immediately.

9. Advanced Play

Experienced Gladiators plan one turn ahead. Instead of asking only what produces the most damage now, ask what leaves the best combination of position, Momentum, target state, and follow-up options for the next turn.

Quick Reference

Find the important target → Engage → Build Momentum → Maintain the useful chain → Spend with purpose → Set up the next turn.
