How to Play Spiritmaster

The Spiritmaster fights through a bonded elemental spirit and a flexible spell toolkit. Your character is most effective when you treat yourself and your spirit as one tactical system.

Core Gameplay Loop

Choose the spirit plan → Position the pair → Direct pressure → Support the spirit → Exploit Spirit Bond → Adapt

Your Job

Combine your own spells with your spirit's contribution to control the encounter and apply the right kind of pressure.

Your Resource

Spirit Bond is part of your class engine. Its value depends on how well your actions and spirit's actions support one another.

Your Position

Track two positions: yours and your spirit's. Both matter.

1. Think as a Pair

Before acting, ask what your spirit is currently doing and what you need to do that it cannot. If the spirit is already applying pressure, your spell may be better spent on control or support. If the spirit cannot safely reach the target, reposition rather than wasting turns duplicating a job.

2. Choose the Right Spirit Plan

The elemental spirit is not merely a passive companion. Its contribution should influence your spell choices, positioning, and target selection. Read the current encounter, decide what role your spirit should play, and build your own actions around that plan.

3. Use Spirit Bond Deliberately

Spirit Bond should advance the current plan. Before using a bond-based effect, check your spirit's position and condition and consider whether the payoff is immediate, defensive, controlling, or setting up the next exchange.

4. What to Do on Your Turn

  1. Check your spirit's position and current state.
  2. Identify the encounter's biggest need: pressure, control, defense, or support.
  3. Choose an action that complements what your spirit is already doing.
  4. Exploit Spirit Bond when the payoff is meaningful.
  5. Reposition yourself or your spirit if the next exchange would otherwise be poor.

5. Decision Guide

  • Spirit well positioned: capitalize on its current contribution rather than moving it without reason.
  • Spirit threatened: protect or reposition it before committing to a sequence that depends on it.
  • Spirit pressure is enough: use your own action for control, support, or another complementary role.
  • Spirit Bond ready: spend it when it changes the tactical state rather than simply because it is available.
  • Battlefield shifts: reassess the pair together; do not let your own position become disconnected from your spirit.

6. Positioning

A Spiritmaster effectively manages a two-piece formation. Keep your spirit close enough to perform its role, while keeping yourself safe enough to continue directing the encounter. Avoid situations where one movement strands the other.

7. Specialization Play

Your Specialization changes how your spirit and spell toolkit are emphasized. Identify what your chosen path wants you to protect, exploit, or repeat, then choose spirit positioning and spell sequencing around that purpose.

8. Common Mistakes

  • Leaving the spirit out of position.
  • Using your spells without considering the spirit's current job.
  • Spending Spirit Bond without a meaningful tactical reason.
  • Trying to perform the same role twice while another battlefield problem is ignored.
  • Forgetting that the spirit's safety can determine whether your next turn works.

9. First Session

Build one habit: before every turn, look at your spirit first. Ask what it is doing, then decide what you should do to make that contribution better.

10. Advanced Play

Advanced Spiritmasters create deliberate two-part sequences: the spirit creates a condition, position, or pressure point and the Spiritmaster exploits it—or the Spiritmaster establishes the field and the spirit capitalizes on it.

Quick Reference

Manage the pair → Coordinate roles → Build the bond → Exploit the opening → Reposition together.
