How to Play Chanter

The Chanter is a hybrid martial support class. Its core engine rewards deliberate alternation: weapon attacks, spells, and active Mantra management contribute to Cadence while you continue to support the party.

Core Gameplay Loop

Attack → Cast → Manage Mantra → Alternate → Build Cadence → Spend

Your Job

Contribute meaningful offense while maintaining the support effects the party needs.

Your Resource

Cadence rewards varied action categories. Build it while still making good decisions, then spend it for meaningful payoff.

Your Position

Remain close enough to use your martial tools and support the party without exposing yourself unnecessarily.

1. Understand the Alternation Engine

Cadence is not just a meter to fill. The current class engine rewards alternating categories of play. When the battlefield allows it, weave weapon attacks, spells, and active Mantra management rather than repeating the same category without a reason.

The important rule is: make a good combat decision first, then look for the alternation that advances Cadence.

2. Manage Mantras

Mantras are manifested fields of spiritual Aether. When you activate or change a Mantra, you may place it at a point you can see within 30 feet of yourself. The Mantra is centered on that point and remains there; it does not follow you after placement.

The Mantra Radius measures outward from the placement point. The 30-foot placement range is separate from the Mantra Radius and does not increase when the Mantra Radius increases unless a feature explicitly says so. You do not need to be inside your own Mantra's radius to maintain it.

When multiple Mantras are maintained, each Mantra has its own placement point. Activating or replacing one Mantra places that Mantra without moving the others. Think of a Mantra like summoning a spiritual field into the battlefield: you decide where the effect manifests, then position yourself around that field.

Choose the active Mantra based on what the party currently needs and recognize when changing it can improve the next sequence. Do not change Mantras simply to force a resource pattern if the current effect is still the correct support choice.

3. Build Cadence With Purpose

Do not chase maximum Cadence at the cost of useful actions. The ideal turn both helps the party and advances your engine. Before building toward a spend, know what the eventual payoff is meant to accomplish.

4. What to Do on Your Turn

  1. Identify the party's immediate support need.
  2. Identify the offensive contribution you can make.
  3. Check which action category best serves the situation.
  4. Look for a useful alternation with your previous actions.
  5. Maintain or change your Mantra when it serves the party.
  6. When changing a Mantra, choose a visible placement point within 30 feet that best serves the battlefield.
  7. Build Cadence without sacrificing meaningful combat actions.
  8. Spend Cadence when the payoff changes the encounter.

5. Decision Guide

  • Need support: choose the Mantra or spell that answers the party's problem even if it is not the fastest Cadence route.
  • Need the Mantra elsewhere: replace or change it and place the new Mantra at a visible point within 30 feet.
  • Cadence building well: continue alternating when the resulting actions remain useful.
  • Cadence ready for a major effect: spend when the effect matters, not simply because the meter is full.
  • Same action available again: check whether another category can advance both your combat goal and Cadence.
  • Party safe: lean into meaningful martial pressure while maintaining your support engine.

6. Positioning

The Chanter's hybrid role makes positioning important. You want access to the fight without becoming the party member who needs rescue. Because Mantras remain at their placement points, the Chanter can establish a support field near allies and then reposition without dragging that field along.

7. Specialization Play

Your Specialization changes which side of the hybrid engine receives more emphasis. Identify whether your chosen path makes offense, support, resource generation, or another defined function more important, then adjust your action alternation around that goal.

8. Common Mistakes

  • Repeating one action category when another useful option is available.
  • Changing Mantras without considering the party's needs.
  • Placing a Mantra without considering where its Radius will be most useful.
  • Building Cadence while making poor combat decisions.
  • Spending Cadence on a minor advantage when a decisive moment is approaching.
  • Forgetting that offense and support are supposed to work together.

9. First Session

Learn to recognize the three action categories that feed your engine. Once that rhythm becomes comfortable, add deliberate Cadence planning and more precise Mantra timing and placement.

10. Advanced Play

Advanced Chanters plan several actions ahead. They recognize when an otherwise ordinary weapon attack, spell, or Mantra change can simultaneously solve a battlefield problem and place the Cadence engine in a better state for the next turn.

Quick Reference

Choose the party need → Place the right Mantra → Alternate useful actions → Build Cadence → Spend when it matters.
