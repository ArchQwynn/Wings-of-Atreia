How to Play Aethertech

The Aethertech is a piloted war machine. You are managing a Large Bastion, its integrated armaments, Aether Core, Heat, defenses, repairs, and battlefield position as one system.

Core Gameplay Loop

Choose system → Engage → Spend Core → Generate/manage Heat → Vent or repair → Re-engage

Your Job

Bring the Bastion's integrated systems to bear while remaining operational long enough to control the encounter.

Your Resources

Aether Core enables powerful systems. Heat is the pressure that limits how aggressively you can operate.

Your Position

The Bastion is large and system-driven. Position determines what your integrated armaments can accomplish and when you can safely vent or repair.

1. Think Like a Pilot

Do not treat every weapon or system as a separate button. First decide what the Bastion needs to accomplish: eliminate a threat, control an area, protect an ally, survive an exchange, or create an opening. Then select the integrated system that best serves that purpose.

2. Manage Aether Core

Aether Core is the fuel for your stronger system decisions. Spending it can produce a powerful turn, but the correct question is not “Can I spend Core?” It is “Does spending Core now create enough value to justify what it costs me later?”

3. Respect Heat

Heat is your operational pressure. Aggressive use of Bastion systems can create a strong immediate result while making the next turn harder. Plan venting before Heat becomes an emergency whenever possible.

Think of Heat as future action debt. A strong turn that leaves you unable to operate effectively on the next turn may be worse than a slightly weaker turn that keeps the Bastion stable.

4. What to Do on Your Turn

  1. Check Bastion position, current Heat, available Core, and operational state.
  2. Identify the highest-value enemy or objective.
  3. Select the integrated armament or system that best answers that need.
  4. Account for the Heat and resource consequence before committing.
  5. Decide whether your next turn is likely to be another attack, venting, repair, or repositioning.

5. Decision Guide

  • Core high, Heat manageable: look for a decisive system use.
  • Heat rising: stop thinking only about damage; begin planning the vent window.
  • Heat dangerous: preserve operational stability instead of forcing another aggressive sequence.
  • Systems damaged or Bastion threatened: repair or reposition if that prevents a larger future loss.
  • Enemy approaching: consider which integrated system and position will be best after the enemy closes distance.

6. Positioning

Because the Bastion is a Large combat platform, movement has strategic weight. Think ahead about firing access, enemy approach lanes, allies you need to protect, and where you can safely vent or repair.

7. Specialization Play

Your Specialization changes which Bastion systems or combat priorities receive emphasis. Learn which part of your chassis or system package your chosen path wants you to exploit, then build your Core and Heat plan around that identity.

8. Common Mistakes

  • Spending Core without accounting for Heat.
  • Waiting until Heat is an emergency before planning to vent.
  • Using a system because it is available rather than because it solves the current problem.
  • Ignoring Bastion positioning.
  • Forgetting that repairs and operational stability are part of the class loop.

9. First Session

Learn two gauges first: How much Core do I have? and How much Heat can I safely take? Once those become automatic, add system-specific optimization.

10. Advanced Play

Advanced Aethertech play is about forecasting the machine's state one or two turns ahead. The best pilots know when to spend aggressively, when to vent before it becomes urgent, and when preserving Bastion integrity is worth sacrificing immediate damage.

Quick Reference

Pilot the whole machine → Spend Core intelligently → Track Heat → Vent or repair before crisis → Keep the Bastion operational.
