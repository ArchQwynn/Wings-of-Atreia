How to Play Sorcerer

The Sorcerer is a spell specialist. You win encounters by correctly identifying the battlefield problem and selecting the spell and spell-shaping choice that solves it, not by automatically choosing the largest available effect.

Core Gameplay Loop

Read the field → Identify the problem → Choose the spell → Shape it → Resolve → Reassess

Your Job

Use spells to create the most valuable change in the battlefield: damage, control, denial, defense, or another defined effect.

Your Resource

Aether Focus supports the Sorcerer's spell-shaping engine. Spend it when the improvement matters.

Your Position

Stay safe enough to continue casting while maintaining useful lines and areas for your spells.

1. Think in Spell Functions

Before casting, name the problem you are solving. Is an enemy too dangerous? Are several enemies grouped together? Does the party need control, protection, or space? Once you know the problem, select the spell that addresses it.

2. Shape Spells With Purpose

Aether Focus should improve a meaningful outcome. Do not spend it merely because the option exists. Ask whether the shaped version creates a better target, stronger control, more useful area, or another result that materially changes the encounter.

3. What to Do on Your Turn

  1. Read enemy positions, ally positions, and immediate threats.
  2. Choose the battlefield problem that matters most.
  3. Select the spell that solves that problem.
  4. Check whether Aether Focus can improve the spell enough to justify spending it.
  5. Resolve the spell, then reassess because the battlefield has changed.

4. Decision Guide

  • One dangerous target: concentrate on solving the threat efficiently.
  • Multiple grouped targets: consider area effects and control rather than tunnel-visioning one enemy.
  • Enemy already controlled: avoid spending additional resources merely to duplicate the same advantage.
  • Aether Focus low: reserve shaping for moments where the difference is substantial.
  • Party under pressure: remember that preventing future damage can be more valuable than adding another damage roll.

5. Positioning

Your position determines which targets and areas you can influence. Avoid standing where a single enemy movement can force you out of useful range or expose you to unnecessary retaliation.

6. Specialization Play

Your Specialization changes the emphasis of your spell toolkit. Identify the role your chosen path rewards and build your spell selection around it. The strongest specialization play still begins with reading the battlefield and selecting the correct spell function.

7. Common Mistakes

  • Choosing a spell because it looks powerful instead of because it solves the problem.
  • Spending Aether Focus on a negligible improvement.
  • Ignoring enemy and ally positioning when selecting areas.
  • Using control after the threat it would solve is already irrelevant.
  • Forgetting that your next spell may matter more than maximizing the current one.

8. First Session

Learn one habit first: identify the problem before choosing the spell. Then add Aether Focus management once you can consistently recognize what each spell is trying to accomplish.

9. Advanced Play

Advanced Sorcerers think in sequences. A control spell can create the positioning for your next attack; an area effect can force enemies into a predictable route; a shaped spell can change the next two turns rather than just the current one.

Quick Reference

Read the battlefield → Solve the biggest problem → Shape only when worthwhile → Reassess → Prepare the next spell.
