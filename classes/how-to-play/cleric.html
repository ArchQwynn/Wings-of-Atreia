How to Play Cleric

The Cleric is a support specialist whose strength comes from making the right intervention at the right time. Healing is important, but effective Cleric play is about reading danger before it becomes a crisis.

Core Gameplay Loop

Observe → Prioritize → Support → Build Divine Grace → Intervene → Reassess

Your Job

Keep the party functional by preventing, repairing, and responding to the consequences that matter most.

Your Resource

Divine Grace rewards meaningful support. Build it through useful intervention and spend it when the payoff is significant.

Your Position

Stay close enough to reach the allies who need you while avoiding unnecessary exposure.

1. Do Not Heal Automatically

Missing HP is information, not always an emergency. Ask whether the ally is actually in danger, whether a condition or incoming attack is more important, and whether prevention is stronger than recovery. Your best action is the one that keeps the party out of its next crisis.

2. Build Divine Grace Through Meaningful Support

Divine Grace is generated through useful support rather than mindless repetition. Look for genuine opportunities to help the party, then spend Grace when it produces a meaningful swing in survival, recovery, or support.

3. What to Do on Your Turn

  1. Scan every ally, not just the one with the lowest HP.
  2. Identify the consequence that would be worst if left unresolved.
  3. Choose prevention, healing, support, control, or offense according to that priority.
  4. Track Divine Grace and decide whether saving it improves your next intervention.
  5. End in a position where you can respond to the party's next likely problem.

4. Decision Guide

  • Ally critically threatened: immediate survival takes priority.
  • Several allies hurt: compare the danger each faces rather than automatically healing the lowest HP.
  • Ally is safe for now: consider prevention, support, or another action that improves the next exchange.
  • Divine Grace high: identify the upcoming moment where spending it will matter most.
  • Party scattered: reposition or communicate before a crisis makes support impossible.

5. Positioning

Stay near the party's likely center of danger. You do not need to stand beside everyone at once; you need to remain close enough that the ally most likely to need you can be reached without wasting the entire turn on movement.

6. Specialization Play

Your Specialization changes which form of support, recovery, judgment, or battlefield contribution receives the most emphasis. Let that specialization shape your priorities, but keep the core Cleric question unchanged: what does the party need most right now?

7. Common Mistakes

  • Healing the lowest HP target automatically.
  • Ignoring dangerous conditions because HP looks acceptable.
  • Spending Divine Grace on a minor problem.
  • Standing too far from the party to respond.
  • Using every turn to heal when another action would prevent more damage.

8. First Session

For your first session, practice one rule: look at the whole party before choosing your action. Then learn to distinguish immediate survival from ordinary damage.

9. Advanced Play

Advanced Clerics play proactively. They recognize which ally is about to become the problem, prepare Divine Grace for that moment, and use positioning and prevention so that emergencies happen less often.

Quick Reference

Read the field → Protect what matters → Build Grace → Make the critical intervention → Prepare for the next threat.
