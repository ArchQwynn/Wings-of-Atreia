How to Play Ranger

The Ranger is a mobile ranged combatant whose effectiveness comes from distance, Focus, traps, and choosing where the fight happens. Your battlefield position is part of your damage plan.

Core Gameplay Loop

Choose position → Prepare the field → Trap → Attack → Relocate → Repeat

Your Job

Maintain ranged pressure while shaping enemy movement and exploiting vulnerable targets.

Your Resource

Focus supports your tactical rhythm. Spend it where it improves the current plan rather than treating it as a button that must be emptied.

Your Position

Distance is a resource. Preserve firing lanes, escape routes, and useful terrain.

1. Own Your Range

Start by asking where you want the enemy to fight. Establish a firing position with room to move, then use traps and movement to keep that advantage. A Ranger who lets an enemy freely close the distance gives up one of the class's biggest strengths.

2. Use Traps as Part of the Plan

Traps are not simply emergency defenses. Place them where enemy movement is likely to intersect them, where they protect your retreat, or where they create the conditions your attacks benefit from. Think about the enemy's next movement, not only its current location.

3. Manage Focus

Focus should support your current tactical sequence. Before spending it, ask what your next exchange needs. If the enemy is already controlled and your normal attack is enough, preserving Focus for a more important moment may be better.

4. What to Do on Your Turn

  1. Check your distance, firing lane, and escape route.
  2. Identify the highest-value target you can reliably affect.
  3. Consider whether a trap can shape the next exchange.
  4. Attack from the position that preserves your advantage.
  5. Move when necessary rather than waiting until the enemy is already on top of you.

5. Decision Guide

  • Enemy far away: exploit your range and prepare the field.
  • Enemy approaching: decide whether to retreat, redirect movement, or commit before losing your range advantage.
  • Enemy affected by a trap: exploit the resulting condition and your class's enhanced critical-hit interactions.
  • Focus high: identify a high-value use rather than spending it automatically.
  • Escape route blocked: relocate before the enemy forces you into a melee fight.

6. Positioning

Think in terms of three locations: where you are now, where the enemy wants to go, and where you want to be next. Good Ranger play keeps at least one useful route open.

7. Specialization Play

Your Specialization changes which part of the Ranger's ranged toolkit receives the most emphasis. Use the base loop as your foundation, then identify what your chosen path rewards—such as a particular range, trap interaction, damage pattern, or tactical role—and position accordingly.

8. Common Mistakes

  • Standing still because the current firing position is comfortable.
  • Placing traps without predicting enemy movement.
  • Waiting until melee contact before repositioning.
  • Spending Focus without considering the next exchange.
  • Choosing damage over a position that keeps the whole encounter favorable.

9. First Session

Focus on three habits: stay at a useful range, place traps with a reason, and move before the enemy reaches you. Once those habits become automatic, learn the deeper Focus and trap interactions.

10. Advanced Play

Advanced Rangers predict movement. Place traps and choose firing positions based on where enemies will be one turn from now. Your strongest turns often begin before you make the attack roll.

Quick Reference

Control distance → Shape movement → Exploit traps → Attack → Move before the enemy dictates the fight.
