How to Play Templar

The Templar is a protective frontline class. You are not simply trying to absorb damage; you are trying to make enemy pressure useful by controlling attention, protecting allies, and turning that pressure into Resolve and defensive opportunities.

Core Gameplay Loop

Identify the threat → Challenge → Take pressure → Build Resolve → Protect → Retaliate

Your Job

Keep dangerous enemies occupied while creating room for the rest of the party to do their jobs.

Your Resource

Resolve is most valuable when it is converted into meaningful protection, mitigation, or a decisive defensive response.

Your Position

Stand between threats and the allies or objectives they are trying to reach.

1. Think Like a Protector

A Templar's success is measured by what happens to the whole battlefield, not just by personal damage. Identify which enemy can hurt your allies most, then position yourself so that enemy has to deal with you or is meaningfully constrained by your presence.

2. Challenge With Purpose

Challenge is a tool for directing pressure. Before using it, ask where the enemy will fight afterward. A well-timed Challenge creates a useful defensive situation; a careless one can pull an enemy into a position that is harder for the party to manage.

3. Build and Spend Resolve

Resolve is connected to the pressure you deliberately take on as a Templar. Do not treat taking damage as failure when the rules reward you for managing that pressure. At the same time, do not seek damage that threatens your survival or leaves allies exposed.

Spend Resolve when the defensive payoff matters: protecting a critical ally, reducing a dangerous burst, surviving a key exchange, or otherwise changing the outcome of the encounter.

4. What to Do on Your Turn

  1. Identify the enemy most likely to endanger the party.
  2. Check which allies need your protection and where they are standing.
  3. Position yourself to intercept or control the threat.
  4. Use Challenge when it meaningfully directs pressure.
  5. Use defensive tools to convert pressure into advantage.
  6. Spend Resolve when the result is more important than saving the resource.

5. Decision Guide

  • Ally threatened: protection takes priority over chasing damage.
  • Resolve low: keep playing your defensive engine; do not force a resource spend.
  • Resolve high: look for the moment where spending it prevents the most dangerous consequence.
  • Enemy focused on you: make that attention useful rather than simply trading damage.
  • Enemy ignores you: reposition or use your tools to make your frontline presence matter again.

6. Positioning

Good Templar positioning creates a problem for the enemy: reaching your allies should require dealing with you. Avoid chasing a distant target if doing so opens a direct path to the party you are supposed to protect.

7. Specialization Play

Your Specialization changes which part of the defensive engine receives the most emphasis. Read its features with the base Templar loop in mind. Identify whether your chosen path asks you to emphasize protection, control, mitigation, retaliation, or another defined role, then adapt your positioning and Resolve spending accordingly.

8. Common Mistakes

  • Playing as a passive damage sponge.
  • Using Challenge without considering the resulting battlefield position.
  • Saving Resolve so long that an ally suffers an avoidable consequence.
  • Spending Resolve on minor damage while a larger threat is imminent.
  • Leaving the protected area to chase personal damage.

9. First Session

Concentrate on three habits: identify the most dangerous enemy, stand where you can protect someone, and learn when Resolve changes the outcome. You will become more efficient as you learn how the battlefield reacts to your presence.

10. Advanced Play

Advanced Templar play is about forecasting enemy behavior. Position before the enemy moves, build Resolve without compromising survival, and spend it on the attack or turn that would otherwise create the largest problem for your party.

Quick Reference

Control attention → Protect the team → Accept useful pressure → Build Resolve → Spend it at the critical moment.
