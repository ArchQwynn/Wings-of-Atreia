Beginner Guide

Never played Wings of Atreia before? Follow this short path. In about 10–15 minutes, you should understand what your character is, choose a faction and class direction, know the rules that matter first, and be ready for Session 0.

10–15 minute overviewNo campaign spoilersNo rules memorization required

1

What is Wings of Atreia?

Wings of Atreia is a heroic fantasy tabletop roleplaying game set in the shattered world of Atreia. You play a mortal who undergoes Ascension and becomes a Daeva—an ageless winged hero able to take part in conflicts that ordinary people cannot survive.

The game combines character-driven adventuring with aerial movement, class-based combat, exploration, spellcasting, political conflict, and large-scale Warfare. Characters advance from Level 1 to Level 20 in a single class through milestone advancement; multiclassing is not used.

2

Choose Your Faction

Your first major identity choice is Elyos or Asmodian. This choice establishes homeland, culture, political loyalties, visual traditions, and the society your character knows. It does not grant a racial or faction ability-score bonus, and neither faction is mechanically superior.

Elyos

Daevas of Elysea, the southern half of shattered Atreia. Elyos civilization is centered on Sanctum and shaped by the Seraphim Lords, with regions ranging from fertile Poeta to arid Eltnen and the devastated lands of Theobomos.

Explore the Elyos →

Asmodians

Daevas of Asmodae, the northern half of shattered Atreia. Asmodian civilization is centered on Pandaemonium and shaped by the Shedim Lords, with lands ranging from the severe frontiers of Altgard and Morheim to frozen Beluslan.

Explore the Asmodians →

You do not need to choose based on which side your friends pick. Wings of Atreia can place Elyos and Asmodian characters in situations where survival, shared objectives, or greater threats force cooperation despite the war between their peoples.

Find the Class That Sounds Like You

There are 11 playable classes. You do not need to understand every feature before choosing—start with the fantasy and playstyle you want, then read that class page.

Gladiator — aggressive martial pressure and Momentum.
Templar — heavy defense, protection, and control.
Aethertech — Aether-powered Bastion combat using Core and Heat.

I want precision and high damage.

Assassin — close-range precision and Rune Marks.
Ranger — ranged pressure, Focus, traps, and positioning.
Gunslinger — Pistols, Muskets, and Tempo.

I want powerful magic.

Sorcerer — destructive and controlling Aetheric spellcasting.
Spiritmaster — spirit-linked spellcasting and supernatural bonds.

I want to support the party.

Cleric — healing, protection, and judgment.
Chanter — martial support through Cadence, chants, and Mantras.

4

Learn Only the Rules You Need First

Do not try to memorize the whole ruleset. For your first session, focus on the basic d20 rule, what you can do on your turn, your class's Level 1 features, and how Flight works.

  • Core resolution: d20 + relevant modifier, plus Proficiency when applicable.
  • Your turn: Action, movement, Bonus Action when granted, and normally one Reaction per round.
  • Your class: know your main resource and Level 1 features.
  • Flight: Wings are a real gameplay system, including Combat Flight Slots.

5

Create Your Character in the Character Manager

The official character-management method is the Wings of Atreia Character Manager. Use it for character creation records, advancement, live HP and resources, equipment, inventory, spells, specialization state, Daeva Points, professions, and other tracked character information.

At creation, choose your faction and class, assign the fixed ability array 16, 14, 14, 12, 10, 8, choose your Background and skills, and establish your character's name, appearance, history, relationships, and motivation. Your class's starting equipment and other starting values should be recorded there.

6

Know Where the Campaign Begins

The default campaign takes place during the Abyss War, centuries after Atreia was shattered. Elyos and Asmodians contest the Abyss while the Balaur stand as a third great power, and the conflict reaches beyond familiar homelands toward the Balaurean frontier.

You do not need to learn every region or Lord before playing. Knowing your homeland, your faction's worldview, and the basic reason the Abyss matters is enough; the rest can be discovered naturally through play.

7

Arrive Ready for Session 0: Ascension

Your first Wings of Atreia experience normally begins with your character still unascended. During Session 0, the character undergoes Ascension, becomes a Daeva, and receives their Wings. The continuing campaign begins with that newly Ascended hero stepping into a much larger world.

Bring a clear character concept rather than a completed personal epic. Know who your character was as a mortal, what matters to them, how they feel about their faction, and why becoming a Daeva changes their life. The campaign will give you room to discover who they become afterward.

  • Class chosen
  • Character concept ready

8. The Core d20 Rule

When a meaningful outcome is uncertain, the GM may call for a test. Roll 1d20 + the relevant ability modifier. Add your Proficiency Bonus when you are proficient in the relevant skill, weapon, or saving throw, or when a rule explicitly tells you to add it. Meet or exceed the target Difficulty Class (DC) to succeed.

You do not roll simply because you describe an action. The GM calls for a roll when the result genuinely matters and is uncertain.

9. What You Track

The Character Manager is the official place to maintain your character record. During play, the information you commonly need includes:

  • Hit Points (HP)
  • Flight Slots
  • Inventory and equipment
  • Your class resource
  • Limited-use class features
  • Spell frequencies, if applicable
  • Daeva Points from Level 10 onward
  • Conditions and other active effects

10. Character Creation Checklist

  1. Choose Elyos or Asmodian.
  2. Choose one of the 11 classes.
  3. Assign 16, 14, 14, 12, 10, 8 among STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, and CHA.
  4. Choose one Background.
  5. Enter your class saving throws and your armor, shield, and weapon access in the Character Manager.
  6. Choose your class skills and two Background skills.
  7. Calculate HP, AC, Initiative, class Save DC, Speed, Passive Perception, and Passive Investigation.
  8. Enter your fixed class starting weapon or implement, armor, Common Wings, and starting Kinah in the Character Manager.
  9. Choose your name, appearance, wing appearance, personality, history, relationships, and motivation.
Specializations begin at Level 3. At Levels 6, 10, and 14, you may continue your current Specialization or replace it with another available to your class. Changing paths replaces the entire old Specialization package through that checkpoint; you never mix features from two Specializations. Level 14 is the final normal reassignment checkpoint. 11. Your Turn in Combat

Combat uses Initiative and rounds. One round represents approximately six seconds, and each participant normally takes one turn in Initiative order.

Action

Normally one per turn. Attacking, many spells, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Hide, Search, Use Object, Grapple, Shove, and other features may use it.

Movement

Move up to your available movement, subject to terrain, conditions, and flight rules.

Bonus Action

Up to one, but only when a feature actually grants something that uses a Bonus Action.

Reaction

Normally one per round, refreshing at the start of your turn unless an explicit class rule says otherwise.

There is also one free simple object interaction per turn. Wings of Atreia has no universal Action-to-Bonus-Action conversion, and unused movement or Actions are not stored for later turns.

12. Flight Is a Core Player System

Your wings are not decorative. Daevas have both exploration-flight rules and Combat Flight Slots. Flight has its own movement, altitude, gliding, falling, and aerial-combat procedures, so read the Flight page before a session where aerial movement is likely to matter.

Read Flight Rules

13. If You Are a Spellcaster

The spellcasting classes are Sorcerer, Spiritmaster, Cleric, Chanter, and Songweaver. Wings of Atreia has no spell slots and no cantrip category. Each spell is individually At-Will, Cooldown-based, Short-Rest limited, or Long-Rest limited.

Spells are class-granted abilities. They unlock through character level and, when applicable, the immediately preceding spell in a linear prerequisite chain. Spell Advancement improves eligible unlocked spells.

14. Daeva Points and Ultimate Abilities

Beginning at Level 10, you track Daeva Points from 0 to 100 and gain your class Ultimate Ability. At 100 DP, your Ultimate is ready. Ultimate Abilities do not require an attack roll. Resolve the Ultimate using its stated effects; any secondary targets make the saving throws specified by that Ultimate.

After using an Ultimate, your next turn is restricted: you cannot take an Action or Bonus Action, but you may move, change altitude, take a Reaction, and interact with an object as normal. This is not an Ultimate hit/miss procedure or a separate recovery-resolution roll.

You do not need to learn this system at Level 1. Return to it when your character reaches Level 10. Read Daeva Points & Ultimates →

16. First-Session Checklist

  • Your character is created and saved in the Character Manager, and your class page is easy to access.
  • You know your AC, maximum HP, Speed, Initiative, and main attack or spell values.
  • You know your class resource and how your Level 1 features use it.
  • Your starting weapon or implement, armor, Common Wings, and Kinah are current in the Character Manager.
  • If you cast spells, you know which starting spells you have and their frequencies.
  • You know how many Combat Flight Slots you currently have.
  • You know which Background skills you chose.
