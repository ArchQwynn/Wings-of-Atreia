Character Creation Step by Step

1. Choose Elyos or Asmodian.

2. Choose one of the 11 classes.

3. Assign 16, 14, 14, 12, 10, 8 among STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS and CHA.

4. Choose one Background.

5. Record class saving throws, armor, shield and weapon access.

6. Choose class skills and two Background skills.

7. Calculate HP, AC, Initiative, class Save DC, Speed, Passive Perception, and Passive Investigation.

8. Record starting Common equipment, Common Wings and starting Kinah.

9. Choose name, appearance, wing appearance, personality, history, relationships and motivation.

Character Sheet. The official three-page fillable Wings of Atreia Character Sheet is reproduced and explained in Appendix K. Use that guide to transfer the results of character creation onto the sheet and to see what is tracked during play.

Starting Equipment & Kinah

Every character begins with one fixed base class weapon or implement, one armor set, Common Wings, and starting Kinah. No character automatically receives potions, ammunition bundles, upgraded equipment, extra adventuring gear, or a shield unless a class feature or other explicit rule grants it.

Starting Kinah = 500 Kinah + 250 Kinah for each character level above 1. A Level 1 character starts with 500 Kinah; a Level 20 character starts with 5,250 Kinah.

Understanding Your Background

Your Class describes what your character has learned to do as an adventuring Daeva. Your Background describes the life, training, profession, upbringing, or experiences that helped shape who they were before the campaign began. Faction tells you where you belong, Background helps explain where you came from, and Class describes how you operate as an adventurer.

A Background does not determine your morality, personality, beliefs, faction loyalty, or future choices. Two characters with the same Background may have completely different lives and goals. Mechanically, use the Background table above exactly as written: choose two skills from your Background’s listed skill pool. The entries below add roleplaying context and do not grant additional rules benefits.

Soldier

What Is a Soldier?. A Soldier has meaningful experience with organized military service: troops, guards, scouts, officers, fortress personnel, or other disciplined fighting forces.

Life and Origins. Military life may have included patrols, garrison duty, campaigns, escort work, reconnaissance, fortification defense, or service under a commander. That service may have occurred before or after ascension.

Motivations and Outlook. Duty, loyalty, comradeship, ambition, patriotism, protection, regret, or the memory of fallen companions may still shape the character. A Soldier may respect military order, resent it, or be deciding what is truly worth fighting for.

Playing a Soldier. Decide what kind of service shaped you, who you served beside, and what relationship you now have with authority and your former comrades. Being a Soldier does not require obedience or a martial Class.

Scholar

What Is a Scholar?. A Scholar has devoted part of their life to seeking, preserving, interpreting, or teaching knowledge. Scholars include researchers, historians, scribes, teachers, philosophers, archivists, investigators, and field specialists.

Life and Origins. Study may have taken place under a teacher, within an archive or institution, or independently. Subjects can include history, geography, creatures, Aether, ruins, cultures, warfare, or any other serious field of inquiry.

Motivations and Outlook. A Scholar may adventure to recover lost knowledge, investigate mysteries, study ruins or creatures directly, protect important discoveries, or prevent dangerous knowledge from being misused.

Playing a Scholar. Choose the subject that fascinates you and decide whether you are an academic, a practical field researcher, or something between. Being scholarly does not require being socially awkward or confined to books.

Acolyte

What Is an Acolyte?. An Acolyte has meaningful experience with religious or spiritual service, sacred traditions, temples, ceremonies, or communities of worship.

Life and Origins. The character may have been raised around a temple, studied under a religious teacher, volunteered in a sacred institution, maintained holy places, assisted ceremonies, or found faith after a life-changing event.

Motivations and Outlook. Faith, service, protection, reform, doubt, duty, spiritual curiosity, or concern for a community may lead an Acolyte into adventure. An Acolyte can be devout, questioning, disillusioned, or seeking a new understanding.

Playing an Acolyte. Decide what teachings shaped you and what relationship you have with them now. The Background does not require the Cleric or Chanter Class and does not automatically grant divine magic.

Noble

What Is a Noble?. A Noble was raised within a household or social circle possessing wealth, prestige, distinguished ancestry, political connections, or significant influence.

Life and Origins. Noble life can involve etiquette, education, reputation, family expectations, household duties, rivalries, political arrangements, and the pressure of representing more than oneself.

Motivations and Outlook. A Noble may seek to serve a family, escape its expectations, restore a reputation, establish an independent legacy, protect a household, or prove that personal worth is greater than inherited status.

Playing a Noble. Decide whether you embrace, reject, exploit, or feel uncomfortable with your upbringing. Noble origin describes where you came from, not how you must behave.

Outlander

What Is an Outlander?. An Outlander has spent much of life beyond major population centers, among frontier settlements, wilderness regions, isolated communities, or people who travel far from established roads.

Life and Origins. Hunters, trackers, guides, scouts, wanderers, remote settlers, and similar people learn to treat terrain, weather, wildlife, supplies, and isolation as ordinary concerns.

Motivations and Outlook. An Outlander may adventure from wanderlust, duty to a remote home, curiosity about the wider world, a search for someone or something, or a desire to protect places others overlook.

Playing an Outlander. Consider where you feel most at home and how you react to crowded cities or unfamiliar institutions. An Outlander need not be antisocial; the Background describes experience, not personality.

Underworld

What Is the Underworld?. Here, Underworld means the hidden networks of crime, illicit commerce, secrecy, unofficial influence, and socially marginal activity beneath respectable society. It does not mean a supernatural realm of the dead.

Life and Origins. The character may have lived or worked among thieves, smugglers, fences, informants, lookouts, infiltrators, fixers, or others who survive through clandestine networks. They may also have entered such circles while working against them.

Motivations and Outlook. Money, survival, freedom from former associates, revenge, protection, redemption, influence, or unfinished debts may draw the character onward.

Playing an Underworld Character. Decide whether you left that life, remain connected to it, or were never truly a criminal at all. This Background does not require evil behavior or continuing criminal activity.

Artisan

What Is an Artisan?. An Artisan is a skilled maker whose life has been shaped by a practical craft. Smiths, armorers, carpenters, tailors, jewelers, cooks, builders, leatherworkers, and many other trades can fit this Background.

Life and Origins. Training may come from family, a master, an apprenticeship, a workshop, or independent practice. Artisan life emphasizes patience, precision, materials, tools, workmanship, and the value of making something well.

Motivations and Outlook. An Artisan may seek rare materials, forgotten techniques, mastery, new customers, the chance to create something memorable, or a way to rebuild a livelihood disrupted by war or adventure.

Playing an Artisan. Choose your craft and consider what workmanship catches your attention. The Background can shape how you view equipment and settlements, but it grants no Profession rank, Training Progress, reduced fee, faster training, or other mechanical Profession advantage. An Artisan may choose any Active Profession under the universal Profession rules exactly like any other character.

Merchant

What Is a Merchant?. A Merchant has substantial experience with trade, markets, supply, negotiation, or commercial relationships. Shopkeepers, brokers, traveling traders, caravaners, suppliers, and members of merchant families can all fit this Background.

Life and Origins. Trade teaches that Kinah is only part of commerce. Reputation, information, relationships, timing, supply, and trust can matter just as much as the goods being exchanged.

Motivations and Outlook. Profit, independence, family prosperity, debt, rare goods, new markets, or wealth intended for a larger purpose may send a Merchant into dangerous places.

Playing a Merchant. Decide what you traded and what you consider truly valuable: wealth, security, reputation, information, rare objects, or relationships. A Merchant can be generous or miserly, honest or deceptive, cautious or daring.

Entertainer

What Is an Entertainer?. An Entertainer has experience performing for others as a musician, singer, dancer, actor, storyteller, comedian, public speaker, or another kind of performer.

Life and Origins. Performance may have begun in taverns, public squares, a troupe, a wealthy household, a traveling company, or under a mentor. Some Entertainers knew fame; others struggled for every audience.

Motivations and Outlook. Fame, recognition, wealth, inspiration, new audiences, unforgettable stories, or the desire to preserve memories can all lead an Entertainer into adventure.

Playing an Entertainer. Choose what you performed and why. Your character may love attention or be quiet away from the stage. The Background does not require you, the player, to perform at the table.

Backgrounds Are Starting Points

Use your Background as a foundation rather than a complete biography. Ask: What did I do before the campaign? Who taught me? Who from that life still matters? Why did I leave, or why am I still connected? What did that life teach me, and what do I want now? Your answers can develop as you learn more about Atreia and your character through play.

Languages

Wings of Atreia uses three general languages: Elyos, Asmodian, and Ancient Atreian. An Elyos Daeva automatically knows Elyos; an Asmodian Daeva automatically knows Asmodian. Ancient Atreian requires an explicit source. A Daeva learns the opposing faction language only through an explicit feature, a Background or story reward, or a GM-approved narrative source. No additional general-purpose languages exist unless later explicitly added to canon.

Territorial Familiarity

Territorial Familiarity applies only while you are within territory currently controlled by your own faction. It represents practical familiarity with ordinary faction-held settlements, routes, public landmarks, infrastructure, and commonly available local information. It does not grant automatic Advantage, does not automatically reveal hidden or secret information, and creates no combat bonus.

When relevant, Territorial Familiarity can justify ordinary narrative knowledge or permission to attempt a local-knowledge check that would not be reasonable for an outsider. Examples include recognizing a commonly used route, knowing where a public service or landmark is normally found, or understanding routine faction infrastructure. The GM still decides whether a check is needed and which Ability or Skill applies. Familiarity never supplies information your faction could not reasonably possess.
