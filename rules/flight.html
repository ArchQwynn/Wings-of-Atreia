28. Flight & Aerial Combat

Altitude Layers

LayerHeight
Ground0 ft
Low30 ft
Mid60 ft
High90 ft

Voluntarily ascending or descending one altitude layer consumes all movement for that turn. A creature cannot both ascend and descend voluntarily in the same turn. Dash does not permit an additional altitude-layer change.

3D Distance

For WoA aerial targeting, total distance = the greater of horizontal distance or vertical distance. Do not use Pythagorean distance and do not add horizontal and vertical distance together.

Melee

Airborne melee uses ordinary reach: usually 5 feet, or 10 feet for a Reach weapon. Being on the same altitude layer does not remove the need to physically move into reach.

Descending Attack

If a melee attacker begins its turn higher than the target, descends at least 25 feet, and then makes its first qualifying melee attack against that lower target, the attack has Advantage.

Physical Ranged Attacks

A lower-altitude physical ranged attacker has Advantage against a higher-altitude winged airborne target because the wings are exposed. This Exposed Wings rule does not apply to wingless creatures flying through Saturated Aether. A higher-altitude ranged attacker gains no automatic Advantage merely for being above the target.

When the physical ranged attacker is above the target, increase only the weapon’s Maximum Range by half the vertical distance, rounded up. Minimum Range is unchanged. Magical attacks do not gain this extension.

A stable airborne Daeva spends 1 Flight Slot at the end of each of its turns. A grounded Daeva recovers 1 Flight Slot at the end of each grounded turn. Short Rest restores half maximum, rounded down; Long Rest restores all. When a creature runs out of Flight Slots while airborne, it immediately falls.

Aether State Interaction

  • Depleted Aether. Flight is fully restricted.
  • Stable Aether. Normal flight rules.
  • Saturated Aether. Daevas spend no combat Flight Slots, though exploration Flight Time limits still apply. Wingless creatures capable of movement may gain flight equal to normal Speed while Saturated; they do not use Flight Slots and are not subject to Exposed Wings.
  • Unstable Zone / Aether Instability. This is hazard-defined scene behavior rather than a universal Aether State. The three universal States are Stable, Depleted, and Saturated. Use the specific Aether Hazard rules for the area.

Aether-State Changes and Boundaries

Aether-State changes take effect immediately. Crossing into a new Aether State immediately applies the entered State. In Depleted Aether, Aether-supported flight is unavailable; an airborne Daeva entering a Depleted area loses flight and falls, using the normal DC 12 Dexterity Emergency Landing Reaction if eligible.

  • Saturated → Stable: Daevas resume normal Flight Slot expenditure. A creature flying only because Saturated Aether granted it flight loses that flight and falls.
  • Saturated → Depleted: affected airborne creatures immediately lose Aether-supported flight and fall.
  • Stable → Saturated: Daevas stop spending Combat Flight Slots, and eligible wingless creatures gain access to Aether-supported flight equal to normal Speed, but no creature automatically takes off.

Falling and Emergency Landing

Falling damage is 1d6 per 10 feet fallen, maximum 20d6. A falling creature normally lands Prone unless an explicit effect says otherwise.

When Flight Slot depletion causes a fall, the creature may use its Reaction for a DC 12 Dexterity save. Success halves fall damage; failure or no Reaction takes full damage. Controlled Descent changes a successful Emergency Landing to 0 fall damage according to that feat.

Falling Onto Creatures

The lower creature makes a DC 12 Dexterity save. On success it avoids the collision and the falling creature takes normal fall damage. On failure, roll normal fall damage once, split the total equally between the two creatures rounded down, and both become Prone. A falling creature two or more sizes smaller gives the lower creature Advantage; two or more sizes larger gives Disadvantage. This collision does not override the universal protection for creatures already Dying at 0 HP. An intentional body slam requires an improvised Action rather than granting a free collision attack.

Aerial Rescue

A flying rescuer with a valid path and free Reaction may attempt DC 12 Dexterity (Acrobatics) to catch a falling creature. Success stops the fall and prevents fall damage. Failure lets the fall continue. Carrying then uses the normal creature-carrying rules, prohibits voluntary ascent, and continues consuming Flight Slots.

Dropped Objects

An unsecured held object dropped or disarmed while airborne falls. It is not automatically damaged. Deliberately dropping an object onto a creature requires an Action, improvised attack, or hazard procedure. A creature with a free hand may use its Reaction and attempt DC 12 Acrobatics to catch an unattended falling object when physically plausible; the GM may adjust the DC. Secured inventory does not spill merely because the bearer falls.

Gliding

Exploration gliding requires at least 30 feet of descent, functioning open wings, and an Aether state that permits flight. It travels 3 feet horizontally per 1 foot descended, consumes neither Flight Time nor Flight Slots, always loses altitude, and has a 1-hour cooldown between glides. Master Glider improves the ratio to 4:1.
