38. Aether Environment, Interaction & Gathering

Aether Interaction

Aether Interaction uses Intelligence (Arcana) for intellectual analysis and Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) for physical manipulation. Typical DC guidance is 10, 12, 15, 18, or 20+ according to complexity and danger.

Aether Sources

Nodes, Currents, Fissures, and other environmental Aether phenomena can provide opportunities, hazards, travel effects, or Refined Aether. Their exact scene behavior is defined by the source.

Aether Hazards

Aether Hazards use the universal sequence Trigger → Save → DC → Effect → Duration. Standard severity bands are Minor DC 10–11, Moderate DC 12–14, Severe DC 15–17, and Extreme DC 18–20+. The four approved hazard families are Aether Storm, Aether Surge, Aether Eruption, and Unstable Zone. Each specific hazard defines its actual Trigger, Save, Effect, and Duration. Dangerous Aether changes are normally telegraphed in advance unless the hazard is specifically concealed. Strategic Refined Aether uses not defined in this handbook are deferred unless a future subsystem explicitly establishes them. Vehicle-related Refined Aether rules remain deferred with vehicle rules, and the current Abyss Warfront system uses no universal Refined Aether conversion for Fortress power.

39. Universal Spellcasting

Universal Model

WoA has no spell slots and no cantrip category. Each spell is individually At-Will, Cooldown-based, Short-Rest limited, or Long-Rest limited. Class resources may modify spells but are not universal casting costs.

Spell Acquisition

Spells are class-granted abilities. They cannot be bought, sold, looted, copied from spellbooks or scrolls, learned from vendors, or acquired by commerce or exploration. A spell becomes available only when the character reaches its Unlock Level and, when applicable, satisfies the immediately preceding spell in its linear prerequisite chain. Unlocking a later spell never removes the earlier spell. Newly unlocked spells begin at Spell Level 1 unless explicitly stated otherwise.

Spell Advancement — Final Rule. A class grants Spell Advancements according to its finalized progression. One Spell Advancement raises one eligible unlocked spell by 1 Spell Level, subject to that class’s Maximum Spell Level and listed prerequisites. Unspent Spell Advancements may be banked indefinitely, but banked or newly earned Spell Advancements may be spent only when the character gains a level; they cannot normally be spent during an adventure, between encounters, or during a rest. A character cannot spend more Spell Advancements than they have earned.

Spell Retraining. There is no universal spell retraining. Once a Spell Advancement is spent, it is permanent unless a specific class feature, explicit story effect, or GM-approved exceptional circumstance directly permits reassignment.

Casting Frequency and Action Economy

At-Will spells may be cast whenever action economy permits. Cooldown X spells become available at the start of the caster’s Xth turn after the casting turn. Round-based cooldowns advance only during genuine combat; artificial Initiative cannot refresh, farm, or manipulate cooldowns or class resources. Outside genuine combat, a Cooldown spell becomes available again 1 minute after use unless the spell states a longer restriction. Short-Rest and Long-Rest spells regain uses after the named rest.

Casting Time is binding. Casting multiple spells in one turn is allowed only when the character actually has the required Actions and no class or spell restriction forbids it. Wings of Atreia has no universal D&D bonus-action-spell restriction.

Spellcasting Classes

The finalized spellcasting classes are Sorcerer, Spiritmaster, Cleric, Chanter, and Songweaver. This handbook integrates the complete 106-spell library: Sorcerer 24, Spiritmaster 22, Cleric 20, Chanter 16, and Songweaver 24.

Required Implements and Components

  • Sorcerer. Must have an equipped Magical Orb or Spellbook.
  • Spiritmaster. Must have an equipped Magical Orb or Spellbook.
  • Songweaver. Must have an equipped Lute or Lyre.
  • Cleric. No mandatory casting implement.
  • Chanter. No mandatory casting implement.

Narrative speech, gestures, music, prayer, or Aetheric movements may describe casting but are not universal mechanical components. Merely being unable to speak or hear does not automatically prevent casting. There are no universal component pouches, reagent costs, gemstones, Refined Aether costs, or Kinah costs unless the particular effect explicitly says so.

Required Implement Loss. A required implement must be equipped and usable according to its hand requirement; merely carrying it is insufficient. Losing, dropping, stowing, being disarmed of, or otherwise unequipping a required implement prevents only new spells and class features that require that implement until an appropriate implement is equipped again. It does not disable the rest of the class, automatically end a spell already successfully cast, or automatically dismiss an already-summoned Spirit unless the specific spell or class feature explicitly requires continued implement use.

Casting and Conditions

A creature cannot cast if an existing Condition prevents the required Action, Bonus Action, or Reaction. Incapacitated, and therefore Stunned/Paralyzed/Unconscious when they include Incapacitated, prevents casting. Grappled, Restrained, Prone, Blinded, or Deafened do not automatically prevent casting, though individual targeting or implement requirements may make a spell impossible.

Spell Interruption

Once a spell is legally used, it resolves normally unless an explicit Reaction, feature, Condition, or effect interrupts or prevents it. Ordinary damage never creates a universal spell-interruption check.

Concentration

Concentration does not use checks or saving throws as a general mechanic. A creature can maintain only one Concentration effect at a time. Beginning another Concentration effect immediately ends the previous one. Damage, attacks, forced movement, falling, and similar events do not test Concentration. Concentration ends when the creature becomes Incapacitated, voluntarily ends it, begins another Concentration effect, or an explicit rule says the Concentration ends.

Spell Resolution Rules

Spell Attacks and Saves. Spell Attack = Proficiency Bonus + the class’s spellcasting ability modifier. Spell Save DC = 8 + Proficiency Bonus + that ability modifier. Sorcerer, Spiritmaster, and Chanter use Intelligence; Cleric uses Wisdom; Songweaver uses Charisma for spells. Class features that use another ability retain their own rule.

Effect-Removal Check DC. When Aether Dispel, a Cleric Purification Check, or another effect explicitly calls for a check against a hostile magical or removable effect’s DC, use that effect’s listed save DC, removal DC, or feature DC when one exists. If no applicable DC is listed, use DC 15. A spell that supplies its own different DC uses that value.

Successfully Affect. For hostile spell interactions, a spell Successfully Affects a creature if the creature takes damage, is hit, fails a save, or suffers a meaningful hostile Condition, penalty, forced movement, or other combat effect. Harmless targeting, immunity, or an effect that changes nothing does not count.

Area Resolution. Unless stated otherwise, each creature in an area rolls its own save once per application. A creature is affected only if its space intersects the spell area and Line of Effect exists.

Cover and Line of Effect. Half Cover applies normally to spell attacks and Dexterity saving throws. Full Cover blocks the relevant Line of Effect and therefore blocks the spell unless the spell explicitly says otherwise. Origin, range, and Cover are measured from the caster unless a class feature explicitly changes the origin.

Self-Range Projection. A Self-range spell cannot be projected from another creature or Spirit unless a rule explicitly allows it.

Critical Hits. When a spell attack critically hits, double all damage dice rolled as part of that attack, including direct spell riders. Independent later damage, zones, damage-over-time ticks, and separate trap or Spirit effects do not double unless explicitly part of the Critical Hit.

Damage-over-Time and Repeated Zones. A creature normally takes a spell’s repeated damage no more than once per turn from that casting unless the spell explicitly says otherwise. A creature cannot repeatedly enter and leave the same area during one turn to multiply a once-per-turn effect.

Suppression vs. Ending. Ending a spell removes it. Suppression pauses its mechanics while its duration continues. When suppression ends, the spell resumes if duration remains.

Stacking. Multiple copies of the same named spell on the same creature do not stack unless the spell says otherwise; use the strongest applicable numeric effect. Different named spells stack unless a class or global rule says they do not. Flat class-feature save or attack penalties do not stack unless explicitly stated.

Cooldown Terminology. The legacy term “1-Round Cooldown” means Cooldown 2: after use, the spell is unavailable on the caster’s next turn and becomes available again on the following turn. A class feature that permits an At-Will or 1-Round-Cooldown spell therefore permits an eligible, currently available Cooldown 2 spell; it does not permit Cooldown 3 unless explicitly stated.

Restoration of Uses. Short-Rest-limited spells recover after a completed Short or Long Rest. Long-Rest-limited spells recover only after a Long Rest. A rest cannot be repeatedly interrupted or manufactured solely to farm uses contrary to genuine play.

NPCs and Monsters. NPC and monster spell-like abilities may use simplified statblock recharge rules. Player spell-access and Spell Advancement rules apply only to player-class spellcasting unless a creature explicitly uses a player class.

Repeatable Healing Safeguard

At-Will or Cooldown healing used outside genuine combat can restore a target only up to half maximum HP unless an explicit exception says otherwise. This prevents artificial noncombat cycling from replacing rest and finite recovery.

Final Spellcasting Progression

Sorcerer, Spiritmaster, Cleric, and Songweaver cap at Spell Level 9: SL1 at character level 1; SL2 at levels 2–3; SL3 at 4–5; SL4 at 6–7; SL5 at 8–9; SL6 at 10–11; SL7 at 12–13; SL8 at 14–16; SL9 at 17–20. Each gains +2 Spell Advancements at every level after Level 1, for 38 total. Chanter caps at Spell Level 7: SL1 at levels 1–2; SL2 at 3–5; SL3 at 6–8; SL4 at 9–11; SL5 at 12–14; SL6 at 15–17; SL7 at 18–20, and gains +1 Spell Advancement at every level after Level 1, for 19 total. All older full-caster Spell Level 10/11 references and Chanter Spell Level 8+ references are superseded.

40. Spell Advancement & Reading Spell Entries

Spell Level is permanent mastery of an individual spell; casting frequency controls how often that spell can be used. Spell Advancements are spent only at allowed level-up timing, and linear prerequisites apply where listed. The complete individual spell entries follow immediately in Part VIII of this handbook.

Reading a Spell Entry

Each spell entry records Class, Chain, Unlock Level, linear Prerequisite, Casting Time, Range, Frequency, Duration, Concentration, Role, Base Effect, Spell-Level Scaling, Milestone Improvements, and any Rules Notes. Spell Level is permanent mastery; class resources are temporary enhancements.
