Turn Structure & Actions

Genuine Combat

Initiative, rounds, and combat-only resources are used only for genuine combat or another situation where structured turn order is necessary. Characters cannot manufacture artificial combat to farm cooldowns, resources, or timing triggers.

Initiative

Initiative = 1d20 + Dexterity modifier. Do not add PB unless an explicit feature says so. Alert adds +2 Initiative.

There is no Surprise Round and Surprise does not grant Advantage on Initiative. When one side genuinely catches another side unaware, the GM may, at discretion, allow one appropriate pre-Initiative attack roll against the target's normal defense before Initiative is rolled. This is not an extra round, is not automatic, and does not create a general Surprise action economy. After that resolution, roll Initiative normally.

Round and Turn

One combat round represents approximately six seconds in the game world. Each participant normally takes one turn in Initiative order. All turns in the round abstract actions occurring within the same six-second span; do not add each turn as another six seconds.

Action Economy

  • Action. One per turn.
  • Movement. Up to the creature’s available movement, subject to terrain and conditions.
  • Bonus Action. Up to one, but only when a feature grants something that uses a Bonus Action.
  • Reaction. Normally one per round, refreshing at the start of the creature’s turn unless an explicit class rule says otherwise.
  • Object interaction. One free simple object interaction per turn.

There is no universal Action-to-Bonus-Action or Bonus-Action-to-Action conversion. Unused movement or Actions are not stored for later turns.

Attack Action

The Attack Action makes one attack unless a feature such as Extra Attack says otherwise. Grapple and Shove are full Actions and do not replace one attack within Extra Attack.

Player Weapon Requirement. A player character must be wielding an approved class weapon to take the normal Attack Action. Unarmed Attacks and Improvised Weapons are not player combat options unless a later explicit feature creates a specific exception.

Opportunity Attacks. Opportunity Attacks remain part of the core combat rules. When a hostile creature you can perceive voluntarily leaves your melee reach, you may use your Reaction to make one melee attack against it, resolving the attack before it leaves that reach. Forced movement does not provoke Opportunity Attacks unless an explicit rule says otherwise.

Universal Actions

Dash. Gain additional movement equal to your current Speed for the turn. Difficult Terrain and other movement costs still apply. While flying, Dash adds horizontal movement but does not grant an additional altitude-layer change.

Disengage. Until the end of your turn, your movement does not provoke Opportunity Attacks.

Dodge. Until the start of your next turn, attacks against you have Disadvantage if you can perceive the attacker, and you have Advantage on Dexterity saving throws. Dodge ends if you become Incapacitated or your Speed becomes 0.

Hide. You must have meaningful concealment or another circumstance that allows hiding. Make Dexterity (Stealth) against Passive Perception or active Perception as appropriate. You cannot Hide while clearly seen. Hidden is not Invisible. Making an attack reveals your position after that attack resolves.

Search. Deliberately search using a GM-selected check, commonly Wisdom (Perception) or Intelligence (Investigation).

Use Object. Use this Action for substantial manipulation beyond the one free simple object interaction. A specific item’s activation rule controls when it states another cost.

Ready

A creature can prepare one Action for a perceivable trigger. When the trigger occurs, the creature may use its Reaction to perform the readied Action. Ready does not store ordinary movement, but Dash can be readied as the Action. Readied attacks retain all weapon properties. A readied spell must still meet implement and targeting requirements when released. Losing a mandatory implement before release prevents release. Concentration begins when the spell actually resolves. Ready never bypasses aerial restrictions, range, or other universal rules.

Simultaneous Effects

Effects resolve one at a time. The active creature orders its own simultaneous effects; the GM orders GM-controlled simultaneous effects. For opposing same-time effects, resolve the active creature first, then use Initiative if needed. Damage resolves before state changes caused by that damage. Start-of-turn and end-of-turn timing is literal. Reactions follow their trigger wording and are not inserted retroactively after the consequence has already resolved unless a rule explicitly allows it.

Movement, Positioning & Physical Interaction

Ground Movement

  • Base Walk. 30 feet for a Daeva unless modified.
  • Difficult Terrain. Costs 2 feet of movement for every 1 foot traveled.
  • Prone. Standing from Prone requires one full Action and does not consume movement. A creature can move normally afterward if movement remains available.
  • No universal Flanking. Opposite sides do not automatically grant Advantage.
  • No universal Facing. Being behind a creature does not automatically grant Advantage.
  • Occupied Spaces. A creature may move through a willing allied creature's space, treating that space as difficult terrain unless an explicit feature says otherwise. A creature cannot move through an enemy or neutral creature's occupied space unless an explicit rule permits it, and cannot end movement in another creature's occupied space unless an explicit rule allows it.

Grapple and Shove

Grapple and Shove each require a full Action. The target must be within reach and no more than one Size larger than you. Make Strength (Athletics) contested by the target’s choice of Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics). On a successful Grapple, the target becomes Grappled; it may use its Action to escape by repeating the contest. Moving a Grappled creature costs 2 feet of movement for every 1 foot moved unless the target is at least two Sizes smaller than you. On a successful Shove, choose to push the target 5 feet or knock it Prone. Grapple and Shove do not replace attacks within the Attack Action and cannot substitute for Extra Attack.

Player Weapon Requirement. Player characters do not use a universal Unarmed Attack damage rule; the normal Attack Action requires an approved class weapon unless an explicit feature says otherwise.

Disarm

Disarm is a full Action. The attacker uses Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Sleight of Hand), contested by the defender’s Strength (Athletics) or Dexterity (Acrobatics). The target must be a reachable, removable held object. Two-handed held items grant the defender +2 on the contest. On success, the item drops nearby unless a direct snatch is physically feasible and the attacker has a free hand. Disarm does not remove worn armor, Wings, integrated equipment, or normally strapped shields.

Creature Carrying and Dragging

  • Same size or one size smaller. Movement costs 2 feet per 1 foot.
  • Two sizes smaller. Normal movement.
  • More than one size larger. Normally requires helpers, a vehicle, a feature, or GM adjudication.
  • Pickup. Picking up a creature in combat normally requires an Action; setting it down in a reasonable space is free.
  • Unwilling creature. Must first be grappled or otherwise controlled.
  • Aerial carrying. Uses the same 2:1 movement rule when applicable, allows no voluntary ascent while burdened, and still consumes normal Flight Slots.

Attacks, Damage, Healing & Temporary HP

Attack Rolls

Attack roll = 1d20 + relevant ability modifier + PB if proficient.
  • Natural 1. Automatic miss on an attack roll.
  • Natural 20. Automatic hit and a critical hit.
  • Critical hit. Double only the rolled damage dice. Flat modifiers are not doubled.

Damage Types

WoA uses exactly twelve universal damage types: Bludgeoning, Piercing, Slashing, Fire, Cold, Lightning, Acid, Poison, Radiant, Necrotic, Psychic, and Force. Thunder is not a universal damage type, and Aether is not a damage type.

Resistance, Vulnerability and Reduction

Apply flat damage reduction before Resistance or Vulnerability. Resistance halves damage, rounded down. Vulnerability doubles damage. Multiple damage types are resolved separately when their defensive interactions differ.

Monster Defenses. A monster has Resistance, Immunity, or Vulnerability only when that trait is explicitly listed in its statblock. Monster rank does not grant any of these automatically. Unless Wings of Atreia explicitly overrides the term, Resistance, Immunity, and Vulnerability use their normal core meanings.

0 HP and Excess Damage

HP never goes below 0. Ordinary excess damage beyond 0 is discarded; there is no massive-damage instant-death rule. Ordinary damage received while already at 0 HP has no effect on HP or Death Saves and cannot kill unless an explicit exceptional effect says otherwise.

Healing

Healing cannot raise current HP above maximum HP. Valid healing that raises a creature at 0 HP to at least 1 HP ends the ordinary Dying/0-HP Unconscious state, clears accumulated Death Save successes and failures, and wakes the creature unless another independent effect still causes Unconsciousness.

A Stable creature at 0 HP also wakes if healed to at least 1 HP. Nonlethally defeated creatures follow the same rule. Healing does not automatically remove Unconsciousness caused by an unrelated spell, condition, Exhaustion 6, or special effect.

Temporary HP

  • Separate buffer. Temporary HP is not healing and does not increase current or maximum HP.
  • No stacking. If a creature receives a new Temporary HP amount, it keeps the higher amount unless an effect explicitly allows stacking.
  • Damage order. After defensive modifications, damage removes Temporary HP first; excess carries into current HP.
  • At 0 HP. Temporary HP does not wake, stabilize, end Dying, or substitute for actual HP.
  • Duration. Temporary HP tied to a duration disappears when that granting effect ends; otherwise it lasts until depleted or replaced.

Area Effects and Friendly Fire

An area effect affects every valid creature or object in its area unless its text excludes or selectively chooses targets. Allies and the caster are not automatically immune. Objects are affected only when the effect says so. Cover applies normally. Overlapping separate effects resolve separately. Forced movement caused by an area only occurs if the effect explicitly says so. In aerial spaces, use the normal WoA 3D-distance rule.

Targeting and Valid Targets

  • Legal wording. An effect can affect only a target permitted by its text.
  • Range. Check range when the effect is used.
  • Line of Effect. Normally required unless the effect says otherwise.
  • “Can see.” Requires both visibility and a valid unobstructed line for the effect.
  • Unseen target. Use the base unseen-attacker/target procedure. If you attack a creature you cannot see but whose location you know, the attack has Disadvantage. If its location is unknown, you must first choose the space you are attacking; the attack automatically misses if the creature is not there. A spell requiring 'a creature you can see' cannot target an unseen creature.
  • Self. “A creature” may include the user unless the text says “another creature.”
  • Objects. Cannot be targeted by creature-only effects.
  • Invalid target. An obvious invalid target caused by a rules misunderstanding does not normally waste the Action; hidden invalidity can still consume it at GM discretion.
  • Choose. Means deliberate selection; it does not imply accidental targets.

Conditions, Dying, Death & Reconstitution

Universal Conditions

Wings of Atreia uses only these fourteen universal Conditions: Blinded, Charmed, Deafened, Frightened, Grappled, Incapacitated, Invisible, Paralyzed, Petrified, Poisoned, Prone, Restrained, Stunned, and Unconscious. Hidden is not a Condition.

Condition Definitions

Blinded

A Blinded creature cannot see and automatically fails Ability Checks that require sight. Attack rolls against it have Advantage, and its attack rolls have Disadvantage. It can still move normally unless another rule limits its movement, and Blinded does not inherently cause an airborne creature to fall.

Charmed

A Charmed creature cannot willingly attack, damage, or directly target its charmer with a harmful ability or effect. The charmer has Advantage on appropriate social Ability Checks directed at the Charmed creature. Charmed is not total mind control: it does not create automatic obedience, betrayal, disclosure of secrets, or suicidal behavior.

Deafened

A Deafened creature cannot hear and automatically fails Ability Checks that require hearing. Deafened creates no generic combat penalty by itself, but the creature cannot benefit from an effect that explicitly requires it to hear the source unless that effect says otherwise.

Frightened

While the source of its fear is within Line of Sight, a Frightened creature has Disadvantage on Ability Checks and attack rolls and cannot willingly move closer to that source. Frightened imposes no general saving-throw penalty. An airborne Frightened creature may remain airborne and may move sideways or away from the source subject to normal flight rules.

Grappled

A Grappled creature’s Speed becomes 0. The condition ends if the grappler becomes Incapacitated, the target is moved outside the grappler’s reach, or an explicit effect ends the Grapple. The Grappled creature may use its Action to attempt the approved escape contest. Grappled does not inherently cause Prone or falling; airborne movement follows the normal grappling and forced-movement rules.

Incapacitated

An Incapacitated creature cannot take Actions, Bonus Actions, or Reactions and cannot maintain Concentration. Incapacitated does not itself reduce Speed to 0, cause Prone, or cause an airborne creature to fall.

Invisible

An Invisible creature cannot normally be seen without a special sense or revealing effect. Attack rolls against it have Disadvantage and its attack rolls have Advantage when the opposing creature cannot otherwise perceive it, subject to the normal unseen-attacker and unseen-target rules. Invisible is not automatically Hidden; sound, footprints, disturbances, and other evidence can still reveal its location.

Paralyzed

A Paralyzed creature is Incapacitated, its Speed is 0, and it cannot move or speak. It automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws, and attack rolls against it have Advantage. Paralyzed does not cause automatic Critical Hits. If unsupported, the creature becomes Prone; an unsupported airborne Paralyzed creature therefore falls.

Petrified

A Petrified creature and its carried nonmagical equipment are transformed into inert solid substance. The creature is Incapacitated, its Speed is 0, it is unaware of its surroundings, it automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws, and attack rolls against it have Advantage. It has Resistance to all damage and does not age or require food, water, air, or sleep while Petrified. An airborne Petrified creature becomes Prone and falls.

Poisoned

A Poisoned creature has Disadvantage on attack rolls and Ability Checks. Poisoned causes no automatic damage unless the effect that imposed it says otherwise.

Prone

A Prone creature can move only by Crawling unless it stands. Standing from Prone requires a full Action and consumes no movement. Attack rolls against the Prone creature have Advantage when the attacker is within 5 feet and Disadvantage when the attacker is farther than 5 feet. An unsupported airborne creature that becomes Prone immediately falls.

Restrained

A Restrained creature’s Speed is 0. Attack rolls against it have Advantage, its attack rolls have Disadvantage, and it has Disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws. Restrained does not automatically cause falling; an airborne Daeva hovers while it can otherwise maintain flight and has Flight Slots as required.

Stunned

A Stunned creature is Incapacitated, its Speed is 0, and it can speak only falteringly. It automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws, and attack rolls against it have Advantage. Stunned does not inherently cause Prone or falling; an airborne Daeva hovers while it can otherwise maintain flight and has Flight Slots as required.

Unconscious

An Unconscious creature is Incapacitated, its Speed is 0, and it is unaware of its surroundings. It drops unsecured actively held items, automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws, and attack rolls against it have Advantage. It becomes Prone unless physically supported; an unsupported airborne Unconscious creature therefore falls. Unconscious does not cause automatic Critical Hits. When Unconscious because it is Dying at 0 HP, the Dying rules govern damage and Death Saving Throws.

Airborne Condition Interaction

Prone is the universal condition that causes an unsupported airborne creature to fall. Unconscious leads to Prone and therefore falling. Unsupported Paralyzed creatures become Prone and fall; Petrified explicitly falls. Stunned, Restrained, Grappled, Incapacitated, Speed 0, Blinded, Charmed, Deafened, Frightened, and Poisoned do not by themselves cause an airborne creature to fall unless their specific text creates Prone or ends flight.

Dying at 0 HP

A creature reduced to 0 HP becomes Unconscious and Dying unless a nonlethal rule or explicit effect says otherwise.

Death Saves

  • Timing. Roll at the start of each turn while Dying.
  • Roll. 1d20, DC 10.
  • 10–20. One success.
  • 1–9. One failure.
  • Natural 1. Exactly one failure.
  • Natural 20. Regain 1 HP immediately, awaken, and clear the Death Save track.
  • Three successes. Become Stable.
  • Three failures. Die.
  • Exhaustion. Does not modify Death Saves.
  • Help. Cannot modify Death Saves.

Stabilizing

A creature within 5 feet may use an Action and attempt DC 10 Wisdom (Medicine). On success the Dying creature becomes Stable. Failure does not cause a Death Save failure.

A Stable creature at 0 HP normally awakens at 1 HP after 1 hour unless another rule, such as Exhaustion 6, prevents waking.

Nonlethal Defeat

Before resolving damage from a melee attack, the attacker may declare it nonlethal. If that attack reduces the target to 0 HP, the target becomes Unconscious and Stable rather than Dying. Ranged attacks, spells, traps, and other effects are lethal by default unless explicit text allows nonlethal resolution. The declaration cannot be made retroactively.

Aetheric Reconstitution

When a Daeva truly dies, the physical body disperses into Aether while worn and carried equipment remains at the death location. Ordinary clothing may reform narratively, but game-relevant gear does not travel with the Daeva.

Obelisk Attunement

  • One anchor. A living Daeva may be attuned to only one functioning Obelisk at a time.
  • Attunement time. 10 minutes.
  • Replacement. Attuning to a new Obelisk replaces the previous anchor.
  • No fee. The universal attunement procedure itself has no mandatory Kinah cost.
  • Dead Daeva. A dead Unanchored Daeva cannot newly attune to a different Obelisk.

Reconstitution Procedure

  1. The Daeva dies and disperses into Aether; equipment remains behind.
  2. If the attuned Obelisk is functioning, the Daeva reconstitutes there 1 hour later.
  3. The Daeva returns at 1 HP and 0 Flight Slots. Reconstitution is not a rest and does not restore class resources or other spent uses.
  4. If no functioning attuned Obelisk exists, the Daeva becomes Unanchored for up to 24 hours.
  5. If the original attuned anchor is restored within the 24-hour window, the 1-hour reconstitution timer begins once it functions again.
  6. If the 24-hour Unanchored period expires without restoration, the Daeva suffers Permanent Death unless an explicit exceptional story effect says otherwise.

An Obelisk with at least 1 HP is fully functional. At 0 HP it is destroyed/nonfunctional. Damaged Obelisks can be repaired by the players after spending Kinah cost equivalent to half its price. Rest, Hit Dice, Exhaustion & Survival

Hit Dice and HP

A character has Hit Dice equal to character level. Fixed HP gained per level is based on class Hit Die: d6 = 4, d8 = 5, d10 = 6, d12 = 7, plus Constitution modifier and any class-specific HP modifier. At level 1, use the maximum Hit Die value plus Constitution modifier and the class modifier.

Known chassis exceptions include Templar d12 with +4 HP per level, Cleric d8 with +2 HP per level, Aethertech d8 with Bastion HP handled separately, Songweaver d6, and Gladiator d12.

Permanent Constitution Changes. If a character's Constitution modifier permanently changes, recalculate maximum HP retroactively for every character level, including level 1, using the new Constitution modifier. Temporary Constitution changes do not recalculate maximum HP unless the effect explicitly says otherwise.

Short Rest

A Short Rest lasts 1 uninterrupted hour. During the rest, you may spend any available Hit Dice one at a time. Each die heals your class Hit Die + your Constitution modifier, with a minimum of 1 HP restored per die. After seeing the result of each die, choose whether to spend another. Hit Dice are normally spent only during a Short Rest unless an explicit rule says otherwise. A Short Rest also restores half maximum Flight Slots, rounded down, subject to explicit features.

Long Rest

A Long Rest lasts 8 hours and normally restores maximum HP, all spent Hit Dice, all Flight Slots, and Long-Rest resources. A creature gains the benefits of only one Long Rest per 24-hour period.

Safe and Dangerous Resting

Rest security is based on the actual location, not faction ownership. Safe locations allow ordinary rest. Dangerous locations still permit resting, but the GM may interrupt it when the fiction warrants. A meaningful interruption breaks a Short Rest. Combat or strenuous activity during a Long Rest does not count toward the required rest time, and a major interruption may require restarting. Watch duty can involve light activity and normal perception without a universal watch formula. Aether state by itself does not prohibit rest.

Exhaustion

  • Levels. 0–6.
  • Levels 1–5. Subtract the current Exhaustion level from all d20 Tests.
  • Levels 4–5. All movement speeds are reduced by 10 feet, minimum 5 feet.
  • Level 6. Unconscious and makes Death Saves under the Exhaustion-collapse procedure; it is not automatic death.
  • Alternative recovery. A creature at Exhaustion 6 may forgo Death Saves and instead complete a 24-hour full rest to awaken with 3 levels of Exhaustion removed.
  • Long Rest. Removes 1 Exhaustion under the established universal recovery rule.

Exhaustion 6 — Collapse Procedure

At Exhaustion 6 you become Unconscious and begin making Death Saving Throws; Exhaustion itself does not reduce your HP to 0. Roll 1d20 at the start of each turn with no modifiers: 10–20 is one success, 1–9 is one failure, a natural 1 is exactly one failure, and a natural 20 restores 1 HP and clears the Death Save track in the normal Dying state. Exhaustion penalties do not modify these saves.

Three successes make you Stable, but you remain Unconscious while still at Exhaustion 6. Three failures cause death and the normal Daeva reconstitution procedure if applicable. Instead of making Death Saves, you may forgo them and complete an uninterrupted 24-hour full rest; if completed, you awaken at Exhaustion 3.

Suffocation and Drowning

Hold Breath = 1 minute + Constitution modifier minutes, minimum 30 seconds.

When the held-breath duration expires, gain 1 Exhaustion at the end of each turn while unable to breathe. An Unconscious creature cannot intentionally hold its breath and begins suffocating if it cannot breathe. Once Exhaustion reaches 6 and the creature remains unable to breathe, apply the established Exhaustion-6 Death Save procedure. Returning to breathable air stops new suffocation Exhaustion but does not remove existing levels.

Underwater Combat. Wings of Atreia has no separate universal underwater-combat subsystem. Underwater scenes use the ordinary combat rules; the universal underwater concern is breathing and suffocation unless a specific environment, creature, weapon, spell, or feature states otherwise.

Extreme Heat and Cold

During meaningfully dangerous exposure, make a Constitution save roughly every 2 hours. Recommended DCs are 12 for severe conditions, 15 for dangerous conditions, and 18+ for exceptional conditions. Failure causes 1 Exhaustion. Adequate protection can remove the need for a check.

Dehydration and Starvation

After 24 hours without adequate water, make DC 12 Constitution; increase the DC by 3 for each subsequent 24-hour period. Failure causes 1 Exhaustion. After 3 days without adequate food, make DC 10 Constitution every additional 24 hours, increasing the DC by 5 each time; failure causes 1 Exhaustion. Use these rules only when deprivation is a meaningful threat.
