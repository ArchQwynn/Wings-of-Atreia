Weapons & Ranged Combat

Access Is Not Ownership. Class weapon, shield, armor, and implement access identifies what your class is allowed to use; it does not mean you own every authorized item. Starting equipment determines what you actually begin play with. Equipment-dependent features function only while their stated requirement is satisfied and do not grant the required equipment unless they explicitly say so.

Straight-Line Fire

Crossbows, Pistols, and Muskets use Straight-Line. If an allied creature intersects the line between shooter and target, the nearest such ally makes DC 15 Dexterity. On success, the shot proceeds. On failure, the ally is hit and takes normal damage; the original target is not hit. The intercepted hit cannot inherit a Natural-20 critical result rolled against the original target. Resolve multiple possible interceptors nearest first. The same rule applies in 3D aerial lines.

Ammunition

All fired ammunition is lost. Fired Arrows, Bolts, Pistol Bullets, and Musket Bullets are never recovered.

Implements

Magical Orbs, Spellbooks, Lutes, and Lyres are class implements, not damaging weapons. Implement Type Properties. Implement type defines playstyle separately from quality bonuses.

Magical Orb — Power Channel. Once on each of your turns when a class spell deals rolled damage, add +1 damage to one damage resolution.

Spellbook — Calculated Casting. Once on each of your turns when one of your class spells affects a hostile creature without dealing damage to it, increase one forced-movement distance or Speed reduction caused by that spell by 5 feet.

Lute — Harmonic Accompaniment. Once on each of your turns, when a Harmony or Guard Songweaver spell restores HP, grants temporary HP, or reduces damage, increase one such amount by +1.

Lyre — Resonant Edge. Once on each of your turns, when a Dissonance or Requiem Songweaver spell deals rolled damage, add +1 damage to one damage resolution.

  • Orb. One hand.
  • Spellbook. One hand while readied.
  • Lyre. One hand.
  • Lute. Two hands while actively used.

Hands and Equipped Items

  • Hands. A normal Daeva has two usable hands.
  • One-handed item. Occupies one hand when actively held.
  • Two-handed item. Requires both hands while actively used; it may be carried in one hand when not being used.
  • Versatile. Switching grip is free if the second hand is already free; otherwise an occupied object must first be stowed or dropped.
  • Shield. Occupies one hand and must be removed before actively using another two-handed item.
  • Free hand. Required for pickup, catching, snatching, and mechanisms when the fiction demands it.
  • Unconscious. Drops unsecured actively held items. Worn, strapped, sheathed, or stored items remain secured.
  • Prone. Does not by itself cause held items to drop.

Changing Equipment

Drawing, readying, stowing, or switching one weapon or implement uses the turn’s free object interaction. Dropping an item is free. Picking one up uses the interaction. Another substantial object manipulation normally requires the Use Object Action. Equipping or removing a shield requires a full Action.

Shields have no Profession +1/+2 stages. Armorcrafting never upgrades a Shield. Shield base AC, hand use, class access, and other Shield-specific rules remain unchanged; no universal Shield Damage Reduction is added.

Acquisition

Weapons, armor, shields, and implements are not normally sold by ordinary merchants. They enter play through starting equipment, faction issue, exploration, battlefield loot, caches, armories, relic sites, defeated enemies who actually possess them, quest rewards, and major adventures. Profession equipment work upgrades an already-obtained eligible item and never crafts protected equipment from scratch. Aether equipment does not randomly appear on generic enemies, but an NPC who actually owns an Aether item can drop it. Legendary equipment is never generic stock.

Aethermaster Enhancement

Aether enhancement is unavailable before character Level 10. Weapons, Armor, and Implements must already be Profession +2 before an Aethermaster may attempt Aether +1 or Aether +2. Shields and Wings require Level 10+ but have no Profession prerequisite. Professions cannot perform Aether enhancement. Aether +1 costs 200 Refined Aether and Aether +2 costs 500. Aether +2 remains a direct full-cost choice from an eligible base; there is no reduced-cost +1-to-+2 step. Make two separate flat d20 rolls, both DC 10, with no modifiers unless an explicit rule says otherwise. Roll 1 determines Completion Time: success = 1 hour, failure = 2 hours. After that time, Roll 2 determines the Enhancement Result. On failure, committed Refined Aether is consumed. There is no universal fixed Kinah service fee; any fee charged is spent once the service begins and is not refunded on failure.

Identification and Attunement

Common and Aether equipment are automatically identified. Legendary items may have story-hidden properties. There is no universal magic-item attunement limit. A Legendary item may define its own bonding or attunement requirement.

Equipment Damage and Repair

Ordinary combat does not damage gear. Equipment is damaged only by explicit effects or catastrophic circumstances. A Damaged item provides none of its normal mechanical benefits until repaired. A Destroyed item is unusable and cannot be restored by an ordinary repair.

Repairing a Damaged weapon, armor, shield, or other repairable equipment uses an appropriate specialist and any GM-determined Kinah/resource cost. The player makes two separate flat d20 rolls, both DC 10, with no modifiers unless an explicit rule says otherwise. Roll 1 determines Completion Time: success = 1 hour, failure = 2 hours. After that time has elapsed, Roll 2 determines the Repair Result: success repairs the item; failure means the repair fails. Any Kinah or resources already committed are not refunded on failure. A physically repaired Aether item retains its quality and does not require paying its enhancement cost again unless the enhancement itself was specifically disrupted. Destroyed items still cannot be restored by an ordinary repair.

Wings as Equipment

Every Daeva begins with functional Common Wings. Looted ordinary Wings are normally cosmetic appearance changes only and do not increase flight statistics. Aether +1 and Aether +2 Wings are Level 10+ only and require an Aethermaster.

External Storage

External storage is location-based and never increases personal inventory limits. Items stored externally do not count against personal slots. There is no universal external-storage slot formula; the GM judges reasonable capacity.

  • Physical access. Stored items can be used only when physically accessible; no magical shared or remote inventory exists unless an explicit effect says so.
  • Retrieval. Uses ordinary physical access and object-interaction timing.
  • Ownership. Party storage is governed by player agreement, not point shares.
  • Security. Genuinely secure player-controlled storage is normally assumed safe unless the story establishes a reason otherwise.
  • Bulk logistics. Moving large quantities uses labor, repeated trips, faction help, and story logistics rather than weight formulas.
  • Oversized objects. Handled narratively rather than assigned arbitrary slot counts.
  • Temporary storage. Story-specific temporary storage is allowed.
  • Campaign restrictions. Access can be blocked by events without deleting the stored items unless the story establishes loss, destruction, or confiscation.
