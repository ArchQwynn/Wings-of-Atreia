PLAYER-CREATED LEGIONS

A player-created Legion is a lightweight organization for shared identity, missions, resources, and cross-faction cooperation when the campaign permits it. Legion membership does not create a new character progression track or grant automatic combat bonuses.

When a Legion Can Be Founded

  • Campaign Gate. The Campaign Director or controlling GM must explicitly declare Legion Founding available. Reaching Level 11 by itself does not unlock the system.
  • Founding Player. One Founding Player must be Level 11 or higher. That character becomes the initial Legion Leader.
  • Other members. Every other founding or joining member must be Level 3 or higher before joining and must already have reached their Specialization or Archetype choice where applicable. A Level 1-2 character cannot join early.
  • Membership has no maximum size. There is no minimum number of additional founders; a qualifying Founding Player may establish the Legion alone if all other founding requirements are satisfied.
  • Elyos and Asmodians may belong to the same Legion once the required campaign state is active. Every member retains their original faction identity and its normal consequences.

Founding Fund and Treasury

  • Founding requires 5,000 Kinah deposited into the new Legion Treasury. Eligible founding members may contribute toward the total.
  • Before establishment, the Founding Fund cannot be withdrawn. If founding is abandoned, each contributor receives their contribution back. Once established, the 5,000 Kinah remains usable Treasury money.
  • Any Legion member may deposit Kinah. Only the Legion Leader may withdraw Kinah or authorize spending.
  • Legion missions may reward participating members, the Treasury, or both as determined by the GM.
  • There is no passive Legion income, automatic salary, interest, business-profit engine, or mandatory recurring membership dues.

Leadership and Membership

  • The only default roles are Legion Leader and Legion Member.
  • The Legion Leader admits and removes members, controls permissions for Legion assets, may transfer leadership, and may formally dissolve the Legion.
  • Leadership may be transferred to any existing legal Legion member. The new Leader does not need to be Level 11; Level 11 is a founding requirement only.

Leadership Succession, Dissolution, and Removed Members

  • Leadership succession: If the Legion Leader becomes permanently unavailable or leaves the campaign without transferring leadership, the remaining members may nominate a successor. The GM confirms the succession. The successor need not meet the Level 11 founding requirement.
  • Dissolution and shared assets: When a Legion dissolves, its Treasury and shared Storage contents do not automatically duplicate or return to contributors. The Legion Leader proposes a distribution of remaining assets, subject to GM approval and member agreements. The GM resolves disputes and assets that remain undistributed.
  • Removed members: Removing a member does not automatically transfer ownership of items that member deposited into Legion Storage. The Leader and GM resolve disputed ownership based on the circumstances and any recorded agreement made when the item was deposited.

Member Benefits

  • Members may display the Legion name, colors, crest, and insignia. Ordinary Legion identification occupies no Inventory Slot.
  • Members may accept Legion missions and may use resources, facilities, contacts, and support the Legion actually possesses when the Legion Leader permits it.
  • Where the campaign's cross-faction arrangements recognize the Legion, its insignia provides a legitimate reason for Elyos and Asmodian members to operate together. It does not grant diplomatic immunity or unrestricted access.
  • Legion membership grants no automatic Hit, AC, damage, HP, saving throw, skill, Advantage, Spell DC, DP, class-resource, equipment-quality, or personal Inventory bonus.

Legion Storage and Headquarters

  • An established Legion has one secure shared Storage location with a capacity of 100 Inventory Slots. Normal Inventory Slot and stacking rules apply; Kinah remains in the Treasury and uses no Storage Slots.
  • Any Legion member may deposit eligible items. Only the Legion Leader may withdraw items or authorize another member to retrieve them.
  • Legion Storage is stationary and cannot be accessed remotely. It never increases a member's personal Inventory capacity in the field.
  • A Legion may designate one legitimate location as its Headquarters. The Headquarters is simply its meeting place and Storage location; it grants no automatic bonuses and has no upgrade track.

Lightweight Rule. There is no Legion XP, Legion Level, officer hierarchy, skill tree, research tree, reputation meter, headquarters progression, or other default Legion management subsystem.

Abyss Warfront System

Warfronts resolve formation-scale warfare separately from ordinary character-scale combat. They are used for Fortress Wars and Open Abyss Battles. Strategic travel and marching between major engagements are handled narratively, off-table, or during downtime.

Shared Warfront Command

The player side uses one shared strategic board. Players collectively decide how to delegate Battlefronts or grids and who rolls the relevant Warfront dice. A player's PC location and that player's responsibility for rolling a different Battlefront are separate.

Universal Synchronized Warfront Cycle

  1. Simultaneous Movement Phase. Both sides complete legal strategic movement in the same Movement Phase. Where opposing adjacent forces could advance directly into one another, the player side has advance-declaration priority.
  2. Battle Phase. Identify every engagement created by that movement. Each active engagement resolves completely through its Preliminary Round, Main Combat, and Decision Windows.
  3. No Early New Movement. A Battlefront or grid that resolves early cannot begin a new normal strategic move while another engagement from that Battle Phase is unresolved.
  4. End-of-Battle-Phase Resolution. After all engagements conclude, resolve applicable control changes, recovery for eligible withdrawn Units, eliminations, retreats, and any External Reinforcement Check that is due. Then begin the next Movement Phase.

Warfront Round Limit. A Warfront does not have a maximum round limit. Victory conditions apply depending on the type of Warfront session (e.g. Fortress Capture or Hold Out for Fortress War, and total elimination of enemy's units present in the battlefield for Open Abyss Battles). If one side chooses to surrender or give up, the Warfront ends immediately. The GM may choose if battle is won or lost depending on objectives.

No Zone of Control. Enemy adjacency by itself never locks movement. An engagement begins only when opposing forces legally enter or share the same Battlefront or grid.

War Assets (WA)

War Assets represent personnel, equipment, authorization, logistics, transportation, Aether resources, supplies, and the sustained resources needed to field formations.

  • The GM declares a separate Base WA allowance for each participating side before force selection and deployment.
  • The GM may restrict available Unit Catalogue options, Tiers, or Specialist formations for a specific operation.
  • There is no recurring Warfront Upkeep.
  • Unused operation-specific Base WA and Supplemental WA may be banked for use in a later warfare operation.
  • Any Warfront Units that survive the operation are banked and remain available for a later warfare mission. The players are responsible for listing their banked surviving Units and identifying which of those Units they will bring to their next warfare mission.
  • Banked WA and surviving Units are not automatically transferred between sides or separate player forces.
  • Only the player-controlled side may buy Supplemental WA with Kinah.

Preliminary Round, Main Combat, Battle Exchanges, and Decision Windows

After an engagement is identified, resolve the Preliminary Round before any normal Battle Exchange. Both sides resolve their applicable Preliminary actions, then all Preliminary effects are applied and Units reduced to 0 END are removed before Main Combat begins.

Preliminary Round

Artillery. Every Artillery Unit makes one Preliminary Attack. Roll 1d6; only a result of 1 is a successful Hit. A successful Preliminary Artillery Hit deals 1 Hit to an eligible enemy Infantry or Ranged/Caster Unit. The owner of the targeted force chooses which eligible Unit suffers the Hit. Artillery cannot target Specialists, Aerial Units, other Artillery, or Support Units. This special roll does not use Artillery's normal ATK 0.

Siege. Every Siege Unit makes one Preliminary Attack. Roll 1d6; only a result of 1 is a successful SIEGE Hit, removing 1 Fortification Point. This Preliminary threshold of 1 is separate from the Siege Unit's normal SIEGE statistic, which is used during Main Combat.

Scout. During the Preliminary Round of a Fortress War, expend the Scout to reveal the total number of enemy Warfront Units in the next Battlefront. Scouts provide no additional information in Open Abyss Battles because all GM-side Unit tokens are visible on the board. A Scout cannot be used after the Preliminary Round.

Assassin. During the Preliminary Round, expend one Assassin to target one eligible enemy Warfront Unit and reduce that Unit's current END by 2. An Assassin cannot target Artillery, Siege Units, PCs, or the Brigadier-General. Each Assassin can target only one enemy Unit, and multiple Assassins cannot gang up on the same target. Any END reduction beyond the target's remaining END is lost.

Preliminary actions are resolved before Main Combat. All Hits, END reduction, eliminations, and other applicable effects are resolved before Main Combat begins. A Unit eliminated during the Preliminary Round does not participate in Main Combat.

Main Combat Order

Both sides resolve each category simultaneously before moving to the next category:

  1. Ranged/Caster
  2. Aerial
  3. Infantry and Artillery
  4. Siege and Saboteur
  5. Engineer and Support
  6. PC and Brigadier General

Within each category, use the normal Warfront ATK/DEF/SIEGE rules unless a rule explicitly provides a different roll. Artillery's normal Main Combat statistic remains ATK 0 / DEF 2 / END 1. Siege uses its listed SIEGE statistic during Main Combat. Saboteur can modify only a Main Combat Siege attack and cannot modify a Preliminary Siege Attack. Engineer performs its repair during the Engineer + Support Main Combat step.

Commitment

At the beginning of a contested Battlefront or grid engagement, each side chooses which eligible Units are committed. Other eligible Units may be held back. Once the Preliminary Round begins, no Unit may reinforce, pull back, redeploy, or retreat during that Preliminary Round or Main Combat exchange.

Each committed attacking combat Unit rolls one ATK die when its Main Combat category resolves. Each committed defending combat Unit rolls one DEF die when its Main Combat category resolves. A successful DEF roll does not cancel an attacking Hit; it creates a separate Hit against the attacking force. Resolve Hits, END reduction, eliminations, and applicable effects according to the category timing before continuing to the next category.

DecisionEffect
ContinueFight another Battle Exchange with the surviving committed force.
ReinforceAdd eligible held-back or legally reinforcing Units before the next Battle Exchange.
Pull BackRemove selected surviving committed Units from the next exchange.
RetreatWithdraw according to the scenario's retreat rules.

Who declares first in a Decision Window is determined by GM discretion.

Decision-Window Reinforcement Movement

  • An eligible Unit may move exactly 1 connected active Battlefront/grid during a Decision Window to reinforce that engagement.
  • This 1-grid limit also applies to Aerial Units during a Decision Window; their 2-grid allowance applies only in the normal Movement Phase.
  • A reinforcing Unit joins the next Battle Exchange and cannot chain multiple Decision-Window reinforcement moves during the same Battle Phase.
  • Reinforcing Support immediately grants its legal ATK/DEF bonus for the next Main Combat.
  • Legal Artillery and Saboteur effects can apply to the next Main Combat after entry, subject to their normal timing restrictions.
  • Scout and Assassin cannot be introduced or used during a Decision Window; both are Preliminary Round Specialists.

Battlefront Changes and External Reinforcements

A Battlefront change is counted per distinct strategic transition, not per Unit token. Multiple Units moving together across the same connection count as 1 change.

  • Retreating from one active Battlefront/grid to another: 1 change.
  • Lateral movement between active connected Battlefronts/grids: 1 change.
  • An Aerial Unit traversing 2 active Battlefront/grid transitions in one legal move: 2 changes.
  • Battle Exchanges and casualties never advance the counter.

Threshold: changes accumulate to 3. No more than one External Reinforcement Check can resolve per completed Battle Phase; excess changes carry forward.

1d10External Reinforcement Result
1–4Friendly reinforcements arrive.
5–8Opposing reinforcements arrive.
9–10Nothing happens.

If the roll results in Friendly reinforcements, the Players choose who will roll the first d20 to determine the composition of the reinforcements according to the rollable table that is provided. If the result is for the Opposing reinforcements, it will be the GM that will roll the provided die. A second roll table will determine the Tier of the units. In the event that the Unit composition has a set maximum Tier level that is less than the result of the 2nd roll, it means an automatic maximum Tier available to that unit composition.

Infantry

Only Infantry captures or recaptures Major Ground Objectives and Open Abyss grids.

Levy Infantry

Levy represent hastily mobilized reserves, militia, auxiliaries, emergency garrison troops, and other expendable mass manpower. They remain ATK 1 / DEF 1 at every Tier; higher Tier represents formation depth and END rather than better training.

  • Cannot receive Support's +1 ATK, +1 DEF, or END restoration.
  • Cannot receive Artillery's additional Infantry Attack Die.
  • Allied Artillery can still grant Levy permission to attack Aerial Units.
  • Cannot alone capture or recapture a Major Ground Objective or change an Open Abyss grid's control; at least one surviving non-Levy Infantry Unit must be present when control changes.

Ranged / Caster Units

Ranged/Caster Units must physically occupy the Battlefront/grid where they fight and may target Aerial Units.

Defending Ranged/Caster Units within the same grid that has at least 1 Fortification point will receive a +1 to their DEF roll. This applies to all Ranged/Caster units.

Artillery

ATK 0 / DEF 2 / END 1. During the Preliminary Round, each Artillery makes one special Preliminary Attack: roll 1d6, and only a 1 scores 1 Hit. The Preliminary Attack can target only Infantry and Ranged/Caster Units; it cannot target Specialists, Aerial Units, other Artillery, or Support Units. The target's owner chooses which eligible Unit suffers the Hit. The Preliminary Attack does not use ATK 0.

During Main Combat, Artillery uses its normal ATK 0 / DEF 2 statistics. One Artillery may grant one additional Attack Die to one eligible attacking Infantry Unit: Light, Line, Assault, or Vanguard. Heavy, Guard, Elite, Levy, and non-Infantry Units are ineligible. One Infantry can receive at most one Artillery bonus die in the same Battle Exchange. Artillery also enables all allied Infantry in the same Battlefront/grid, including Levy, to attack Aerial Units.

Siege Units

Each Siege Unit makes one Preliminary Attack before Main Combat. Roll 1d6; only a 1 is a successful Preliminary SIEGE Hit, removing 1 Fortification Point. During Main Combat, Siege uses its listed SIEGE statistic normally. Saboteur can modify only a Main Combat Siege attack. Siege cannot proceed beyond the Main Gate and is excluded from Open Abyss Battles.

Support

ATK 0 / DEF 2 / END 2.

Support defense: A Support Unit does not make an attack roll (ATK 0). However, when the Support Unit itself is the Unit defending in a grid, it may roll its DEF die normally using DEF 2.

  • A Support aura affects no more than five eligible allied Units in the same Battlefront during a Battle Exchange.
  • Multiple Support Units do not stack the bonus on the same Unit and do not expand the five-Unit aura limit.
  • When attacking, eligible allied Units gain +1 ATK; when defending, they gain +1 DEF.
  • Support does not increase Artillery ATK or Siege SIEGE.
  • After casualties, each surviving Support that already participated in the engagement may restore 1 END to one surviving damaged eligible Unit in that Battlefront during the following Decision Window.
  • Levy Infantry are not eligible for any Support bonus or END restoration.

Specialists

All Specialists are Tier I, END 1, and roll no ATK or DEF die.

Players can choose them as casualties in exchange of other more important units.

Aerial Units

Warfront Aerial Units do not track character-scale Flight Slots or individual Wind Serum consumption; their WA cost abstracts sustained mass-flight logistics.

Warfront Unit Encyclopedia

This encyclopedia describes the military identity, training, purpose, and typical deployment of the formations in the Universal Unit Catalogue. These entries are descriptive reference material only. They do not add abilities, modify statistics, change War Asset costs, or override any Warfront timing, targeting, movement, or objective-control rule. Where a formation's exact tactical doctrine is not separately defined by the rules, the description below is explanatory rather than a new mechanic.

Infantry Formations

Levy Infantry — Emergency Mobilization. Levy Infantry are hastily mobilized Daevas, reserve personnel, militia, auxiliaries, and emergency garrison troops assembled when regular forces cannot meet the demands of a campaign. Their ranks may include newly recruited defenders, inexperienced combatants, and personnel reassigned from other duties. Their principal value is available manpower rather than advanced training. Higher Tiers represent formation depth and endurance, not improved individual training. Levy retains its published ATK 1 / DEF 1 profile and all existing restrictions.

Light Infantry — Mobile Ground Troops. Light Infantry are organized and equipped for flexible deployment, with an emphasis on mobility rather than heavy protection. They can reinforce threatened positions, secure approaches, and support larger formations as the battle develops. They provide commanders with adaptable regular troops for assignments that do not require specialized assault or defensive formations.

Line Infantry — Formation Warfare. Line Infantry form the dependable core of a conventional field army. Trained to operate in coordinated ranks and follow battlefield commands, they establish and maintain the main battle line, support advances, and reinforce neighboring formations. Their value lies in discipline and consistency throughout a prolonged engagement.

Assault Infantry — Offensive Pressure. Assault Infantry are trained and equipped for direct offensive action. Their doctrine emphasizes closing with enemy formations, disrupting defensive cohesion, and concentrating pressure at a chosen point. They are committed when a commander needs to challenge a defensive line and create an opening for the wider force.

Vanguard Infantry — Forward Assault. Vanguard Infantry lead demanding advances and are expected to establish a foothold in contested ground while maintaining enough cohesion for supporting forces to follow. They are commonly assigned to breakthrough attempts and important approaches where the first successful advance can shape the rest of the engagement.

Heavy Infantry — Armored Ground Forces. Heavy Infantry are equipped and trained to endure concentrated opposition. They reinforce critical portions of the battle line and remain engaged in conditions where lighter troops may struggle to hold their position. Their military identity centers on protection and sustained combat rather than speed.

Guard Infantry — Position Defense. Guard Infantry specialize in protecting strategically important positions. Their doctrine emphasizes formation discipline, resistance to penetration, and the defense of approaches and ground that cannot easily be abandoned. They may also help secure ground captured by advancing forces.

Elite Infantry — Veteran Professionals. Elite Infantry are highly trained, experienced, and carefully selected formations drawn from the most capable personnel available to a legion or regional command. Their preparation and discipline make them suitable for demanding assignments, decisive engagements, and critical defensive positions. They may reinforce a failing line, lead an important assault, or serve as a commander's dependable reserve. Their scarcity makes their commitment an operational priority rather than a routine choice.

Ranged/Caster Formations

Ranged/Caster formations combine conventional ranged troops and combat spellcasters under one Warfront category. Their battlefield identity is defined by ranged offensive support rather than by whether their personnel wield weapons or Aether-based magic. They must follow the existing Ranged/Caster rules and catalogue profiles.

Light Ranged/Caster — Basic Ranged Support. These formations comprise regular archers, crossbow troops, and combat spellcasters trained to contribute ranged attacks to a larger engagement. They provide an accessible ranged capability and are commonly deployed alongside ground forces that can help secure their position.

Battle Ranged/Caster — Coordinated Offense. Battle formations represent professionally trained ranged combatants and spellcasters accustomed to coordinated military operations. They support major field engagements through disciplined attacks and integration with the movements of allied formations.

War Ranged/Caster — Advanced Ranged Forces. War formations represent highly capable ranged troops and combat spellcasters prepared for major military operations. They are committed when a commander requires substantial ranged or magical pressure as a principal component of the battle plan.

Aerial Formations

Aerial formations consist of Daevas organized for military operations above the battlefield. They provide an airborne presence, engage opposing aerial forces, and support wider operations. Their movement and resource abstractions remain governed by the published aerial rules; these descriptions do not create additional flight capabilities.

Light Aerial — Flexible Air Support. Light Aerial formations are airborne troops prepared for basic aerial combat and general assignments. They give commanders a flexible aerial contingent without requiring the commitment of more specialized formations.

Aerial Line — Organized Air Formations. Aerial Line formations are the dependable core of an organized airborne force. Their training emphasizes maintaining formation, coordinating movement, and carrying out established orders during aerial engagements.

Aerial Assault — Offensive Airpower. Aerial Assault formations specialize in delivering direct offensive pressure from the air. They are deployed when a commander needs an aggressive aerial commitment to challenge opposing formations and create opportunities for allied forces.

Aerial Vanguard — Forward Air Operations. Aerial Vanguard formations lead demanding airborne advances. They are expected to operate at the front of an aerial engagement and establish opportunities for supporting formations to follow.

Aerial Guard — Defensive Air Formation. Aerial Guard formations are organized to resist enemy pressure and maintain a dependable defensive presence in the air. Their doctrine emphasizes defensive coordination and the protection of formation cohesion during an engagement.

Aerial Defender — Airborne Force Protection. Aerial Defender formations are assigned to protect important elements of an airborne force and preserve the security of aerial operations. Their doctrinal emphasis is protective duty, rather than serving as the principal offensive formation.

Elite Aerial — Veteran Airborne Professionals. Elite Aerial formations consist of highly experienced Daevas selected for their aerial combat discipline and operational reliability. They are reserved for decisive aerial engagements and critical sectors where less experienced formations may not be sufficient.

Artillery

Artillery — Heavy Ranged Support. Artillery consists of organized crews operating heavy ranged weapons to deliver fire against enemy formations and support allied ground operations. Depending on the army and its technology, these may include conventional projectile weapons, Aether-powered launchers, or other military engines. Artillery contributes pressure during its defined Preliminary and Main Combat procedures, but does not replace frontline troops or independently capture objectives. Its exact targets, statistics, and timing remain those stated in the rules.

Siege Formations

Field Siege — Mobile Breaching Equipment. Field Siege formations use comparatively mobile siege engines and trained crews to support fortress assaults. They provide dedicated breaching capability while remaining practical to deploy as part of a broader military operation.

Heavy Siege — Heavy Breaching Equipment. Heavy Siege formations employ powerful engines and specialized crews for substantial defensive works. They are committed when a fortress or defensive obstacle demands a concentrated siege effort beyond ordinary field equipment.

Armored Siege — Protected Siege Equipment. Armored Siege formations use reinforced or protected siege engines designed to continue operating under dangerous battlefield conditions. Their defining military purpose is to protect the machinery and crew during contested approaches. They remain siege formations and do not substitute for mobile infantry.

Support

Support — Battlefield Coordination and Sustainment. Support represents personnel and command structures that help an army function as a coordinated fighting force. Depending on the force, these may include tactical coordinators, signal personnel, supply specialists, battlefield aides, and other trained staff. Their value lies in assisting eligible allied formations and helping sustain combat effectiveness. All Support limits, bonuses, restoration, and eligibility restrictions remain exactly as published.

Support defense: A Support Unit does not make an attack roll (ATK 0). However, when the Support Unit itself is the Unit defending in a grid, it may roll its DEF die normally using DEF 2.

Specialist Formations

Specialists are purpose-trained military assets assigned to specific operational tasks rather than ordinary formation combat. They have the published Specialist statistics and timing restrictions; the following entries explain their military role without adding new effects.

Scout — Reconnaissance. Scouts observe enemy movements and gather information relevant to military planning. In a Warfront, their published Preliminary Round function provides a limited count of enemy Units in the next Battlefront. A Scout is expended when used and cannot be introduced after the Preliminary Round.

Engineer — Fortification Maintenance. Engineers are construction and fortification specialists trained to assess structural damage and restore defensive works under operational conditions. Their Warfront role is limited to the published defense-only repair procedure, Repair Capacity, timing, and operation-wide limits.

Saboteur — Siege Disruption. Saboteurs are specialists trained to interfere with enemy military equipment and siege operations. Their Warfront function is the published one-use modification to a single Main Combat Siege attack; they cannot modify a Preliminary Siege Attack.

Assassin — Targeted Elimination. Assassins are covert operatives trained to strike selected enemy personnel before or at the opening of a conventional engagement. Their Warfront use is limited to the published Preliminary Round effect against one eligible personnel-based enemy Unit. Assassins cannot gang up on one target and cannot be used after the Preliminary Round.

Player Character Warfront Participation

Each participating PC uses one d6. PCs at Levels 1–10 have ATK 3 / DEF 3. PCs at Levels 11–20 have ATK 4 / DEF 4.

PC Rerolls. Each PC has a maximum of 3 rerolls for the entire Warfront. A PC chooses whether to reroll a Warfront die they personally rolled. All three rerolls may be spent on the same original dice roll if the PC chooses. Once all 3 rerolls are spent, no further rerolls are available for that PC during that Warfront.

  • PCs are not Warfront Units, have no Tier or END, and cost no WA.
  • PC ATK/DEF are not modified by Support, Artillery, Unit effects, class features, equipment, spells, Wings, feats, or other character-scale mechanics unless a rule explicitly names PCs.
  • Ordinary Warfront Hits cannot target or be assigned to PCs, and Assassin Specialists cannot target PCs.
  • PCs cannot capture Major Ground Objectives or Open Abyss grids.
  • PCs may target Aerial Units directly without Artillery, but do not grant allied Infantry anti-Aerial capability.
  • At least one allied Warfront Unit must normally accompany PCs in an active Battlefront/grid.
  • At the start of the overall battle, each PC declares Ground or Aerial assignment; this determines that PC's Warfront movement mode for the battle.
  • Daeva Point and Ultimate Exclusion: PCs neither gain nor spend DP and cannot activate Ultimates anywhere within a Warfront operation. Existing DP is retained but inaccessible until the Warfront ends.

PC Targeting and Casualty Selection

A PC may target only an enemy Unit in the same Battlefront or grid where that PC is actively clashing. Before making an attack roll, the player must declare one target category: Infantry, Ranged/Caster, Artillery, Support, Specialists, or Aerial. The declared category must be present in the same active clash.

The player declares only the target category, not a specific Unit profile or individual Unit token. If the player declares Infantry, for example, the GM may select any enemy Infantry Unit in that same grid as the casualty, whether it is Levy, Line, Heavy, or another Infantry profile. The GM chooses the specific eligible Unit that suffers the PC attack's Hit(s) from within the declared category.

The player must declare the target category before rolling. A successful Hit cannot be redirected to a different category after the roll. PCs may target Aerial Units directly if an enemy Aerial Unit is present in the same active clash; this does not grant allied Infantry the ability to attack Aerial Units. Fortifications are not a valid PC target. Fortifications can only be attacked during the Preliminary Round using the applicable Warfront rules.

Universal END Recovery

  • Decision Window Recovery: a surviving Unit that remains committed and will continue into the next exchange regains 1 END, up to maximum.
  • A Unit that Pulls Back, Retreats, or otherwise leaves during that Decision Window does not receive that immediate recovery.
  • Post-Battle-Phase Recovery: eligible withdrawn Units recover only after the entire Battle Phase across the board concludes, subject to scenario location restrictions.
  • Support restoration is separate from normal END recovery.
  • Inter-Battle Reset: after a complete separate Warfront battle ends, every surviving Unit returns to maximum END. END loss never carries between separate Fortress Wars or Open Abyss Battles.

Fortress Wars

Fortress Classes

FortressMain GatesRampart Requirement
Standard2Each Gate requires both Ramparts connected to that Gate.
Major1All four Ramparts must be captured before the single Main Gate may be contested.

Both use the same strategic depth: Outer Approach → Inner Approach → Rampart → Main Gate → Inner Fortress → Aether Controller → Fortress Core.

Directional Exterior Structure

Each Fortress has North, East, South, and West exterior sectors. Each sector contains an Outer Approach, Inner Approach, and Rampart. Units may move only across connections shown by the Fortress board. Captured Battlefronts do not require a standing garrison; control persists until enemy Infantry recaptures them.

Standard Fortress Topology

  • South + West Ramparts connect to one Main Gate.
  • North + East Ramparts connect to the other Main Gate.
  • Both Ramparts linked to a specific Gate must be captured before that Gate may be contested.
  • The two Gate routes progress independently and converge into the Inner Fortress.

Major Fortress Topology

  • Exactly one Main Gate.
  • North, East, South, and West Ramparts all connect to it.
  • All four Ramparts must be captured before the Gate may be contested.

Main-Gate Unlock Timing

When the final required Rampart is captured, its Gate becomes accessible immediately. Until a still-uncaptured Gate is actually captured, defenders can close access again by recapturing a prerequisite Rampart. Once a Main Gate itself has been captured, later loss of an exterior prerequisite Rampart does not relock that captured Gate.

Fortress Aerial Movement

  • Outside the Main Gate, Aerial Units may move up to 2 connected Battlefront levels per normal Movement Phase.
  • Inside the Main Gate, they revert to normal 1-Battlefront movement.
  • An Aerial Unit cannot cross an uncontrolled intermediate Battlefront to penetrate deeper.
  • A legal two-transition move counts as two changes for the External Reinforcement counter.

Fortress External Reinforcements

Fortress external reinforcements enter from outside. Roll 1d4: 1 North, 2 East, 3 South, 4 West. They cannot bypass an enemy-controlled exterior Battlefront to appear deeper in the Fortress. The first three Fortress reinforcement checks use the normal 1d10 table; after the third Fortress reinforcement check, later qualifying checks automatically reinforce the Fortress defender.

Brigadier-General

Every active Fortress Core has a Brigadier-General. The Brigadier-General is not purchased with WA and is the mandatory final defender of every active Fortress Core. It remains exclusively inside the Fortress Core and cannot move, redeploy, reinforce another Battlefront, or defend the Aether Controller.

Aether Controller and Fortress Core

The Aether Controller is a normal Major Ground Objective and requires at least one surviving attacking Infantry Unit to capture. The Fortress Core is inaccessible while the Aether Controller remains defender-controlled.

The Fortress Core uses a two-phase procedure as described below.

  • Only PCs physically present at the Core when the encounter begins participate. PCs elsewhere cannot join after the encounter begins.
  • Allied Warfront Units do not become individual creatures in the Phase 2 character-scale encounter; their surviving END contributes to the PCs through the Allied Force HP Conversion rule.
  • The broader Warfront pauses during Phase 2 of the Brigadier-General encounter.
  • If PCs withdraw and later return while the Brigadier-General remains alive, the Brigadier-General begins the new encounter at maximum HP and maximum resources. Converted END cannot be reimbursed back.
  • Defeating the Brigadier-General immediately captures the Fortress and ends all remaining Warfront battles.

Player-site boundary: the exact GM-facing Brigadier-General enemy statblock and closed defender-side GM resolution table are intentionally not published here.

Fortress Retreat and End Conditions

  • Surviving defending Units may escape off-map and remain in their Force Pool.
  • If the attacking side declares overall retreat, the Fortress War ends.
  • After the Warfront ends, pursuit, surrender, capture, escape, resistance, and campaign consequences are narrative/campaign material.

Fortress Core Phase 1 - Last Defense

Core Command Unit. When attackers enter the Fortress Core, the Brigadier-General is represented on the Warfront board as a special Core Command Unit: ATK 0 / DEF +4 / END 1.

The Brigadier-General cannot be selected as a casualty, cannot be targeted by Assassins, and receives no Support modifiers. Consequently, the Brigadier-General does not receive Fortification DEF benefit because the Fortress Core has no Fortification Points. Ordinary defending Units fight around the Brigadier-General normally.

Phase 1 continues until every ordinary defending Unit in the Fortress Core, except the Brigadier-General (as well as the PCs in case they are the defenders), is eliminated. Normal Decision Window reinforcement remains available while Phase 1 is active.

If all ordinary defenders are removed but no PCs are physically present in the Fortress Core, Phase 2 cannot begin. The attacking Warfront Units pull back to the Aether Controller, leaving the Brigadier-General alone in the Core until PCs arrive and the attackers later re-enter. If the Aether Controller is controlled by the defenders, the retreating attacker units will have a clash at the Aether Controller as normal.

Fortress Core Phase 2A — Player Assault Against an NPC Fortress

Use this procedure when the player-controlled force attacks a Fortress held by the GM. If the attacking side wins Phase 1 and one or more PCs are physically present in the Fortress Core at the transition, immediately begin a separate character-scale encounter against the Brigadier-General. Only PCs physically present at the transition participate. PCs elsewhere cannot join after the encounter begins. The broader Warfront pauses for the duration of this encounter.

Allied Force HP Conversion. Total the current remaining END of every surviving player-side Warfront Unit physically present in the Core. Convert at 1 remaining END = 1 temporary encounter HP. Divide that pool equally among participating PCs, rounding each share up. Each participating PC increases both current HP and maximum HP by that amount for this encounter only. The temporary increase ends when this encounter ends and never carries forward.

If PCs withdraw and later return while the Brigadier-General remains alive, it begins the new encounter at maximum HP and maximum resources. Previously converted END is not reimbursed.

Fortress Core Phase 2B — Player Defense Against a GM Assault

Use this procedure when the player-controlled force defends its Fortress against a GM-controlled attacking force. If the GM attackers win Phase 1 and one or more PCs are physically present in the Core, resolve the final defense using an opposed d100 roll rather than a character-scale boss encounter.

GM side wins: The Fortress is captured by the GM-controlled attacking force. Resolve the campaign consequences normally.

Grid adjacency: A Unit may move one adjacent grid during the normal Movement Phase. Aerial Units may move up to two adjacent grids. This movement allowance applies to all Unit categories unless another rule expressly restricts them.

Victory and Deployment

  • Victory comes from removing all opposing Units or forcing the opposing side to Retreat/Surrender.
  • Reserve Units count as surviving forces.
  • If both sides completely withdraw surviving forces into Reserve, the battle ends and the GM narrates the outcome.
  • Each side deploys freely within its own 3×3 zone and may also place Units in Reserve. There is no grid capacity limit.

Grid Control

  • Only Infantry captures or recaptures enemy-controlled grids.
  • An Infantry Unit entering an empty enemy-controlled grid captures it automatically without a Battle Exchange.
  • Levy Infantry cannot change grid control by themselves; at least one surviving non-Levy Infantry must be present when control changes.
  • PCs never capture or control grids by themselves.

Open Abyss Movement

  • Most Units move 1 connected grid during the normal Movement Phase.
  • Aerial Units may move up to 2 connected grids under the Open Abyss movement rules.
  • Decision-Window reinforcement remains limited to 1 connected active grid even for Aerial Units.
  • Reserve-to-Rear movement is not a Battlefront change because Reserve is not a Battlefront.
  • Siege Units are excluded from Open Abyss Battles.
  • Assassin Specialists have no Open Abyss operation-wide cap.

Retreat, Encirclement, and Cut Off PCs

Retreat movement: A retreating Unit may move only into an adjacent allied-controlled active grid. It cannot retreat into an empty or enemy-controlled grid. If every adjacent grid is enemy-controlled, retreat is not possible; the side must attack an enemy-controlled grid to create a breakthrough before it can retreat through that route. If the allied-controlled destination is already engaged, the retreating Unit enters it but joins only at that engagement's Decision Window.

When a side declares overall Retreat or Surrender, a surviving Unit escapes only if a connected route to its own Reserve remains open and is not enemy-controlled. Trapped Units with no valid route are eliminated if no PCs are present.

A PC with no valid connected route back to Reserve is Cut Off. A Cut Off PC remains in Warfront play, cannot Retreat until a route reopens, stays in the current grid, and uses its level-based ATK/DEF values. If the last allied Unit with a Cut Off PC is eliminated, the PC remains on the board as the explicit exception to the normal accompaniment rule. Ordinary Warfront Hits still cannot be assigned to that PC, and the PC still cannot control the grid.

Open Abyss External Reinforcements

  • Uses the universal 3-change threshold and maximum-one-check-per-completed-Battle-Phase pacing.
  • Hard maximum of 3 External Reinforcement Checks for the entire Open Abyss Battle.
  • After the third check, no further external reinforcements can arrive.
  • External reinforcements arrive in the receiving side's Reserve Line.
  • Once either side declares overall Retreat or Surrender, no further External Reinforcement Checks or arrivals occur.

What Makes Up One Warfront Round?

One complete Warfront Round has three phases, resolved in this order:

  1. Movement Phase. Both sides complete their legal strategic movement.
  2. Battle Phase. Resolve every engagement created by that Movement Phase. Each engagement begins with Commitment, then the Preliminary Round, then Combat and a Decision Window. If opposing Units remain in the same grid after the Decision Window, another Combat takes place, followed by another Decision Window. This Combat → Decision Window sequence repeats until the grid is captured or successfully defended and the engagement is resolved. The Battle Phase continues until all engagements from that Movement Phase have been resolved.
  3. Support Recovery Phase. After the Battle Phase is complete, Support Units restore END to eligible friendly Units in their own grids that were not engaged in combat. Each Support Unit can restore 1 END to 1 eligible Unit in the same grid. Levy Units are not eligible. Units that have been pulled out of combat can therefore be considered for recovery if they are in a grid that was not engaged in combat and the other requirements are met.

Commitment: Committed Units are the Units each side chooses to remain and engage in that particular grid during its Battle Phase. At the start of the engagement, both sides decide which of their Units will commit. Each side may pull back any Units it does not want to commit to that Battle Phase. Units that are pulled back are not part of that grid's committed force and do not take part in its Combat.

Additional commitment during a Decision Window: Both sides may commit additional eligible Units to an engagement during a Decision Window. Each additional Unit must be in a grid adjacent to the grid currently in combat and must be capable of moving into that combat grid. Eligible categories are Infantry, Ranged/Caster, Artillery, Support, and Aerial. These Units move into the engaged grid and join the next Combat. Siege Units and Specialists cannot join an ongoing engagement during a Decision Window; they may join only during the next Warfront Round, subject to normal movement and engagement rules.

The Warfront Round ends after Support Recovery. Only then does the next Movement Phase begin. Support-based END recovery occurs at two distinct times: during a Decision Window, each Support Unit can restore 1 END to 1 eligible friendly Unit in the same engaged grid that will continue into the next Combat; after the entire Battle Phase, each Support Unit can restore 1 END to every eligible allied Unit (except Levy) in its own grid, provided that grid was not engaged in combat at any point during the Warfront Round.

How to Play — Step-by-Step Warfront Tutorial

Beginner guide · Illustrative examples

This tutorial walks through the order of play using sample forces and fictional dice results. The numbers below demonstrate how the existing rules operate; they are not additional bonuses, special abilities, or new rules. Where the rules leave a choice to the GM or the owning side, the example says so rather than creating a universal procedure.

Before the battle: build and deploy your force

  1. Receive your War Asset allowance. The GM announces the operation's Base WA and any restrictions. The player side may purchase Supplemental WA at 1,000 Kinah per +1 WA, up to 25% of its Base WA, rounded down.
  2. Select Units. Choose Units from the Universal Unit Catalogue and keep the total within the available WA. Check each Unit's ATK, DEF, SIEGE (if applicable), END, Tier, and WA cost.
  3. Set END. A tiered Unit's END equals its Tier (Tier I = 1 END through Tier V = 5 END). Fixed-END Units use the value printed in the catalogue.
  4. Deploy. Follow the scenario's deployment rules. In an Open Abyss Battle, each side deploys within its own 3×3 zone and may also place Units in its protected Reserve Line.
  5. Assign PCs. Each participating PC declares Ground or Aerial assignment at the start of the overall battle. PCs are not Warfront Units, do not cost WA, and do not have Tier or END.

Read the dice: what counts as a Hit?

For a normal Warfront attack, roll 1d6. A result equal to or lower than the Unit's ATK scores 1 Hit. When defending, roll 1d6; a result equal to or lower than DEF scores 1 Hit against the attacking force. A successful DEF roll does not cancel an attacker's Hit: both sides can inflict Hits in the same category.

An engagement is now identified. At Commitment, both sides choose which Units will remain to engage in that grid. Each side may pull back any Units it does not want to commit to this Battle Phase. Step 2 — Preliminary Round. Before Main Combat, resolve applicable Preliminary actions. The Field Siege rolls 1d6. The sample result is 1, so it scores a Preliminary Siege Hit and removes 1 Fortification Point from the targeted Fortification. If the die had shown 2–6, it would not have scored a Preliminary Siege Hit.

The Artillery also rolls 1d6. The sample result is 1, so it deals 1 Hit to an eligible enemy Infantry or Ranged/Caster Unit. It cannot target Artillery, Siege, PCs, or the Brigadier-General, and another Assassin cannot join it against the same target.

Step 3 — Main Combat categories. Resolve the categories in order: Ranged/Caster, Aerial, Infantry and Artillery, Siege and Saboteur, Engineer and Support, then PC and Brigadier-General. Only resolve categories for which the engagement has eligible participants.

For the Infantry and Artillery category, the Vanguard attacks with ATK 3 and rolls 2: 1 Hit. The Line attacks with ATK 2 and rolls 4: no Hit. The GM's committed defender rolls its DEF die; for this example it rolls 5 against DEF 2 and scores no defensive Hit. If a defender had rolled 1 or 2, it would have inflicted a separate Hit against the attacking force; it would not cancel the Vanguard's Hit.

Step 4 — PC participation. A PC actively clashing in this same Battlefront chooses a target category that is present there before rolling. For example, the player declares Infantry, then rolls the PC's d6 using the PC's level-based ATK. If the roll is a Hit, the GM chooses which eligible enemy Infantry Unit in this same Battlefront suffers it. The player cannot redirect the Hit to another category after seeing the roll. PCs cannot target Fortifications; those are attacked in the Preliminary Round.

Step 5 — Decision Window. After Main Combat, the sides decide whether to continue, reinforce, pull back, or retreat. Both sides may commit additional Units that are in a grid adjacent to the grid in combat and are capable of moving into it. Only Infantry, Ranged/Caster, Artillery, Support, and Aerial Units may join during this Decision Window; Siege Units and Specialists cannot join until the next Warfront Round. Eligible reinforcements move into the engaged grid and participate in the next Combat, not the Combat that has already resolved. If a friendly Support Unit is present in the engaged grid, it may restore 1 END to 1 eligible friendly Unit in that grid that will continue into the next Combat. Each Support Unit can restore END to only one Unit during that Decision Window. If opposing Units remain in the same grid, another Combat follows, then another Decision Window. This Combat → Decision Window sequence repeats until the grid is captured or successfully defended and the engagement is resolved.

Step 6 — Repeat or withdraw. If opposing Units remain in the grid, resolve another Combat, then open another Decision Window. Repeat Combat → Decision Window until the grid is captured or successfully defended and the engagement is resolved. The Battle Phase is not over until every engagement created during the Movement Phase has been resolved. After that, the Warfront enters the separate Support Recovery Phase.

Continuing the Fortress assault

As the players advance, the Fortress's directional topology matters. For a Standard Fortress, both Ramparts connected to a particular Main Gate must be captured before that Gate can be contested. Once the required Ramparts are captured, that Gate becomes accessible. After the Gate, the force advances through the Inner Fortress and Aether Controller. The Aether Controller must be captured by at least one surviving attacking Infantry Unit before the Fortress Core becomes accessible.

At the Core, follow the Fortress Core Phase 1 procedure. The Brigadier-General is represented by the special Core Command Unit while ordinary defenders fight around it. If the attackers win Phase 1 and PCs are physically present, use the correct Phase 2 procedure for the situation: player assault against an NPC Fortress transitions to the character-scale Brigadier-General encounter; player defense against a GM assault uses the closed opposed d100 procedure. The two procedures are not interchangeable.

Decision Window Support Recovery:

Support Recovery Phase: After the entire Battle Phase, each Support Unit restores 1 END to all allied Units except Levy in its own grid, provided that grid was not engaged in combat at any point during the Warfront Round.

Remember: a successful DEF roll inflicts a separate Hit; it does not cancel an ATK Hit. A Unit at 0 END is eliminated. Only Infantry captures grids. PCs attack only an enemy Unit category present in the same grid/Battlefront where they are actively clashing.

Quick Warfront Combat Reference

SIMULTANEOUS MOVEMENT → IDENTIFY ENGAGEMENTS → PRELIMINARY ROUND → REMOVE PRELIMINARY CASUALTIES → MAIN COMBAT (RANGED/CASTER → AERIAL → INFANTRY & ARTILLERY → SIEGE & SABOTEUR → ENGINEER & SUPPORT → PC & BRIGADIER GENERAL) → DECISION WINDOW → NEXT BATTLE EXCHANGE → END-OF-BATTLE-PHASE RESOLUTION → NEXT MOVEMENT PHASE
  • Resolve Artillery and Siege Preliminary Attacks and Scout/Assassin abilities before Main Combat.
  • Apply all Preliminary Hits and effects, then remove Units reduced to 0 END before Main Combat.
  • Both sides resolve each Main Combat category simultaneously in the stated hierarchy.
  • Each successful normal ATK/DEF die inflicts 1 Hit; DEF Hits do not cancel ATK Hits.
  • Reduce END and remove Units at 0 END.
  • After Main Combat, open the Decision Window.
  • Units staying committed for the next exchange regain 1 END; Units leaving do not.
  • After all engagements on the board conclude, resolve eligible post-Battle-Phase recovery and at most one External Reinforcement Check if the accumulated counter is at least 3.
  • Apply scenario-specific control, movement, retreat, and reinforcement rules.
Scale Separation
Do not transfer ordinary AC, weapon quality, spell attack bonuses, class resources, Combat Flight Slot expenditure, or other character-scale mechanics directly into Warfront dice unless a Warfront rule explicitly says so.
