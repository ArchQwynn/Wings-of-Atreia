PLAYER-CREATED LEGIONS

A player-created Legion is a lightweight organization for shared identity, missions, resources, and cross-faction cooperation when the campaign permits it. Legion membership does not create a new character progression track or grant automatic combat bonuses.

When a Legion Can Be Founded

  • Campaign Gate. The Campaign Director or controlling GM must explicitly declare Legion Founding available. Reaching Level 11 by itself does not unlock the system.
  • Founding Player. One Founding Player must be Level 11 or higher. That character becomes the initial Legion Leader.
  • Other members. Every other founding or joining member must be Level 3 or higher before joining and must already have reached their Specialization or Archetype choice where applicable. A Level 1-2 character cannot join early.
  • Membership has no maximum size. There is no minimum number of additional founders; a qualifying Founding Player may establish the Legion alone if all other founding requirements are satisfied.
  • Elyos and Asmodians may belong to the same Legion once the required campaign state is active. Every member retains their original faction identity and its normal consequences.

Founding Fund and Treasury

  • Founding requires 5,000 Kinah deposited into the new Legion Treasury. Eligible founding members may contribute toward the total.
  • Before establishment, the Founding Fund cannot be withdrawn. If founding is abandoned, each contributor receives their contribution back. Once established, the 5,000 Kinah remains usable Treasury money.
  • Any Legion member may deposit Kinah. Only the Legion Leader may withdraw Kinah or authorize spending.
  • Legion missions may reward participating members, the Treasury, or both as determined by the GM.
  • There is no passive Legion income, automatic salary, interest, business-profit engine, or mandatory recurring membership dues.

Leadership and Membership

  • The only default roles are Legion Leader and Legion Member.
  • The Legion Leader admits and removes members, controls permissions for Legion assets, may transfer leadership, and may formally dissolve the Legion.
  • Leadership may be transferred to any existing legal Legion member. The new Leader does not need to be Level 11; Level 11 is a founding requirement only.

Member Benefits

  • Members may display the Legion name, colors, crest, and insignia. Ordinary Legion identification occupies no Inventory Slot.
  • Members may accept Legion missions and may use resources, facilities, contacts, and support the Legion actually possesses when the Legion Leader permits it.
  • Where the campaign's cross-faction arrangements recognize the Legion, its insignia provides a legitimate reason for Elyos and Asmodian members to operate together. It does not grant diplomatic immunity or unrestricted access.
  • Legion membership grants no automatic Hit, AC, damage, HP, saving throw, skill, Advantage, Spell DC, DP, class-resource, equipment-quality, or personal Inventory bonus.

Legion Storage and Headquarters

  • An established Legion has one secure shared Storage location with a capacity of 100 Inventory Slots. Normal Inventory Slot and stacking rules apply; Kinah remains in the Treasury and uses no Storage Slots.
  • Any Legion member may deposit eligible items. Only the Legion Leader may withdraw items or authorize another member to retrieve them.
  • Legion Storage is stationary and cannot be accessed remotely. It never increases a member's personal Inventory capacity in the field.
  • A Legion may designate one legitimate location as its Headquarters. The Headquarters is simply its meeting place and Storage location; it grants no automatic bonuses and has no upgrade track.

Lightweight Rule. There is no Legion XP, Legion Level, officer hierarchy, skill tree, research tree, reputation meter, headquarters progression, or other default Legion management subsystem.

Abyss Warfront System

Warfronts resolve formation-scale warfare separately from ordinary character-scale combat. They are used for Fortress Wars and Open Abyss Battles. Strategic travel and marching between major engagements are handled narratively, off-table, or during downtime.

Shared Warfront Command

The player side uses one shared strategic board. Players collectively decide how to delegate Battlefronts or grids and who rolls the relevant Warfront dice. A player's PC location and that player's responsibility for rolling a different Battlefront are separate.

Universal Synchronized Warfront Cycle

  1. Simultaneous Movement Phase. Both sides complete legal strategic movement in the same Movement Phase. Where opposing adjacent forces could advance directly into one another, the player side has advance-declaration priority.
  2. Battle Phase. Identify every engagement created by that movement. Every active engagement resolves completely, including all Battle Exchanges and Decision Windows.
  3. No Early New Movement. A Battlefront or grid that resolves early cannot begin a new normal strategic move while another engagement from that Battle Phase is unresolved.
  4. End-of-Battle-Phase Resolution. After all engagements conclude, resolve applicable control changes, recovery for eligible withdrawn Units, eliminations, retreats, and any External Reinforcement Check that is due. Then begin the next Movement Phase.

No Zone of Control. Enemy adjacency by itself never locks movement. An engagement begins only when opposing forces legally enter or share the same Battlefront or grid.

War Assets (WA)

War Assets represent personnel, equipment, authorization, logistics, transportation, Aether resources, supplies, and the sustained resources needed to field formations.

  • The GM declares a separate Base WA allowance for each participating side before force selection and deployment.
  • The GM may restrict available Unit Catalogue options, Tiers, or Specialist formations for a specific operation.
  • There is no recurring Warfront Upkeep.
  • Unused operation-specific Base or Supplemental WA is not banked, transferred, or refunded.
  • Only the player-controlled side may buy Supplemental WA with Kinah.

Battle Exchanges and Decision Windows

Each committed attacking combat Unit rolls one ATK die. Each committed defending combat Unit rolls one DEF die. A successful DEF roll does not cancel an attacking Hit; it creates a separate Hit against the attacking force. Resolve Hits, END reduction, eliminations, and applicable effects before opening the Decision Window.

Commitment

At the beginning of a contested Battlefront or grid engagement, each side chooses which eligible Units are committed. Other eligible Units may be held back. Once the Battle Exchange begins, no Unit may reinforce, pull back, redeploy, or retreat during that exchange.

DecisionEffect
ContinueFight another Battle Exchange with the surviving committed force.
ReinforceAdd eligible held-back or legally reinforcing Units before the next Battle Exchange.
Pull BackRemove selected surviving committed Units from the next exchange.
RetreatWithdraw according to the scenario's retreat rules.

Who declares first in a Decision Window is determined by GM discretion.

Decision-Window Reinforcement Movement

  • An eligible Unit may move exactly 1 connected active Battlefront/grid during a Decision Window to reinforce that engagement.
  • This 1-grid limit also applies to Aerial Units during a Decision Window; their 2-grid allowance applies only in the normal Movement Phase.
  • A reinforcing Unit joins the next Battle Exchange and cannot chain multiple Decision-Window reinforcement moves during the same Battle Phase.
  • Reinforcing Support immediately grants its legal ATK/DEF bonus for the next exchange, but must participate in one full exchange before its END-restoration ability becomes available.
  • Legal Artillery and Saboteur effects can apply to the next exchange immediately after entry.
  • Assassin exception: Assassin Specialists use their ability only at the start of an engagement, not after entering an already-active engagement during a Decision Window.

Battlefront Changes and External Reinforcements

A Battlefront change is counted per distinct strategic transition, not per Unit token. Multiple Units moving together across the same connection count as 1 change.

  • Retreating from one active Battlefront/grid to another: 1 change.
  • Lateral movement between active connected Battlefronts/grids: 1 change.
  • An Aerial Unit traversing 2 active Battlefront/grid transitions in one legal move: 2 changes.
  • Battle Exchanges and casualties never advance the counter.

Threshold: changes accumulate to 3. No more than one External Reinforcement Check can resolve per completed Battle Phase; excess changes carry forward.

1d10External Reinforcement Result
1–4Friendly reinforcements arrive.
5–8Opposing reinforcements arrive.
9–10Nothing happens.

The GM determines the size, WA-equivalent strength, Unit composition, and exact makeup of arriving external forces.

Levy Infantry

Levy represent hastily mobilized reserves, militia, auxiliaries, emergency garrison troops, and other expendable mass manpower. They remain ATK 1 / DEF 1 at every Tier; higher Tier represents formation depth and END rather than better training.

  • Cannot receive Support's +1 ATK, +1 DEF, or END restoration.
  • Cannot receive Artillery's additional Infantry Attack Die.
  • Allied Artillery can still grant Levy permission to attack Aerial Units.
  • Cannot alone capture or recapture a Major Ground Objective or change an Open Abyss grid's control; at least one surviving non-Levy Infantry Unit must be present when control changes.

Artillery

ATK 0 / DEF 2 / END 1. One Artillery may grant one additional Attack Die to one eligible attacking Infantry Unit: Light, Line, Assault, or Vanguard. Heavy, Guard, Elite, Levy, and non-Infantry Units are ineligible. One Infantry can receive at most one Artillery bonus die in the same Battle Exchange. Artillery also enables all allied Infantry in the same Battlefront/grid, including Levy, to attack Aerial Units.

Support

ATK 0 / DEF 2 / END 2.

  • A Support aura affects no more than five eligible allied Units in the same Battlefront during a Battle Exchange.
  • Multiple Support Units do not stack the bonus on the same Unit and do not expand the five-Unit aura limit.
  • When attacking, eligible allied Units gain +1 ATK; when defending, they gain +1 DEF.
  • Support does not increase Artillery ATK or Siege SIEGE.
  • After casualties, each surviving Support that already participated in the engagement may restore 1 END to one surviving damaged eligible Unit in that Battlefront during the following Decision Window.
  • Levy Infantry are not eligible for any Support bonus or END restoration.

Player Character Warfront Participation

Each participating PC uses one fixed d6: ATK 3 while attacking and DEF 3 while defending.

  • PCs are not Warfront Units, have no Tier or END, and cost no WA.
  • PC ATK/DEF are not modified by Support, Artillery, Unit effects, class features, equipment, spells, Wings, feats, or other character-scale mechanics unless a rule explicitly names PCs.
  • Ordinary Warfront Hits cannot target or be assigned to PCs, and Assassin Specialists cannot target PCs.
  • PCs cannot capture Major Ground Objectives or Open Abyss grids.
  • PCs may target Aerial Units directly without Artillery, but do not grant allied Infantry anti-Aerial capability.
  • At least one allied Warfront Unit must normally accompany PCs in an active Battlefront/grid.
  • At the start of the overall battle, each PC declares Ground or Aerial assignment; this determines that PC's Warfront movement mode for the battle.
  • Daeva Point and Ultimate Exclusion: PCs neither gain nor spend DP and cannot activate Ultimates anywhere within a Warfront operation. Existing DP is retained but inaccessible until the Warfront ends.

Universal END Recovery

  • Decision Window Recovery: a surviving Unit that remains committed and will continue into the next exchange regains 1 END, up to maximum.
  • A Unit that Pulls Back, Retreats, or otherwise leaves during that Decision Window does not receive that immediate recovery.
  • Post-Battle-Phase Recovery: eligible withdrawn Units recover only after the entire Battle Phase across the board concludes, subject to scenario location restrictions.
  • Support restoration is separate from normal END recovery.
  • Inter-Battle Reset: after a complete separate Warfront battle ends, every surviving Unit returns to maximum END. END loss never carries between separate Fortress Wars or Open Abyss Battles.

Fortress Wars

Fortress Classes

FortressMain GatesRampart Requirement
Standard2Each Gate requires both Ramparts connected to that Gate.
Major1All four Ramparts must be captured before the single Main Gate may be contested.

Both use the same strategic depth: Outer Approach → Inner Approach → Rampart → Main Gate → Inner Fortress → Aether Controller → Fortress Core.

Directional Exterior Structure

Each Fortress has North, East, South, and West exterior sectors. Each sector contains an Outer Approach, Inner Approach, and Rampart. Units may move only across connections shown by the Fortress board. Captured Battlefronts do not require a standing garrison; control persists until enemy Infantry recaptures them.

Standard Fortress Topology

  • South + West Ramparts connect to one Main Gate.
  • North + East Ramparts connect to the other Main Gate.
  • Both Ramparts linked to a specific Gate must be captured before that Gate may be contested.
  • The two Gate routes progress independently and converge into the Inner Fortress.

Major Fortress Topology

  • Exactly one Main Gate.
  • North, East, South, and West Ramparts all connect to it.
  • All four Ramparts must be captured before the Gate may be contested.

Main-Gate Unlock Timing

When the final required Rampart is captured, its Gate becomes accessible immediately. Until a still-uncaptured Gate is actually captured, defenders can close access again by recapturing a prerequisite Rampart. Once a Main Gate itself has been captured, later loss of an exterior prerequisite Rampart does not relock that captured Gate.

Fortress Aerial Movement

  • Outside the Main Gate, Aerial Units may move up to 2 connected Battlefront levels per normal Movement Phase.
  • Inside the Main Gate, they revert to normal 1-Battlefront movement.
  • An Aerial Unit cannot cross an uncontrolled intermediate Battlefront to penetrate deeper.
  • A legal two-transition move counts as two changes for the External Reinforcement counter.

At 0 FP a Fortification is Breached. Siege attacks Fortification Points and cannot proceed beyond the Main Gate.

  • Engineer: defense-only, Repair Capacity 3 for the operation; one successful repair restores 1 FP and uses 1 Repair. After the third successful repair the Engineer is expended.
  • Engineer cap: Standard Fortress maximum 2 Engineers for the entire war; Major Fortress maximum 4. Eliminated or expended Engineers still count against the operation-wide cap unless a scenario explicitly grants an exception.
  • A single Fortification can receive at most 1 FP of Engineer restoration at the same end-of-turn timing.
  • Saboteur: improves one Siege attack by +1 SIEGE and is then expended.
  • Assassin: Fortress Wars allow at most three Assassin uses per side for the entire Fortress War.

Fortress External Reinforcements

Fortress external reinforcements enter from outside. Roll 1d4: 1 North, 2 East, 3 South, 4 West. They cannot bypass an enemy-controlled exterior Battlefront to appear deeper in the Fortress. The first three Fortress reinforcement checks use the normal 1d10 table; after the third Fortress reinforcement check, later qualifying checks automatically reinforce the Fortress defender.

Aether Controller and Fortress Core

The Aether Controller is a normal Major Ground Objective and requires at least one surviving attacking Infantry Unit to capture. The Fortress Core is inaccessible while the Aether Controller remains defender-controlled.

Every active Fortress Core has a Brigadier-General. The Core uses a two-phase procedure: a final Warfront-scale defense followed, when the required PCs are physically present, by the established character-scale Brigadier-General encounter. The broader Warfront pauses during that second phase. Defeating the Brigadier-General immediately captures the Fortress and ends unresolved Warfront battles.

Player-site boundary: the exact GM-facing Brigadier-General enemy statblock and closed defender-side GM resolution table are intentionally not published here.

Fortress Retreat and End Conditions

  • Surviving defending Units may escape off-map and remain in their Force Pool.
  • If the attacking side declares overall retreat, the Fortress War ends.
  • After the Warfront ends, pursuit, surrender, capture, escape, resistance, and campaign consequences are narrative/campaign material.
  • Once the Brigadier-General is defeated, unresolved Battlefront engagements end immediately.

Open Abyss Battles

Battlefield Structure

An Open Abyss Battle uses two mirrored 3×3 active grid zones, one per side, for 18 active grids total. Each side has Front, Mid, and Rear rows with Left, Center, and Right positions. Each side also has one protected Reserve Line behind its Rear Line. Reserve is not a Battlefront and cannot be captured or attacked.

Victory and Deployment

  • Victory comes from removing all opposing Units or forcing the opposing side to Retreat/Surrender.
  • Reserve Units count as surviving forces.
  • If both sides completely withdraw surviving forces into Reserve, the battle ends and the GM narrates the outcome.
  • Each side deploys freely within its own 3×3 zone and may also place Units in Reserve. There is no grid capacity limit.

Grid Control

  • Only Infantry captures or recaptures enemy-controlled grids.
  • An Infantry Unit entering an empty enemy-controlled grid captures it automatically without a Battle Exchange.
  • Levy Infantry cannot change grid control by themselves; at least one surviving non-Levy Infantry must be present when control changes.
  • PCs never capture or control grids by themselves.

Open Abyss Movement

  • Most Units move 1 connected grid during the normal Movement Phase.
  • Aerial Units may move up to 2 connected grids under the Open Abyss movement rules.
  • Decision-Window reinforcement remains limited to 1 connected active grid even for Aerial Units.
  • Reserve-to-Rear movement is not a Battlefront change because Reserve is not a Battlefront.
  • Siege Units are excluded from Open Abyss Battles.
  • Assassin Specialists have no Open Abyss operation-wide cap.

Retreat, Encirclement, and Cut Off PCs

Retreating Units move exactly 1 grid backward toward their own Reserve: straight backward, backward-left, or backward-right. If an allied-controlled destination is already engaged, the retreating Units enter it but join only at that engagement's Decision Window.

When a side declares overall Retreat or Surrender, a surviving Unit escapes only if a connected route to its own Reserve remains open and is not enemy-controlled. Trapped Units with no valid route are eliminated if no PCs are present.

A PC with no valid connected route back to Reserve is Cut Off. A Cut Off PC remains in Warfront play, cannot Retreat until a route reopens, stays in the current grid, and continues to use ATK 3 / DEF 3. If the last allied Unit with a Cut Off PC is eliminated, the PC remains on the board as the explicit exception to the normal accompaniment rule. Ordinary Warfront Hits still cannot be assigned to that PC, and the PC still cannot control the grid.

Open Abyss External Reinforcements

  • Uses the universal 3-change threshold and maximum-one-check-per-completed-Battle-Phase pacing.
  • Hard maximum of 3 External Reinforcement Checks for the entire Open Abyss Battle.
  • After the third check, no further external reinforcements can arrive.
  • External reinforcements arrive in the receiving side's Reserve Line.
  • Once either side declares overall Retreat or Surrender, no further External Reinforcement Checks or arrivals occur.

Quick Battle Exchange Reference

SIMULTANEOUS MOVEMENT → IDENTIFY ENGAGEMENTS → BATTLE EXCHANGES / DECISION WINDOWS UNTIL ALL ENGAGEMENTS RESOLVE → END-OF-BATTLE-PHASE RECOVERY / REINFORCEMENT CHECK → NEXT MOVEMENT PHASE
  • Attacker rolls one ATK die per committed combat Unit.
  • Defender rolls one DEF die per committed combat Unit.
  • Each successful die inflicts 1 Hit; DEF Hits do not cancel ATK Hits.
  • Reduce END and remove Units at 0 END.
  • After each Battle Exchange, open the Decision Window.
  • Units staying committed for the next exchange regain 1 END; Units leaving do not.
  • After all engagements on the board conclude, resolve eligible post-Battle-Phase recovery and at most one External Reinforcement Check if the accumulated counter is at least 3.
  • Apply scenario-specific control, movement, retreat, and reinforcement rules.
Scale Separation
Do not transfer ordinary AC, weapon quality, spell attack bonuses, class resources, Combat Flight Slot expenditure, or other character-scale mechanics directly into Warfront dice unless a Warfront rule explicitly says so.
