Appendix A — Character Creation Checklist

□ Choose Elyos or Asmodian.

□ Choose one of the 11 classes.

□ Assign 16, 14, 14, 12, 10, 8 among STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS and CHA.

□ Choose one Background.

□ Record class saving throws, armor, shield and weapon access.

□ Choose class skills and two Background skills.

□ Calculate HP, AC, Initiative, class Save DC, Speed and Passive Perception.

□ Record the fixed class starting weapon/implement, armor, Common Wings, and starting Kinah (500 + 250 per level above 1).

□ Choose name, appearance, wing appearance, personality, history, relationships and motivation.

Appendix B — Level-Up Checklist

Specialization Reassignment: Choose the initial path at Level 3. When gaining Levels 6, 10, and 14, either continue that path or switch to another class Specialization. Switching removes the previous path's entire feature package and replaces it with the new path's complete package through the current checkpoint. Recalculate path-dependent choices and maxima as required; reassignment restores no HP or resources. Level 14 is the final normal choice.

□ Read the full new class-level entry and specialization text.

□ Apply fixed HP gain + CON + class durability.

□ Update PB if needed.

□ Update class resource caps/dice/ranges/chassis.

□ Make ASI/Feat choice if granted.

□ Choose Empyrean Boon at Level 19.

□ Apply Spell Advancements if granted.

□ Update Flight Slots, Exploration Flight and Inventory.

□ Recalculate DCs, spell attacks, AC and passive values.

□ At Level 10, add the Daeva Point tracker and class Ultimate; use the improved values at Levels 15 and 20.

Appendix D — Conditions

Fourteen universal Conditions: Blinded, Charmed, Deafened, Frightened, Grappled, Incapacitated, Invisible, Paralyzed, Petrified, Poisoned, Prone, Restrained, Stunned, Unconscious. Hidden is not a Condition.

ConditionPlayer-Facing Effect
BlindedA Blinded creature cannot see and automatically fails Ability Checks that require sight. Attack rolls against it have Advantage, and its attack rolls have Disadvantage. It can still move normally unless another rule limits its movement, and Blinded does not inherently cause an airborne creature to fall.
CharmedA Charmed creature cannot willingly attack, damage, or directly target its charmer with a harmful ability or effect. The charmer has Advantage on appropriate social Ability Checks directed at the Charmed creature. Charmed is not total mind control: it does not create automatic obedience, betrayal, disclosure of secrets, or suicidal behavior.
DeafenedA Deafened creature cannot hear and automatically fails Ability Checks that require hearing. Deafened creates no generic combat penalty by itself, but the creature cannot benefit from an effect that explicitly requires it to hear the source unless that effect says otherwise.
FrightenedWhile the source of its fear is within Line of Sight, a Frightened creature has Disadvantage on Ability Checks and attack rolls and cannot willingly move closer to that source. Frightened imposes no general saving-throw penalty. An airborne Frightened creature may remain airborne and may move sideways or away from the source subject to normal flight rules.
GrappledA Grappled creature’s Speed becomes 0. The condition ends if the grappler becomes Incapacitated, the target is moved outside the grappler’s reach, or an explicit effect ends the Grapple. The Grappled creature may use its Action to attempt the approved escape contest. Grappled does not inherently cause Prone or falling; airborne movement follows the normal grappling and forced-movement rules.
IncapacitatedAn Incapacitated creature cannot take Actions, Bonus Actions, or Reactions and cannot maintain Concentration. Incapacitated does not itself reduce Speed to 0, cause Prone, or cause an airborne creature to fall.
InvisibleAn Invisible creature cannot normally be seen without a special sense or revealing effect. Attack rolls against it have Disadvantage and its attack rolls have Advantage when the opposing creature cannot otherwise perceive it, subject to the normal unseen-attacker and unseen-target rules. Invisible is not automatically Hidden; sound, footprints, disturbances, and other evidence can still reveal its location.
ParalyzedA Paralyzed creature is Incapacitated, its Speed is 0, and it cannot move or speak. It automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws, and attack rolls against it have Advantage. Paralyzed does not cause automatic Critical Hits. If unsupported, the creature becomes Prone; an unsupported airborne Paralyzed creature therefore falls.
PetrifiedA Petrified creature and its carried nonmagical equipment are transformed into inert solid substance. The creature is Incapacitated, its Speed is 0, it is unaware of its surroundings, it automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws, and attack rolls against it have Advantage. It has Resistance to all damage and does not age or require food, water, air, or sleep while Petrified. An airborne Petrified creature becomes Prone and falls.
PoisonedA Poisoned creature has Disadvantage on attack rolls and Ability Checks. Poisoned causes no automatic damage unless the effect that imposed it says otherwise.
ProneA Prone creature can move only by Crawling unless it stands. Standing from Prone requires a full Action and consumes no movement. Attack rolls against the Prone creature have Advantage when the attacker is within 5 feet and Disadvantage when the attacker is farther than 5 feet. An unsupported airborne creature that becomes Prone immediately falls.
RestrainedA Restrained creature’s Speed is 0. Attack rolls against it have Advantage, its attack rolls have Disadvantage, and it has Disadvantage on Dexterity saving throws. Restrained does not automatically cause falling; an airborne Daeva hovers while it can otherwise maintain flight and has Flight Slots as required.
StunnedA Stunned creature is Incapacitated, its Speed is 0, and it can speak only falteringly. It automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws, and attack rolls against it have Advantage. Stunned does not inherently cause Prone or falling; an airborne Daeva hovers while it can otherwise maintain flight and has Flight Slots as required.
UnconsciousAn Unconscious creature is Incapacitated, its Speed is 0, and it is unaware of its surroundings. It drops unsecured actively held items, automatically fails Strength and Dexterity saving throws, and attack rolls against it have Advantage. It becomes Prone unless physically supported; an unsupported airborne Unconscious creature therefore falls. Unconscious does not cause automatic Critical Hits. When Unconscious because it is Dying at 0 HP, the Dying rules govern damage and Death Saving Throws.
Airborne Reminder. Prone is the universal condition that causes an unsupported airborne creature to fall. Unconscious leads to Prone. Other Conditions do not automatically cause falling unless their text creates Prone or explicitly ends flight.Airborne Reminder. Daeva Points. Beginning at Level 10, record current DP from 0 to 100 and the name of your class Ultimate under Class Features or Universal Features. Mark the Ultimate ready at 100 DP. On a hit, return the tracker to 0. On a miss, choose Release the Power or Hold the Power: Release returns the tracker to 0 and applies the approved half-output miss result; Hold keeps the tracker at 100 but marks the Ultimate unavailable until the next dawn. After any Ultimate attempt, mark your next turn as Ultimate Recovery. During that recovery turn, use movement, flight, interaction, Help, defensive or utility options, and Reactions normally within the Universal Ultimate Recovery restrictions.
