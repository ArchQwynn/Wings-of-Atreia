29. Vision, Stealth, Traps, Locks & Theft

Vision and Light

Light states are Bright Light, Dim Light, and Darkness. Obscuration states are Lightly Obscured and Heavily Obscured. Dim Light lightly obscures; Darkness heavily obscures a creature that cannot otherwise see through it. Daeva Darkvision treats Darkness within 60 feet as Dim Light, not Bright Light.

A recommended ordinary light source has a stated Bright radius plus an equal additional Dim radius unless its own text says otherwise. Bright light does not universally prohibit Stealth. Obscurity and Invisibility do not automatically make a creature Hidden.

Cover, Line of Sight & Line of Effect

No Cover. No defensive benefit from Cover.

Half Cover. A creature or object behind meaningful partial obstruction gains +2 AC and +2 to Dexterity saving throws against effects originating from the covered direction. Intervening creatures can provide Half Cover when they materially obstruct the line.

Full Cover. Full Cover blocks the relevant Line of Effect and prevents direct targeting through that obstruction unless an explicit rule says otherwise. Three-Quarters Cover does not exist in Wings of Atreia. Cover does not stack; use the best applicable Cover. Altitude neither inherently grants nor ignores Cover.

Line of Sight and Line of Effect are distinct. Line of Sight determines whether you can see a target or point. Line of Effect determines whether an attack, spell, or effect can physically reach it. Heavy obscurement is not Cover by itself.

Stealth and Infiltration

Use scene states Undetected, Suspicious, and Alerted; these are not Conditions. Stealth must exceed Passive Perception; ties favor the observer. An active Search uses the GM-selected check, commonly Wisdom (Perception) or Intelligence (Investigation), against the relevant DC or opposing Stealth; ties favor the Searcher where opposed. Do not reroll Stealth every 5 feet; roll again only when circumstances materially change. For group Stealth, at least half the group must succeed. A failed check may create Suspicion rather than immediate full alarm when appropriate.

Distractions create an opportunity to Hide rather than automatic success. Disguises are primarily social. Invisibility is not the same as Hidden. Wings do not automatically prevent Stealth.

Traps and Non-Aether Hazards

Describe a hazard with Trigger, Detection, Avoid/Disable method, Save/Check, Effect, and Reset/Duration. Hazards may be obvious, concealed, or hidden. Detection normally uses Passive Perception, active Perception, or Investigation. Disable methods depend on the mechanism and can use Sleight of Hand, Investigation, Athletics, or another appropriate Skill. There is no mandatory Thieves’ Tools system.

Failure triggers the hazard only when the hazard says so. Damage and Conditions use existing universal rules. Forced movement is horizontal unless the hazard explicitly creates vertical displacement. Hazards normally resolve when triggered rather than taking their own Initiative turns. Repeat attempts require changed circumstances.

The correct key opens a normal lock without a check. Mechanical locks generally use Dexterity (Sleight of Hand). Unusual mechanisms can use Intelligence (Investigation). Forcing uses Strength (Athletics); destroying a lock or door uses normal object/destruction adjudication. Failure does not automatically break or trigger a lock unless its design says so. Aether locks use Aether Interaction. If a task is impossible, do not assign an absurd DC—state that it cannot be done by that method.

Theft and Pickpocket

A theft attempt requires a plausible opportunity. Taking an accessible small item uses Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) against Passive Perception and must exceed it. If the target is actively watching, oppose with active Perception; ties favor the observer. Secured items can require a different approach. Failure may create suspicion or alarm. Success means the theft was unnoticed at that moment; later discovery remains possible.

Distraction creates opportunity rather than automatic success. Help can grant +2 when genuinely meaningful. Worn armor, shields, Wings, wielded weapons, and equipped mandatory implements cannot simply be pickpocketed. Repeated attempts require changed circumstances.

30. Social Interaction, Information & Captivity

Attitudes

NPC attitudes are Friendly, Neutral, or Hostile. Roleplay comes first; a roll is used only when outcome and consequence are uncertain. Common skills include Persuasion, Deception, Intimidation, Insight, and Investigation.

Plausibility

A social check cannot force an impossible concession or make an NPC believe something fundamentally implausible without exceptional circumstances. Leverage affects what is possible and can influence DCs, but it does not automatically grant Advantage.

Interrogation

An interrogated NPC can reveal only information they actually know. Resolve one meaningful line of questioning at a time. Failure may close that approach, create resistance, or produce consequences; repeated checks need changed circumstances. Insight is not mind reading. Deception may oppose Insight when appropriate. Intimidation can succeed while still creating later consequences.

Information Gathering

Information comes from plausible sources. Common knowledge needs no roll. Use the skill that matches the approach. Rumors may be Reliable, Uncertain, or False, but the GM does not label them for the players. Independent sources and evidence can verify claims. Restricted information requires access. There is no fixed universal gathering-time formula and no check spam.

Captivity and Restraints

Physical restraints use Restrained when their actual effect warrants it. Binding an unwilling conscious creature in combat requires control first and normally an Action. Escape can use Sleight of Hand, Athletics, or Acrobatics against the restraint’s DC. A locked cell does not automatically impose Restrained. Restrained does not inherently prevent spellcasting, but implement and action requirements still matter.

Ordinary restraints do not suppress Wings, Aetheric Reconstitution, or class powers unless an explicit specialized restraint says so. Transporting prisoners uses ordinary logistics. Interrogation remains a separate social procedure.

31. Chases, Morale, Escorts & Creature Transport

Chases

EngagedImmediate reach / melee contact
Close30–60 ft
Far60–120 ft
DistantMore than 120 ft or otherwise meaningfully separated

Use normal Initiative and Actions during a chase. A meaningful movement advantage normally shifts one distance band. Obstacles use appropriate checks or saves. Attacks still use real range, cover, visibility, and weapon rules. Grappling at Engaged usually ends the running phase. A quarry escapes once it moves beyond Distant and pursuers have no meaningful contact or tracking method. Aerial chases track altitude separately and retain all flight rules. Chases do not automatically generate Exhaustion.

Morale, Retreat and Surrender

NPCs are not required to fight to 0 HP. The GM determines morale from goals and fiction rather than a universal Morale score/save. Retreat uses normal movement and chase rules and does not grant free movement or Opportunity-Attack immunity. Surrender is narrative, not a Condition; an accepted surrender can end Genuine Combat. False surrender uses normal social/awareness rules and never guarantees Surprise.

Intimidation or negotiation can induce surrender only when plausible. Bosses and major NPCs follow their motivations. Retreat or surrender can satisfy an encounter objective where appropriate.

NPC Allies and Escorts

NPC allies use NPC statblocks rather than automatically using full PC classes. The GM controls them. Combat-capable allies roll Initiative; identical minor allies may share an Initiative when convenient. Simple verbal directions are free; complicated battlefield instructions may require an Action. Allies use normal healing, consumables, inventory, and Help rules and do not provide unlimited storage or resources.

NPC Advancement. Allied and other NPCs do not automatically gain levels or improve when the player characters do. NPC advancement is entirely GM-determined according to story role, training, campaign events, and intended usefulness. The GM may improve an NPC statblock, replace it with a stronger version, or leave it unchanged.

Daeva NPC reconstitution requires the same story prerequisites when relevant. Minor NPCs make Death Saves only when their survival is important to the story. NPC allies do not alter milestone advancement or create XP.

Loot, Rewards, Missions & Selling

Loot Procedure

  1. Secure the area.
  2. Identify obvious possessions and scene loot.
  3. Search for hidden items only when there is a meaningful possibility of something concealed.
  4. Recover items into personal inventory or appropriate external storage.
  5. Players distribute recovered goods by agreement.
  6. Apply quest, faction, or story rewards separately.

There are no universal random loot tables. Enemies drop only what they actually possess. Equipment keeps its real quality. Fired ammunition cannot be recovered; unused ammunition can be looted. Common gear can serve as replacement gear, storage stock, sale goods, or an Aether-enhancement base. Aether gear is not a random generic drop but can be recovered from an NPC who actually owned it. Ordinary Wings are usually cosmetic loot unless explicitly mechanical.

Kinah has no per-enemy formula. Unused consumables are lootable when actually present. Refined Aether is usually gathered from sources but already-refined caches are strategic loot.

Reward Scale

The GM may frame rewards as Routine, Mission, Major, or Story rewards. These are guidance categories rather than mandatory random tables or XP values.

Missions

A mission defines its Objective, Conditions, Outcome, and Reward. The primary objective should be clear; optional objectives may exist. Unless stated, the result matters more than a prescribed method. Failure changes the world rather than resetting the mission. Partial outcomes are allowed. Mission completion contributes to milestone judgment but does not automatically grant a level. Objectives can change dynamically as the fiction changes.

Selling and Disposal

Selling requires a plausible buyer. The GM determines value; there is no universal resale percentage. Consider quality, condition, rarity, local demand, location, faction access, and ownership. The ability to sell an item does not imply that category is ordinarily purchasable.

Aether gear can be sold but does not return its Refined Aether as a universal salvage rule. Legendary items have no ordinary market price. Cosmetic Wings can be sold for GM-determined value. Damaged items sell at a discount; Destroyed items are salvage at best. Stolen goods may create consequences. There is no repeated buyer-reroll loop or assumption of infinite merchant liquidity.

Bosses and Lords. Every ordinary Boss-rank monster uses Boss Actions as defined in its statblock. Empyrean and Dragon Lords use Lord Actions. Boss Action and Lord Action are distinct official Wings of Atreia mechanical terms; Legendary Action is not.
