Beginner Guide

You do not need to memorize the entire handbook before your first session. This page gives you the minimum player-facing framework needed to create a Daeva, understand a normal turn, and know where to look when a rule comes up.

This guide uses the Wings of Atreia Player Handbook v3.13 — CHARACTER_MANAGER_SYNC character-management workflow. It summarizes and links to governing player rules; when a website summary and the full current rules differ, use the full current rule.

1. What Kind of Game Is This?

Wings of Atreia is a heroic fantasy tabletop roleplaying game about Daevas: ageless winged heroes who explore, fight, negotiate, survive, and shape a world divided by the Abyss. Every standard player character begins play as a Daeva.

You control one Daeva. You decide what that character attempts, describe how they act and respond, work with the party, and roll when an outcome is both uncertain and meaningful. Characters advance from Level 1 to Level 20 in one class; multiclassing is not used.

2. The Core d20 Rule

When a meaningful outcome is uncertain:

Roll 1d20 + the relevant ability modifier.
Add Proficiency Bonus when you are proficient in the relevant skill, weapon, or saving throw, or when a rule explicitly tells you to add it. Meet or exceed the target DC to succeed.

You do not roll simply because an action was described. The GM calls for a roll when the result genuinely matters and is uncertain.

3. What You Track

During play, a Daeva commonly tracks the following through the Wings of Atreia Character Manager:

  • Hit Points (HP)
  • Flight Slots
  • Inventory and equipment
  • Your class resource
  • Limited-use features
  • Spell frequencies, if applicable
  • Daeva Points from Level 10 onward
  • Conditions and other active effects

4. Create Your First Daeva

  1. Choose Elyos or Asmodian. The factions differ culturally, historically, politically, visually, and territorially, but use the same universal Daeva mechanical foundation.
  2. Choose one of the 11 classes.
  3. Assign 16, 14, 14, 12, 10, 8 among STR, DEX, CON, INT, WIS, and CHA.
  4. Choose one Background.
  5. Enter your class saving throws and your armor, shield, and weapon access in the Character Manager.
  6. Choose your class skills and two Background skills.
  7. Calculate HP, AC, Initiative, class Save DC, Speed, Passive Perception, and Passive Investigation.
  8. Enter your fixed class starting weapon or implement, armor, Common Wings, and starting Kinah in the Character Manager.
  9. Choose your name, appearance, wing appearance, personality, history, relationships, and motivation.
Specializations begin at Level 3. At Levels 6, 10, and 14, you may continue your current Specialization or replace it with another available to your class. Changing paths replaces the entire old Specialization package through that checkpoint; you never mix features from two Specializations. Level 14 is the final normal reassignment checkpoint. 5. Elyos and Asmodians

All player characters are Daevas. Elyos are the Humans and Daevas of Elysea, while Asmodians are the Humans and Daevas of Asmodae. Choosing a faction establishes culture, homeland, language, and political identity; it does not grant inherent combat superiority.

Ascension does not erase your former Human life. Your character may still have family, relationships, memories, duties, and a personal history from before becoming a Daeva.

6. Your Turn in Combat

Genuine combat uses Initiative and rounds. One round represents approximately six seconds, and each participant normally takes one turn in Initiative order.

Action

Normally one per turn. Attacking, many spells, Dash, Disengage, Dodge, Hide, Search, Use Object, Grapple, Shove, and other features may use it.

Movement

Move up to your available movement, subject to terrain, conditions, and flight rules.

Bonus Action

Up to one, but only when a feature actually grants something that uses a Bonus Action.

Reaction

Normally one per round, refreshing at the start of your turn unless an explicit class rule says otherwise.

There is also one free simple object interaction per turn. Wings of Atreia has no universal Action-to-Bonus-Action conversion, and unused movement or Actions are not stored for later turns.

7. Flight Is a Core Player System

Your wings are not decorative. Daevas have both exploration-flight rules and Combat Flight Slots. Flight has its own movement, altitude, gliding, falling, and aerial-combat procedures, so read the Flight page before a session where aerial movement is likely to matter.

Read Flight Rules

8. If You Are a Spellcaster

The finalized spellcasting classes are Sorcerer, Spiritmaster, Cleric, Chanter, and Songweaver. Wings of Atreia has no spell slots and no cantrip category. Each spell is individually At-Will, Cooldown-based, Short-Rest limited, or Long-Rest limited.

Spells are class-granted abilities. They unlock through character level and, when applicable, the immediately preceding spell in a linear prerequisite chain. Spell Advancement improves eligible unlocked spells.

9. Daeva Points and Ultimates

Beginning at Level 10, you track Daeva Points from 0 to 100 and gain your class Ultimate Ability. At 100 DP, your Ultimate is ready. Ultimate resolution includes its own hit/miss procedure and a mandatory Ultimate Recovery turn after an Ultimate attempt.

You do not need to learn this system at Level 1. Return to it when your character reaches Level 10.

11. First-Session Checklist

  • Your character is created and saved in the Character Manager, and your class page is easy to access.
  • You know your AC, maximum HP, Speed, Initiative, and main attack or spell values.
  • You know what your class resource is and how your Level 1 features use it.
  • Your starting weapon or implement, armor, Common Wings, and Kinah are current in the Character Manager.
  • If you cast spells, you know which starting spells you have and their frequencies.
  • You know how many Combat Flight Slots you currently have.
  • You know which Background skills you chose.
