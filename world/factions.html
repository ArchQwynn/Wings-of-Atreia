Player Guide

Choose Your Faction

Elyos and Asmodians are two peoples born from the same shattered world. Your faction shapes homeland, culture, institutions, loyalties, and the lens through which your Daeva first understands the Abyss War—but it does not make one side mechanically superior.

One World, Two Civilizations

Before the Cataclysm, the people of Atreia were not divided into Elyos and Asmodians. The destruction of the Tower separated the surviving peoples into Elysea and Asmodae, and centuries of isolation, hardship, reconstruction, and conflict produced two distinct civilizations. Their Seraphim and Shedim identities belong to that post-Cataclysm history rather than to ancient pre-existing orders.

At campaign start, the two factions are bitter military rivals in the Abyss, but neither faction receives racial or faction ability-score bonuses. The choice is primarily one of identity, culture, home, and allegiance. Players may freely choose different factions within the same group; the campaign can create reasons for Elyos and Asmodian Daevas to cooperate when larger threats demand it.

What Your Character May Look Like

Faction has a strong visual identity in Wings of Atreia, but it is not meant to make every member of a faction look identical. Players are free to vary height, build, facial features, hairstyle, age of appearance, clothing, scars, and other personal details within the broad visual identity of their people. These appearance choices are descriptive only and do not change character statistics or class access.

Elyos Appearance

Elyos are generally humanlike in proportion and appearance, with broad freedom in facial features, body type, hairstyle, and complexion. Skin tones may range from very fair through tan and deeper natural complexions, while eye colors can vary from ordinary human hues to brighter fantasy tones that suit the character concept. Hair may likewise use natural or stylized colors, allowing individual Elyos to look distinct without changing their faction identity.

Wings: An Elyos Daeva manifests white wings. The exact shade may vary visually from snow-white to ivory or pale silver-white, but the wings remain recognizably white rather than adopting another faction color. This is one of the clearest visual signs of an Elyos Daeva when their wings are revealed.

Asmodian Appearance

Asmodians remain recognizably humanoid, but their appearance reflects the harsher world of Asmodae. Skin tones may range from pale or ashen complexions through cool gray, blue-gray, and darker cool-toned complexions, with wide freedom in facial features, build, hairstyle, and hair color. Their eyes need not constantly glow, but when the characteristic Asmodian Daeva glow is active, the eyes shine red.

Asmodians also possess clawed hands and taloned toes. They do not have elongated canine teeth in Wings of Atreia canon. Wings: an Asmodian Daeva manifests black wings, which may visually range from deep black to charcoal or blue-black while remaining unmistakably black as the faction's wing color.

Visual identity, not a restriction: The descriptions above establish the recognizable WoA faction traits. Within them, players should still have substantial freedom to design a Daeva who looks individual rather than like a standardized faction template.

Elyos

Homeland: Elysea
Capital: Sanctum
Ruling tradition: Seraphim Lords
Abyss foothold: Terminon
Balaurean frontier: Inggison
Wings: White

Elyos civilization developed in the southern half of shattered Atreia. Sanctum stands as its monumental capital, while regions such as fertile Poeta, defensive Verteron, arid Eltnen, mountainous Heiron, scorched Theobomos, cultivated Oriel, and the recognized region of Iluma show that Elysea is far more varied than a single idealized landscape.

To an Elyos character, service as a Daeva can mean defending communities, answering institutions centered on Sanctum, operating under military authority, or carrying the expectations of a culture shaped by the Seraphim Lords. The widespread Elyos belief that their Lords are the chosen of Aion is a cultural and religious tradition of Elysea, not an objective mechanical advantage over the opposing faction.

What It Feels Like to Play Elyos

Asmodians

Homeland: Asmodae
Capital: Pandaemonium
Ruling tradition: Shedim Lords
Abyss foothold: Primum
Balaurean frontier: Gelkmaros
Wings: Black

Asmodian civilization developed in the northern half of shattered Atreia under harsher environmental conditions. Pandaemonium stands as its dark, monumental capital, while Ishalgen, Altgard, Morheim, frozen Beluslan, Brusthonin, cultivated Pernon, and the recognized region of Norsvold show a culture built across difficult and varied terrain.

To an Asmodian character, being a Daeva can mean protecting hard-won settlements, serving institutions centered on Pandaemonium, operating through military structures, or carrying the legacy of a people whose identity was forged through survival and reconstruction. Shedim traditions shape Asmodian civilization culturally and institutionally, but they do not grant faction-exclusive mechanical superiority.

What It Feels Like to Play Asmodian

Playing an Elyos Daeva

An Elyos character begins with familiarity with Elysea and the social world surrounding Sanctum. Their personal outlook does not have to match every institution or every other Elyos: a character may be dutiful, skeptical, ambitious, compassionate, proud, pragmatic, or deeply conflicted about the war. Faction gives you a starting culture, not a mandatory personality.

Good character questions include: What part of Elysea feels like home? What does your character believe about the Seraphim Lords? Have they ever met an Asmodian before the campaign? What would make them cooperate with one? What ordinary people or places are they determined to protect?

Playing an Asmodian Daeva

An Asmodian character begins with familiarity with Asmodae and the social world surrounding Pandaemonium. As with Elyos, there is no single required Asmodian personality: your character can embrace or question inherited traditions, military expectations, rivalries, and assumptions about the opposing faction. The harsh history of Asmodae informs the culture without dictating every individual.

Good character questions include: Which region of Asmodae shaped your character most? What do they believe about the Shedim Lords? What stories were they taught about Elyos? What could persuade them to trust an Elyos Daeva? What does survival mean to them beyond simply winning battles?

What Faction Changes—and What It Does Not

Faction Does ChangeYour homeland, cultural background, political loyalties, starting social context, faction institutions, and the side from which you first experience the Abyss War.
Faction Does Not ChangeYour ability-score array, class access, class mechanics, maximum ability scores, or the fundamental power ceiling of your Daeva. Elyos and Asmodians use the same player mechanics.

Classes are available independent of faction. Cultural associations and Lord patronage can influence roleplay and institutions, but they do not lock a class to one side.
