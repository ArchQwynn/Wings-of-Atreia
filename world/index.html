Current Campaign Start: The Present Age

The default Wings of Atreia campaign begins circa 1,000 AC, during the long Abyss War. Player characters begin as newly Ascended Daevas who have only recently received their Wings and entered an immortal life of service, discovery, and conflict. Elyos and Asmodians remain bitter rivals, with Terminon and Primum serving as their enduring footholds in Reshanta, while the Balaur stand as a powerful third force rather than a distant legend. Beyond the familiar realms, the known Balaurean frontier has opened through Inggison, Gelkmaros, and Silentera Canyon, but Greater Balaurea remains largely unknown.

Your Daeva begins with a faction, a homeland, and reasons to distrust the opposing side, but the world does not promise that every enemy will remain simple. Early adventures can place Elyos and Asmodians against the same Balaur threat, and circumstances may force rivals to decide whether survival matters more than inherited hatred. Lepharist revolutionaries are active in this age and may become part of the characters’ first encounters with the wider conflict, but their deeper motives are something players discover through play. The starting world is therefore a snapshot, not a fixed future: fortresses can change hands, alliances can form or collapse, and the actions of player characters can become part of Atreia’s history.

The Twelve Empyrean Lords

The Empyrean Lords are extraordinary Daevas elevated into Lordhood whose names stand at the heart of Atreia’s history. After the Cataclysm, the surviving rulers became associated with the Seraphim tradition of Elysea and the Shedim tradition of Asmodae, while the two Tower Guardians remain central to the story of the world’s division. To player characters, these Lords are not merely distant mythology: their institutions, teachings, military orders, laws, temples, and legacies shape the societies in which Daevas live.

Seraphim Lords of Elysea

Ariel — Lady of Light
Kaisinel — Lord of Illusion
Nezekan — Lord of Justice
Vaizel — Lord of Freedom
Yustiel — Lady of Life

These Lords stand among the highest powers recognized in Elyos civilization. Their influence reaches player characters through Sanctum, Elyos institutions, military service, cultural traditions, and the expectations placed upon Daevas. A player may serve organizations shaped by a Lord’s legacy, receive missions from authorities acting in a Lord’s name, or eventually become important enough for the concerns of the Lords themselves to matter.

Shedim Lords of Asmodae

Azphel — Lord of Shadow
Lumiel — Lady of Wisdom
Marchutan — Lord of Fate
Triniel — Lady of Death
Zikel — Lord of Destruction

These Lords stand among the highest powers recognized in Asmodian civilization. Their influence is felt through Pandaemonium, Archon service, Asmodian institutions, cultural traditions, and the hard-won identity of Asmodae. Player characters may encounter their authority through commanders, temples, missions, judgments, and eventually events important enough to draw the attention of the Lords themselves.

The Tower Guardians

Israphel — Lord of Space
Siel — Lady of Time

Israphel and Siel are remembered as the two Guardians of the Tower of Eternity and are counted among the Twelve Empyrean Lords of Atreia’s great history. Their roles are different from those of the post-Cataclysm faction rulers: they belong to the story of the Tower, the Cataclysm, and the sacrifice that shaped the divided world. For beginning players, their importance is primarily historical, and deeper truths concerning their legacy are meant to be discovered through the campaign rather than explained in advance.

The Six Dragon Lords

The Dragon Lords are the supreme powers of the Balaur and represent threats on a scale far beyond ordinary soldiers or monsters. Their names are known through history, warfare, rumor, and the devastation associated with Balaur dominion. Player characters are far more likely to encounter their armies, agents, fortresses, schemes, and consequences before ever standing before a Dragon Lord directly.

Fregion — Dragon Lord of Fire

Fregion is remembered as the first Dragon Lord and the beginning of the transformation that produced the Balaur order. To players, his name represents the oldest and greatest shadow behind Balaur power. Any direct involvement with Fregion belongs to the highest scale of Daeva conflict rather than ordinary adventuring.

Meslamtaeda — Dragon Lord of Rage and Destruction

Meslamtaeda embodies overwhelming destructive power among the Dragon Lords. Players should understand his influence through the fear, devastation, and military consequences associated with forces acting beneath such a ruler. Confronting power of this magnitude is not the same as facing a conventional battlefield commander.

Ereshkigal — Dragon Lord of Ice and Justice

Ereshkigal is one of the great Balaur powers whose name is bound to the long struggle surrounding the Abyss. For players, she represents a strategic power capable of shaping entire theaters of war rather than merely individual encounters. Her deeper circumstances in the Present Age are intentionally left for discovery during play.

Beritra — Dragon Lord of Darkness

Beritra is a Dragon Lord whose power extends through Balaur forces, plans, and conflicts reaching beyond any single region. Player characters may experience that influence through enemies and events long before understanding the full design behind them. His true objectives and present movements are matters for the campaign to reveal.

Tiamat — Dragon Lord of Earth and Death

Tiamat commands power associated with conquest, warfare, and Balaur dominion. To beginning Daevas, her name belongs to the class of enemies capable of moving armies and changing the fate of territories. What she seeks in the Present Age—and how her struggles with other Balaur powers affect Atreia—should emerge through play.

Apsu — Dragon Lord of Wind

Apsu is counted among the Six Dragon Lords and remains part of the history and power structure players inherit at the start of the campaign. Public knowledge does not provide beginning characters with a complete account of his present circumstances. His deeper role is therefore deliberately left unexplained here so that the campaign can reveal it at the proper time.

What the Lords mean to your character: Empyrean Lords can stand behind the institutions, duties, beliefs, and authorities that shape a Daeva’s life, while Dragon Lords define the scale of the Balaur threat looming over both factions. Neither group should be reduced to a list of quest-givers or boss fights. As the campaign grows, the players’ relationship to these powers can change from distant history and military consequence to direct choices capable of affecting Atreia itself.

Elysea

Elysea forms the southern half of shattered Atreia and is the homeland of the Elyos. Its regions range from fertile countryside and monumental cities to arid badlands, mountain forests, devastated islands, and places where ancient history remains close to the surface.

Sanctum

Sanctum is the monumental floating capital of Elysea, suspended above Oriel and visible as one of the great symbols of Elyos civilization. Its luminous plazas, temples, government halls, Legion offices, archives, and scholarly institutions make it a center of civic life as much as military power. The city was raised during the long southern reconstruction after the Cataclysm rather than surviving unchanged from ancient Atreia. Public ceremony, learning, art, diplomacy, and service all contribute to the capital’s cultivated identity.

For Elyos Daevas, Sanctum is where victories become policy, discoveries enter archives, and service to Elysea can draw the attention of powerful institutions. Most civic areas are orderly and accessible, while military compounds, temple spaces, and restricted records remain controlled. Adventures here tend toward investigation, diplomacy, political consequences, research, or threats hidden beneath the security of the capital. Sanctum represents what Elyos society believes it rebuilt from the ruin of the old world.

Oriel

Oriel is the cultivated residential sanctuary directly beneath Sanctum on the same greater floating geography, not a distant island of its own. Gardens, estates, homes, workshops, and carefully maintained public spaces give it a gentler character than the monumental capital overhead. It reflects the domestic side of Elyos civilization: households, artistry, hospitality, and the patient work of maintaining a beautiful place to live. Its closeness to Sanctum ties everyday life directly to the political and religious heart of Elysea.

Daevas who come to Oriel encounter the lives that continue beyond military service—families, artisans, residents, visitors, and generations shaped by an immortal society. The region can host social stories, community problems, personal obligations, celebrations, quiet investigations, and consequences that feel very different from fortress warfare. Its tranquility should not make it meaningless; Oriel helps show players what Elyos characters are actually protecting. Looking upward toward Sanctum also reinforces the intimate relationship between capital and home.

Poeta

Poeta is a fertile rural region of Elysea, defined by broad plains, farms, forests, and clear lakes. Akarios Village stands among a landscape that includes Cliona Lake, Daminu Forest, Kabarah Strip Mine, and Timolia Mine. Compared with the grandeur of Sanctum, Poeta feels close to the land and to the ordinary rhythms of food, work, family, and local community. Its pastoral appearance makes it one of the clearest expressions of the hospitable southern environment.

For many Elyos, Poeta represents homeland in its most immediate form: fields worth tending, communities worth defending, and wilderness that still holds mysteries of its own. Daevas may be called to protect travelers, investigate trouble around mines and forests, assist isolated settlements, or deal with dangers that ordinary residents cannot face alone. The region works especially well for adventures where local stakes matter before the campaign expands into larger wars. Poeta reminds heroes that the fate of Atreia is ultimately measured in the lives that survive its conflicts.

Verteron

Verteron is a temperate defensive frontier where plains, coastline, woodland, roads, and old ruins meet. Verteron Citadel anchors the region alongside Tolbas Village, Verteron Observatory, Cantas Coast, Tursin Garrison, Zumion Ruins, and the road leading toward Eltnen. Its settled routes are never entirely removed from military vigilance, giving the region the feel of civilization pressed against contested ground. Ancient remains and active defenses stand side by side across the landscape.

Service in Verteron emphasizes readiness, patrols, escort duty, reconnaissance, and the defense of communities that live close to danger. Daevas can move from a village problem to a ruined site or hostile position without leaving the region’s broader defensive story. The coexistence of roads, settlements, ruins, and enemy activity makes Verteron a natural place for exploration and field missions. It is a reminder that Elysea’s beauty survives partly because someone is always watching its frontiers.

Eltnen

Eltnen is Elysea’s distinctly arid region, a landscape of dry badlands, exposed stone, scarce water, and scattered greener pockets where life gathers. Its harsher terrain contrasts sharply with Poeta’s fertility and demonstrates that the southern realm is not uniformly lush. Settlements and travelers must respect distance, water, weather, and the limits imposed by the land. Ruins and isolated routes can feel especially exposed beneath the bright Elysean sky.

Life in Eltnen is shaped by adaptation rather than abundance. Daevas operating here may protect water access, cross dangerous open country, investigate remote ruins, escort travelers, or respond to threats that exploit the region’s isolation. Communities that endure here have practical knowledge of the terrain and should feel capable rather than helpless. Eltnen gives Elyos adventures a frontier defined as much by environment and distance as by warfare.

Heiron

Heiron is a forested and mountainous region of Elysea, rich in waterways, steep terrain, old roads, and places where wilderness can conceal history. Its dense landscapes and changes in elevation distinguish it from both arid Eltnen and rural Poeta. The region carries a strong sense of vigilance and memory, with civilization existing beside terrain that can quickly become difficult or dangerous. Water, forest, and stone shape both travel and settlement.

For Daevas, Heiron favors scouting, pursuit, defense, historical investigation, and expeditions into places where the landscape can hide ruins or hostile movement. Roads and communities remain important, but control of a route does not mean control of everything beyond it. The region can support stories about protecting settlements while uncovering traces of earlier ages. Heiron’s beauty is therefore inseparable from the need to remain watchful.

Theobomos

Theobomos is a scorched and devastated eastern island whose landscape bears wounds that ordinary weather and time have not erased. Ruins, archaeological sites, burned terrain, and supernatural scars make it one of Elysea’s clearest reminders that the wars of Atreia can permanently change the land. The region is not simply empty destruction; expeditions, researchers, soldiers, and those reclaiming knowledge still have reasons to enter it. Its damaged environment gives every surviving structure and recovered record unusual weight.

Daevas come to Theobomos to investigate the past, secure dangerous sites, recover history, and confront threats that flourish in devastated ground. Archaeology here is rarely a quiet academic exercise, because ancient remains can intersect with hostile forces, unstable conditions, and the consequences of old battles. The region supports adventures that make history physically present rather than something confined to books. For Elyos characters, Theobomos is both a warning and a challenge: devastation happened here, yet the story of the land is not finished.

Iluma

Iluma is a recognized region of Elysea associated with sacred archaeology and the recovery of ancient memory. It belongs to the wider WoA Gazetteer even though it is outside the first-wave continental atlas, so its exact map presentation is intentionally deferred. The region is best understood as a place where the remains of earlier Atreia carry religious, historical, and cultural importance. Its identity should come from discovery and reverence rather than from importing later events as predetermined canon.

For player characters, Iluma offers expeditions centered on ancient sites, contested interpretations of history, relics, and questions about what the people of the present choose to preserve. Scholars, religious authorities, soldiers, and explorers may all value the same discovery for very different reasons. The region is well suited to stories where knowledge itself has political or spiritual consequences. Because its future is not locked to later source continuity, discoveries in Iluma can belong to the campaign the players actually create.

Asmodae

Asmodae forms the northern half of shattered Atreia and is the homeland of the Asmodians. Perpetual night and colder conditions shaped its civilization, but the realm contains functioning cities, frontiers, varied mountains, frozen country, damaged lands under reclamation, and places of deep cultural memory.

Pandaemonium

Pandaemonium is the monumental capital of Asmodae, a great center of government, military organization, scholarship, religion, trade, and culture beneath the northern darkness. It is a functioning city rather than a fortress pretending to be one, with institutions and households that reflect centuries of Asmodian reconstruction. Pernon occupies the southern portion of the same island and serves as the capital’s cultivated residential counterpart. The city embodies the Asmodian conviction that civilization can flourish in a world shaped by hardship.

Archons and other Daevas may come to Pandaemonium for orders, research, hearings, ceremonies, trade, institutional disputes, or the consequences of victories won elsewhere. Restricted military and sacred spaces coexist with ordinary civic life, giving the GM room for intrigue without making every visit a crisis. The capital should display craftsmanship, warmth, discipline, and beauty adapted to perpetual night rather than treating darkness as corruption. Pandaemonium is where Asmodae turns endurance into culture, law, memory, and ambition.

Pernon

Pernon is the cultivated residential counterpart to Pandaemonium, occupying the southern portion of the same island rather than existing as a distant independent landmass. Homes, gardens, workshops, gathering places, and carefully maintained spaces show a quieter expression of Asmodian life. Its beauty is adapted to darkness, emphasizing shelter, craft, community, and the visual character of the northern realm. The region gives domestic life a place beside the monumental institutions of the capital.

For Daevas, Pernon can make centuries of Asmodian history personal through households, neighbors, artisans, celebrations, and obligations that persist between campaigns. Adventures here may involve community concerns, social investigation, family ties, or consequences carried home from the front. It is also an important reminder that Asmodians do not exist only as soldiers in an endless war. Pernon shows what endurance is meant to preserve: a life worth returning to.

Ishalgen

Ishalgen is a rugged island frontier whose terrain and isolated communities foster a strong sense of independence. Uneven ground, wilderness, exposed routes, and local settlements make the region feel removed from the monumental scale of Pandaemonium. Its people are accustomed to solving practical problems with limited support and to respecting dangers beyond settled ground. The landscape reflects the northern world without reducing it to a frozen wasteland.

Daevas in Ishalgen may be asked to protect communities, cross difficult terrain, investigate remote disturbances, or deal with threats that cannot wait for distant authorities. Local knowledge and self-reliance matter here, making cooperation with residents as important as raw power. The region is well suited to grounded adventures where a small settlement can matter as much as a fortress objective. Ishalgen expresses Asmodian resilience through independence rather than through grand military spectacle.

Altgard

Altgard is a settled military frontier where Asmodian communities, roads, defenses, and patrol routes exist in close relationship with contested territory. Unlike the capital, its identity is shaped by the expectation that danger may arrive from beyond the next defended line. Soldiers and civilians share the same landscape, creating a region where preparedness is part of ordinary life. Its settlements demonstrate that a frontier can be both inhabited and militarily important.

Archons operating in Altgard can expect patrols, escorts, reconnaissance, local defense, and missions where information about movement is as important as defeating an enemy. Communities should feel practiced in frontier life rather than permanently helpless, while Daevas provide the extraordinary force needed when ordinary defenses are exceeded. The region naturally supports stories about responsibility and the cost of keeping routes open. Altgard makes Asmodae’s military experience visible at the scale of roads, settlements, and watchful borders.

Morheim

Morheim is a mountainous and highly varied region, with sharp changes in terrain that make travel and local conditions unpredictable. Its identity is one of adaptability and defiance: communities and travelers learn quickly that one part of the region cannot stand for the whole. The landscape carries scars from major conflicts, and portions of Morheim have been profoundly altered by supernatural cold. Even so, the region remains more than the disasters that have struck it.

Daevas in Morheim may move between mountain routes, settlements, damaged territory, and areas where old conflicts continue to shape present danger. Exploration and military operations can demand flexibility because terrain, weather, and strategic conditions change across the region. Stories here can focus on communities refusing to abandon difficult ground, as well as on the recovery of routes and places transformed by war. Morheim rewards characters who can adapt rather than assume every problem has the same solution.

Beluslan

Beluslan is the most strongly snowbound and frozen of Asmodae’s major regions, defined by severe cold, ice, and landscapes where survival infrastructure matters. Its settlements and routes exist because generations learned how to live with the environment rather than waiting for it to become gentle. The region carries a strong reclamation identity, with inhabited places standing beside damaged, dangerous, or difficult territory. Snow and darkness are part of home here, not symbols of evil.

For Daevas, Beluslan can turn weather, distance, damaged routes, and isolated communities into meaningful parts of an adventure. Missions may involve reclaiming lost ground, protecting travelers, restoring access, investigating ruins, or confronting forces that exploit the region’s difficult conditions. Residents value competence and preparation, but that does not mean they are without warmth, art, or community. Beluslan shows Asmodian perseverance in one of its clearest physical forms.

Brusthonin

Brusthonin is a damaged but still living region shaped by contamination, loss, and long efforts at recovery. Parts of the land carry the consequences of Beritra-linked devastation, yet the region should never be presented as uniformly dead or beyond hope. Settlements, reclaimed ground, and people committed to restoration exist beside places that remain hazardous. Its defining cultural theme is patient recovery rather than simple ruin.

Daevas who serve in Brusthonin may protect reclamation efforts, investigate contaminated sites, recover lost history, escort workers, or confront threats that return to weakened ground. Progress can be slow and uneven, which makes small successes meaningful instead of insignificant. The people living here are participants in recovery, not scenery waiting to be rescued. Brusthonin allows campaigns to show that defending Atreia can mean healing a place as much as winning a battle.

Norsvold

Norsvold is a recognized Asmodian region associated with sacred memory and the deeper historical identity of the northern people. It remains part of the WoA Gazetteer while sitting outside the first-wave continental atlas, so detailed map production is intentionally deferred. Its significance lies in places where history, belief, and the Asmodian experience of survival intersect. The region should be developed from WoA canon rather than treated as a requirement to reproduce later source-story outcomes.

For player characters, Norsvold can support journeys involving sacred sites, historical evidence, old obligations, and disagreements over how the past should guide the present. Religious authorities, historians, soldiers, and local communities may all approach the same places with different priorities. Adventures can explore memory without declaring every inherited belief objectively correct. Like Iluma, Norsvold remains open enough for the campaign’s discoveries to shape its future rather than merely reenacting a predetermined storyline.

Reshanta / The Abyss

Reshanta is the immense physical space between Elysea and Asmodae. Floating landmasses, fortresses, Tower remnants, and vast open gaps make flight central to travel and warfare throughout the Abyss.

Lower Abyss

The Lower Abyss occupies the portion of Reshanta nearer Elysea, a vast aerial theater of floating landmasses separated by open voids. Terminon is the enduring Elyos staging base and a crucial point of support for operations in the region. Fortresses and routes beyond that permanent bridgehead can change hands as the campaign develops, so the military map is never completely static. Flight is fundamental here because enormous gaps make ordinary ground travel insufficient.

For Daevas, the Lower Abyss turns aerial movement into strategy: patrol routes, escorts, fortress approaches, reconnaissance, and control of safe staging areas all matter. Elyos and Asmodian forces contest the wider Abyss while Balaur remain an active third power, ensuring that no simple two-sided front explains every battle. A mission can shift from open-air travel to fortress warfare or desperate rescue with little warning. The Lower Abyss is one of the clearest places where Wings of Atreia’s flight and Warfront identity becomes part of the world itself.

Upper Abyss

The Upper Abyss lies nearer Asmodae and shares the shattered aerial geography that defines Reshanta. Primum is the enduring Asmodian staging base, providing a lasting foothold amid territory whose other fortresses may change control through the campaign. Floating islands, fortress positions, open gaps, and exposed flight paths make geography inseparable from military planning. The region is physical space within Atreia, not another dimension.

Archons and other Daevas operating here must think in three dimensions, because height, approach routes, airborne interception, and the security of landing points can determine whether a mission succeeds. Elyos rivals and Balaur forces can both threaten an operation, while changing fortress control continually reshapes local priorities. The Upper Abyss supports patrols, raids, rescues, reconnaissance, and full Warfront engagements without requiring every encounter to become a mass battle. Its proximity to Asmodae makes the centuries-long Abyss War feel immediate to northern characters.

Core / Eye of Reshanta

The Core, also called the Eye of Reshanta, occupies the central strategic heart of the Abyss between Elysea and Asmodae. It is the most symbolically and militarily charged part of Reshanta, centered on the Divine Fortress and the central Balaur order that holds the starting-era position. Tower remnants and the immense broken interior of Atreia dominate the surrounding sense of scale. Reaching the Core means moving toward the center of a war that has endured for centuries.

Neither Elyos nor Asmodians can treat the Core as ordinary homeland territory, and operations here carry consequences far beyond a single patrol. Balaur power, fortress strategy, aerial approaches, and the ambitions of both Daeva factions converge around the Eye. Player actions may eventually change the balance, because the starting map establishes a beginning rather than a protected future. The Core should feel like a place where tactical success can become history.

Balaurea

Balaurea lies across the outer faces of shattered Atreia. Daevas know only a limited frontier in detail; beyond Inggison, Gelkmaros, and Silentera, Greater Balaurea remains vast, dangerous, and deliberately not exhaustively mapped.

Inggison

Inggison is the principal Elyos foothold on the known Balaurean frontier, reached through stable physical routes that can support real military logistics. It combines dangerous wilderness, ancient Balaur infrastructure, fortress corridors, expeditionary positions, and contact with peoples who have lived under Balaur pressure far longer than most Elyos newcomers. The region is a foothold, not a conquered province, and maintained routes become less certain as expeditions move beyond established corridors. Its approaches also form one side of the strategic access toward Silentera.

Daevas in Inggison may defend fortresses, escort expeditions, work with Reians and other local peoples, explore ruins, or respond to Balaur activity in territory no faction fully controls. Success often depends on coalition-building and learning from residents rather than assuming Elyos institutions already understand the outer world. The frontier can support military missions and cultural encounters in the same journey. Inggison marks the point where the familiar war of Elysea opens into the much larger uncertainty of Balaurea.

Gelkmaros

Gelkmaros is the principal Asmodian foothold on the known Balaurean frontier, a dangerous outer-world region of wilderness, Balaur infrastructure, refugee communities, fortress corridors, and routes toward Silentera. Gelkmaros Fortress anchors Asmodian operations, while places such as Rhonnam Refugee Village, Vorgaltem Citadel, Crimson Temple, and other outposts reveal a frontier shared with communities that are not simply extensions of Asmodae. Old Balaur routes and structures remain embedded in the landscape. Control is practical and local rather than absolute.

Archons and other Daevas may defend refugees, negotiate with local peoples, secure routes, investigate hostile infrastructure, or undertake expeditions beyond reliable maps. The region rewards field competence and the ability to protect without assuming that protection grants ownership or assimilation. Not every Shulack or other Balaurean people encountered here is automatically hostile, making judgment as important as combat readiness. Gelkmaros is where Asmodian resilience meets a world whose conflicts are older and more complicated than the familiar rivalry with Elysea.

Silentera Canyon

Silentera Canyon is a vast physical subterranean network of canyons, tunnels, chambers, ruins, and hidden routes linking the Inggison and Gelkmaros frontiers. It is neither a Rift network nor a separate dimension; Daevas enter an actual deep fracture system beneath the Balaurean frontier. No single modern faction controls the whole network, and knowledge of one mapped corridor does not imply knowledge of every branch. Beshmundir Temple is the greatest known deep site associated with these routes.

For expeditionary Daevas, Silentera is a place of uncertain maps, ambushes, sealed ruins, difficult escape routes, and encounters where Elyos and Asmodian interests may collide far from reinforcement. Guides may know particular passages without understanding the entire system, while deeper areas remain contested, Balaur-dominated, or simply unexplored. The canyon supports dungeon exploration, rescue, espionage, archaeology, and situations where temporary cooperation can become more useful than faction pride. Every descent should reinforce the feeling that the known frontier is only a narrow opening into a much larger outer world.

Greater Balaurea

Beyond the known frontier lies an enormous outer world of wilderness, ruins, cities, Dragon Lord dominions, independent peoples, Drana zones, and infrastructure that Daevas have not exhaustively mapped. WoA intentionally leaves this greater territory open rather than importing every later location or storyline into the starting setting.

Travel beyond maintained corridors should feel like genuine expedition into uncertain territory. Future campaigns may discover new settlements, routes, allies, enemies, and regions consistent with established WoA canon, but those discoveries are not predetermined. The known Balaurea frontier is therefore a beginning, not a complete atlas.
