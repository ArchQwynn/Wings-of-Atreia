History of Atreia

Before there were Elyos and Asmodians, before the Abyss became a battlefield, Atreia was one world. Its history is a story of ascension, war, catastrophe, survival—and a division that has never truly healed.

Before the Division

Ancient Atreia

Atreia was once a single world, held together around the immense Tower of Eternity. The Tower was more than a monument: it stood at the heart of the world and became inseparable from the life, history, and survival of Atreia itself.

Long before the peoples now called Elyos and Asmodians existed as separate civilizations, their ancestors shared this world. That unity would not survive what came next.

The First Great Threat

The Rise of the Balaur

Among the ancient Drakan, one rose beyond the limits of his kind: Fregion. Elevated by Aion for his exceptional nature, Fregion became the first of what would later be known as the Dragon Lords.

In time, Fregion discovered Id and drew upon a power beyond the ordinary strength of the Drakan. Through that power, other exceptional Drakans were raised into Dragon Lords. Under their rule, the old Drakan order transformed into the power remembered and feared as the Balaur.

Their growing strength brought them into catastrophic conflict with the rest of Atreia.

An Age of War

The Millennium War

The struggle against the Balaur became a war measured not in seasons, but in generations. As the conflict deepened, extraordinary Daevas were elevated into the Twelve Empyrean Lords, champions whose power and leadership became central to Atreia's resistance.

The war changed societies, hardened loyalties, and placed the Tower of Eternity itself at the center of Atreia's fate. Eventually, the desperate search for an end to the conflict led to events that would reshape the world forever.

The World Breaks

The Cataclysm

The final crisis around the Tower ended not with the restoration of the old world, but with disaster. The Tower of Eternity was shattered. Atreia broke apart, and the world that had once been whole was divided into two great surviving realms.

The Tower Guardians, Siel and Israphel, stood at the heart of that terrible moment. Their actions became part of the history and legend surrounding the world's survival, even as the Cataclysm ended the age of a united Atreia.

From that point forward, the survivors of the north and south would rebuild separately.

Two Peoples Emerge

Elysea and Asmodae

In the south, survivors rebuilt beneath the brighter skies of Elysea. Over generations, their civilization became the Elyos, and the Lords who guided them came to be known as the Seraphim Lords.

In the north, the survivors of Asmodae endured a harsher land. Their people became the Asmodians, guided by the Lords later known as the Shedim Lords.

These identities were born after the Cataclysm. The Seraphim and Shedim were not ancient rival orders that had always existed; they emerged from survival, reconstruction, and the different beliefs that developed on each side of the broken world.

Separated for generations, Elyos and Asmodians came to understand the Cataclysm—and one another—through very different histories. By the time the two peoples could meet again, neither saw the other simply as long-lost kin.

The Divide Becomes a Road

The Abyss Opens

Between Elysea and Asmodae lay the shattered space known as Reshanta—the Abyss. What had separated the two realms also became a path between them: a vast and dangerous expanse of floating land, ruined places, unstable space, and strategic positions unlike anything found in the lands below.

When Elyos and Asmodian forces began entering the Abyss, they encountered one another again. Centuries of separation had not prepared either side for reconciliation. Suspicion became confrontation, and confrontation became battle.

The Abyss offered routes, fortresses, resources, and control over the space between worlds. Neither side was willing to surrender it to the other.

The Campaign Era Begins

The First Phase of the Abyss War

The conflict in the Abyss did not remain a war between two peoples. The Balaur were there as well, turning the struggle into a three-sided war in which yesterday's battlefield could become tomorrow's fortress and no victory could be assumed permanent.

The Elyos established their foothold at Terminon. The Asmodians held Primum. Beyond them, Balaur power remained entrenched across the greater Abyss. Daevas now took to the air not merely to explore the shattered realm, but to fight for it.

Elyos. Asmodians. Balaur. Three powers contest the Abyss, each with its own purpose, history, and claim upon the future of Atreia.

This is the age into which newly Ascended Daevas spread their wings.

This player history ends at the opening campaign era. What comes next belongs to the story at the table.
